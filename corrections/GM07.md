# Graph Minors VII: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. VII. Disjoint paths on a surface*, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. B **45** (1988), 212–254.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM07/GM07.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM07/GM07.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Statement of (2.1), page 215: the converse needs $U(J)$ connected

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** (2.1) states that a graph $J$ in $\Sigma$ is a seam graph for some net if and
only if (i)–(iv) hold. The "if" direction is false. The point set of a seam graph is
$\phi(bd(\Delta))$, the image of a circle, so it is connected and non-empty, and (i)–(iv) do not
force this. Two examples on the sphere:

- Let $J$ be the graph with no vertices and no edges. Then (i) holds since $bd(\Sigma) = \emptyset$
  and $J$ has no edges, (ii) holds since $J - bd(\Sigma)$ has no vertices, (iii) holds since the
  sphere is connected, and (iv) holds since every $O$-arc of the sphere bounds a closed disc. But
  $J$ is not a seam graph.
- Let $J$ consist of two disjoint edges, each with two ends. Then (i), (ii) and (iv) hold as before,
  and (iii) holds since the complement of two disjoint arcs in the sphere is connected. But $U(J)$
  is not connected.

**Correction.** Add the condition

(v) $U(J)$ is connected (and so non-empty).

The "only if" direction holds as printed, and every seam graph satisfies (v), since
$U(J) = \phi(bd(\Delta))$. The proof of the converse needs a further step, given in the correction
to the proof of (2.1) below.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM07.theorem_2_1` in `GraphMinor/GM07/Results.lean` proves the corrected
statement in both directions. `GraphMinor.GM07.not_theorem_2_1` in the same file proves that the
printed statement is false, by the first example.

## 2. Proof of (2.1), page 216: null-homotopy in $\Delta$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof of the converse says "by (iv) every $O$-arc included in
$\Delta - bd(\Delta)$ is null-homotopic". Condition (iv) speaks of null-homotopy in $\Sigma$, not in
$\Delta$. In the second example of the correction to (2.1) above, $\Delta$ is an annulus, and its
middle circle is null-homotopic in $\Sigma$ but not in $\Delta$.

**Correction.** Assume (i)–(v), and let $\phi$ be the surjection from the surface $\Delta$ obtained
by cutting along $U(J - bd(\Sigma))$. The missing step is the following. Let $F$ be an $O$-arc in
$\Delta - bd(\Delta)$. Then $\phi(F)$ is an $O$-arc in $\Sigma - U(J)$, and by (iv) it bounds a
closed disc $D$ of $\Sigma$. Since $U(J)$ is connected by (v) and misses $\phi(F)$, either
$U(J) \cap D = \emptyset$ or $U(J) \subseteq D - \phi(F)$. In the first case, $F$ bounds the closed
disc $\phi^{-1}(D)$ in $\Delta - bd(\Delta)$. In the second case, $bd(\Sigma) = \emptyset$, because
$bd(\Sigma) \subseteq U(J)$ by (i) and $D - \phi(F)$ contains no point of $bd(\Sigma)$. If $\Sigma$
is a sphere, the other closed disc bounded by $\phi(F)$ misses $U(J)$, and we use it in place of
$D$. Otherwise some $O$-arc of $\Sigma$ is not null-homotopic, and it can be moved off $D$, into
$\Sigma - U(J)$, contrary to (iv). So every $O$-arc in $\Delta - bd(\Delta)$ is null-homotopic in
$\Delta$, and the rest of the printed proof applies. So (2.1) holds with (v).

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM07.theorem_2_1` in `GraphMinor/GM07/Results.lean`.

## 3. Proof of (2.6), page 218: the inequality from minimality

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** Let $e$ be an edge of type (ii) of $J'$, with ends $a$ and $b$ (these $a, b$
are not the parameters of $\Sigma(a, b, c)$), and let $s, t$ be the ends of $F$. The proof pastes
$f = g$, cuts along $F$, and deduces from the minimality of $(\Delta, \Pi)$ that
$|e \cap V(G)| \le |\phi(F^*) \cap V(G)|$. This inequality can fail when $\phi(s), \phi(t) \in V(G)$
and $a, b \notin V(G)$. Let $\Sigma$ be an annulus with cuffs $C_1, C_2$. Let $G$ have a vertex
$s_0 \in C_1$, a vertex $t_0 \in C_2$ and a vertex $z$ in the interior, two edges between $s_0$ and
$z$ that together form a circle around the annulus, and two edges between $t_0$ and $z$ that form a
second such circle, meeting the first only at $z$.

- Every $G$-normal net has at least four points of $bd(\Delta)$ mapped into $V(G)$. Each of
  $s_0, t_0$ is in $bd(\Sigma) \subseteq U(J)$, so it has at least one such point. If $z \in U(J)$,
  then $z$ has at least two, since $z \notin bd(\Sigma)$. If $z \notin U(J)$, then the first circle
  meets $U(J)$ only at $s_0$. If $s_0$ had only one preimage, the first circle would be the image of
  an $O$-arc of the disc $\Delta$, and so would be null-homotopic in $\Sigma$, which it is not. So
  $s_0$ has two preimages, and likewise $t_0$.
- The net with one pair, cut along an arc $e$ from $C_1$ to $C_2$ that passes through $z$ and whose
  ends $a, b$ are not vertices of $G$, has exactly four such points. So it is minimal.
- In the disc $\Delta$, the preimage of $G$ is a quadrilateral with corners $s_0$, $t_0$ and the two
  copies of $z$. The straight $I$-arc $F$ from $s_0$ to $t_0$ inside it is a $\phi^{-1}(G)$-normal
  proper $I$-arc that separates $f$ and $g$. But $|e \cap V(G)| = 1$ and
  $|\phi(F^*) \cap V(G)| = 0$.

**Correction.** Pasting $f = g$ and cutting along $F$ changes the number of points of $bd(\Delta)$
mapped into $V(G)$ by exactly
$$-2|e \cap V(G)| - [a \in V(G)] - [b \in V(G)] + 2|\phi(F^*) \cap V(G)| + [\phi(s) \in V(G)] + [\phi(t) \in V(G)],$$
where $[\,\cdot\,]$ is $1$ if the condition holds and $0$ otherwise. So minimality gives
$$2|e \cap V(G)| + [a \in V(G)] + [b \in V(G)] \le 2|\phi(F^*) \cap V(G)| + [\phi(s) \in V(G)] + [\phi(t) \in V(G)].$$
Sum this over the edges of type (ii) of the path $P$ of (2.5). The other edges of $P$ are of type
(i), so they lie in $bd(\Sigma)$ together with their ends. So a vertex of $P$ off $bd(\Sigma)$ is an
end of two edges of type (ii) if it is an inner vertex of $P$, and of one if it is an end of $P$. So
the sum gives that at most $|E(P)| \cdot |\phi(F^*) \cap V(G)| + |E(P)| + 1$ vertices of $G$ lie in
$U(P) - bd(\Sigma)$. Since $|E(P)| + 1 = |V(P)|$, this is the displayed bound for $|U(P) \cap V(G)|$
in the proof, and (2.6) follows as printed.

`GraphMinor/GM07/NetSeparateCount.lean` (the exact change in the count),

## 4. Claim (1) in the proof of (3.2), page 219: the ends of $F$ must be fixed

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** Claim (1) states that if a homeomorphism $\alpha$ with null signature maps
$F \in \mathcal{F}$ to $F' \in \mathcal{F}$, then $F$ is parallel to $F'$. This fails when $\alpha$
exchanges the common ends $s, t$ of the members of $\mathcal{F}$. Let $\Sigma$ be an annulus, let
$s, t$ be antipodal points of the outer cuff, let $F$ be a proper $I$-arc from $s$ to $t$ that
passes on one side of the hole, and let $\alpha$ be the rotation by a half turn. Then $\alpha$ maps
each cuff to itself and keeps its orientation, so it has null signature, and $F' = \alpha(F)$ passes
on the other side of the hole. One of the two arcs of the outer cuff between $s$ and $t$ bounds a
disc together with $F$, and an annulus together with $F'$. A homeomorphism that fixes $bd(\Sigma)$
pointwise and maps $F$ to $F'$ would map the disc onto the annulus, so $F$ and $F'$ are not
parallel.

**Correction.** Claim (1) assumes in addition that $\alpha(s) = s$ and $\alpha(t) = t$. In the rest
of the proof, sort the members $F$ of $\mathcal{F}$ by the signature of $\alpha_F$ and by whether
$\alpha_F$ fixes or exchanges $s$ and $t$. For $F, F'$ in the same class,
$\alpha = \alpha_{F'} \cdot \alpha_F^{-1}$ has null signature and fixes $s$ and $t$, so $F$ is
parallel to $F'$ by the corrected Claim (1). There are still only finitely many classes, so (3.2)
holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.Surface.parallelArcs` in `GraphMinor/Surface/CutParallel.lean` proves the
corrected Claim (1), and `GraphMinor.GM07.theorem_3_2` in `GraphMinor/GM07/Results.lean` proves
(3.2).

## 5. Definition of "surrounds" in the proof of (3.4), page 220: two components in one region

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** In the proof of (3.4), an $X$-matching $G$ surrounds a $Y$-forest $H$ if
$U(G) \cap U(H) = \emptyset$ and each component of $H$ that meets $bd(\Sigma)$ lies in a simply
connected region of $G$. This allows two components of $H$ in the same region, and then Claim (2) is
false. Let $\Sigma$ be a closed disc, $Y = \{y_1, y_2\}$, and let $X = \{x_1, x_1', x_2, x_2'\}$ be
in this cyclic order on $bd(\Sigma)$, with $y_i$ in the arc $F_{y_i}$ between $x_i$ and $x_i'$. The
$X$-matching $G$ with the two chords $x_1'x_2$ and $x_2'x_1$ has a simply connected middle region
that contains $y_1$ and $y_2$. So $G$ surrounds both the $Y$-forest with one edge $y_1y_2$ drawn in
that region and the $Y$-forest with no edges. These two forests are not homotopic, since only the
first has a path from $y_1$ to $y_2$.

**Correction.** In the definition of "surrounds", add: "and any two points of $Y$ in the same region
of $G$ are joined by a path of $H$". With this, Claim (1) still holds, by the same thickening
argument, and Claim (2) holds. So (3.4) holds as printed.

## 6. Proof of (3.5), page 221: a forest that meets the matching in a finite set

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper leaves the proof of (3.5) to the reader.

**Proof.** We give a proof. Let $H_0 \in \mathscr{B}$, and let $Y = V(H_0) \cap bd(\Sigma)$. Choose
the $I$-arcs $F_y$ $(y \in Y)$ and the set $X$ as in the proof of (3.4), and thicken $H_0$ as in the
proof of (3.4)(1). This gives mutually disjoint closed discs, one around each component of $H_0$
that meets $bd(\Sigma)$, whose insides miss $bd(\Sigma)$, and an $X$-matching $G_1$ in their
boundaries that surrounds $H_0$. Moreover, $G_1$ surrounds every $Y$-forest whose edges and vertices
off $bd(\Sigma)$ lie in the insides of these discs, provided that any two points of $Y$ in the same
disc are joined by a path of the forest. We build such a forest $H$ with $U(G) \cap U(H)$ finite.

First, any two points $y, y'$ of $Y$ in the same disc $D$ are joined by an $I$-arc whose interior
lies in the inside of $D$ and which meets $U(G)$ in only finitely many points. To see this, leave
$y$ along a short arc into the inside of $D$ that meets $U(G)$ at most in $y$. This is possible
since $G$ is a matching, and so near $y$ the set $U(G)$ is empty or a single arc of an edge ending
at $y$. Do the same at $y'$. The inside of $D$ is open and connected, and near each of its points
the set $U(G)$ is empty or an arc, since the vertices of $G$ are in $bd(\Sigma)$. Hence the far ends
of the two short arcs are joined in the inside of $D$ by a curve that meets $U(G)$ in finitely many
points, and this curve contains an arc with the same ends.

Now choose one point $r_D$ of $Y$ in each disc $D$. For each other point $y$ of $Y$, take an $I$-arc
as above from $y$ to the point $r_D$ of its disc. Add these arcs one at a time, and cut each at the
first point where it meets what has been built already, starting from $y$. Each new arc meets the
earlier ones in one end only, so the union is the drawing of a forest $H$. Its vertices in
$bd(\Sigma)$ are the points of $Y$, each of them is joined in $H$ to the point $r_D$ of its disc,
and $U(G) \cap U(H)$ is finite. So $G_1$ surrounds both $H_0$ and $H$, and by (3.4)(2) they are
homotopic. Hence $H \in \mathscr{B}$, taking the identity for $\alpha$ in the definition of
homoplastic.

## 7. Statement of (4.3), page 222: a path with one edge

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** (4.3) concludes that there are at least $v + 1$ paths of $G$, each with one end
in $C$ and the other in $bd(\Sigma) - C$, mutually vertex-disjoint except for their ends. Read with
$v + 1$ distinct paths, this is false. Let $\Sigma$ be a cylinder with cuffs $C, C'$, and let $G$
have a vertex $c \in C$, a vertex $c' \in C'$ and one edge between them. A $G$-normal $O$-arc $F$
with $F \cap bd(\Sigma) = \emptyset$ meets $U(G)$ only in vertices of $G$, and these lie in
$bd(\Sigma)$, so $F$ misses $U(G)$. The cylinder minus $U(G) \cup bd(\Sigma)$ is an open disc, so
$F$ is null-homotopic. So the hypothesis holds for every $v$, but $G$ has only one path from $C$ to
$bd(\Sigma) - C$. The proof fails at the use of Menger's theorem. There $X$ must miss $bd(\Sigma)$,
so no such $X$ meets a path with one edge, and a path that avoids every $X$ does not give $v + 1$
distinct paths.

**Correction.** The conclusion reads: there are paths $P_1, \dots, P_{v+1}$ of $G$, each with one
end in $C$ and the other in $bd(\Sigma) - C$, mutually vertex-disjoint except for their ends, where
$P_i$ and $P_j$ ($i \ne j$) may be the same path if it has no internal vertex. This is the form that
the Menger step of the proof gives, and the printed statement holds when no edge of $G$ has one end
in $C$ and the other in $bd(\Sigma) - C$. The only use, in the proof of (4.6), needs only paths that
meet in their ends, so it works with this form.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM07.theorem_4_3` in `GraphMinor/GM07/Results.lean`, with the conclusion
`GraphMinor.GM07.CuffToRestDisjointPathsStatement` in `GraphMinor/GM07/Defs.lean`.

## 8. Proof of (4.4), page 223: a forest that avoids the cut

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says that the proof of (4.4) is easy, suggests an induction on
$|E(L)|$, and leaves it to the reader.

**Proof.** We give a proof. Let us call a homeomorphism $\alpha \colon \Sigma \to \Sigma$ a *move*
if $\alpha(x) = x$ for all $x \in bd(\Sigma)$ and $\alpha$ maps each path with ends in $bd(\Sigma)$
to a homotopic path. The image of a forest under a move is a forest homotopic to it. The *parts* of
$L$ are the components of $U(L)$. Since no subset of $U(L)$ is an $O$-arc or an $I$-arc, each part
is the drawing of a tree, and it meets $bd(\Sigma)$ in at most one point. Let $Z$ be the set of
vertices of $H_0$ in $bd(\Sigma) \cap U(L)$.

First, for each part $P$ choose a point $\rho(P)$ of $P$, in $bd(\Sigma)$ if $P$ meets $bd(\Sigma)$,
and let $R(P) = \{\rho(P)\}$. We show that if $N(P)$ is an open set including $R(P)$, for each part
$P$, and these sets are mutually disjoint and each misses the other parts, then one move maps each
part $P$ into $N(P)$. Choose mutually disjoint open sets $W(P)$ with $P \subseteq W(P)$, one for
each part. It is enough to find for each part $P$ a move $\alpha_P$ that maps $P$ into $N(P)$ and
fixes every point outside $W(P)$. For then $\alpha_P(P) \subseteq W(P)$, and the composition of
these moves maps each part $P$ into $N(P)$, since each $\alpha_{P'}$ with $P' \ne P$ fixes $W(P)$
pointwise.

We find $\alpha_P$ by induction on the number of edges of the tree $P$, for every open set $N$
including $R(P)$ in place of $N(P)$. If $P$ has no edge, then $P = \{\rho(P)\}$, and we take the
identity. Otherwise, list the edges of $P$ in order of their distance from $\rho(P)$, and let $A$ be
the closure of the last one. Then $A$ is an arc whose ends are a vertex $y$ nearer to $\rho(P)$ and
a vertex $x$ of valency 1 in $P$. Also $x \ne \rho(P)$, and so $x \notin bd(\Sigma)$, since $P$
meets $bd(\Sigma)$ at most in $\rho(P)$. Let $P'$ be $P$ minus $A - \{y\}$, which is a tree with one
edge fewer and contains $\rho(P)$. By induction there is a move $\beta$ that maps $P'$ into $N$ and
fixes every point outside $W(P)$. The set $\beta^{-1}(N) \cap W(P)$ is open and contains $y$. Choose
a small open disc $B$ around $y$ in this set. The arc $A$ minus $B$ is at positive distance from
$P'$, from $bd(\Sigma)$ and from the other parts, and so it has a closed disc neighbourhood
$D \subseteq W(P)$ that misses all of them. Sliding $A$ along itself towards $y$ gives a
homeomorphism $\alpha$ of $\Sigma$ that is the identity outside $D$ and maps $A$ into $B$. Since
$\alpha$ is the identity outside a closed disc that misses $bd(\Sigma)$, it is isotopic to the
identity by an isotopy fixing $bd(\Sigma)$, and so it is a move. It fixes $P'$, since $D$ misses
$P'$. Thus $\beta \circ \alpha$ is a move that maps $P$ into $N$ and fixes every point outside
$W(P)$, which completes the induction.

Second, a move takes the finitely many points of the sets $R(P)$ that are not in $bd(\Sigma)$ off
$U(H_0)$, one at a time. For let $a$ be such a point. Since $U(H_0)$ contains no open set, there are
points near $a$ not in $U(H_0)$, and a slide along a short line, which is the identity outside a
small closed disc around $a$ that misses $bd(\Sigma)$ and the other points, moves $a$ to such a
point. As in the first step, this slide is a move. Let $H_1$ be the image of $H_0$ under the inverse
of the composed move. Then $H_1$ is homotopic to $H_0$, and every point of a set $R(P)$ that is in
$U(H_1)$ is in $Z$, since $U(H_1) \cap bd(\Sigma) = V(H_0) \cap bd(\Sigma)$.

Third, choose mutually disjoint open sets around the points $\rho(P)$, each missing the parts other
than its own. Each point $z$ of $Z$ is $\rho(P)$ for the part $P$ containing it, since it is the
point of $P$ in $bd(\Sigma)$. For $z \in Z$ we choose this set, $F(z)$ say, so small that every
closed curve in $F(z)$ is null-homotopic in $\Sigma$. Since $H_1$ is a forest, each edge of $H_1$ at
$z$ has a closure that is an arc with one end $z$, and we split it at a point in $F(z)$ such that
the part from $z$ to this point lies in $F(z)$. This gives a connected piece in $F(z)$ that ends at
$z$ and a closed piece that misses $z$. Let $N(z)$ be $F(z)$ minus these closed pieces, minus the
closures of the edges of $H_1$ not at $z$, and minus the vertices of $H_1$ other than $z$. It is
open and contains $z$. For a point $y = \rho(P)$ not in $Z$, let $N(y)$ be its open set minus
$U(H_1)$, which is open and contains $y$ by the second step. For each part $P$ let
$N(P) = N(\rho(P))$. Let $\beta$ be the move given by the first step for these sets, and let $H_2$
be the image of $H_1$ under $\beta^{-1}$. Then $H_2$ is homotopic to $H_0$, since the inverse of a
move is a move. A part $P$ meets $U(H_2)$ only where $\beta(P)$ meets $U(H_1)$, and
$\beta(P) \subseteq N(\rho(P))$. The set $N(y)$ misses $U(H_1)$ for $y \notin Z$, and for $z \in Z$
the set $N(z)$ meets $U(H_1)$ only in $z$ and in the pieces in $F(z)$ of the edges at $z$. So $P$
meets $U(H_2)$ only if $\rho(P)$ is a point $z$ of $Z$, and then only in $z$ and in the images under
$\beta^{-1}$ of these pieces, which are pieces of the edges of $H_2$ at $z$.

Fourth, let $z \in Z$, let $P$ be the part containing $z$, and let $F'(z) = \beta^{-1}(F(z))$. Every
closed curve in $F'(z)$ is null-homotopic in $\Sigma$, since $\beta$ is a homeomorphism. The set
$F'(z)$ includes $P$, since $\beta(P) \subseteq N(z) \subseteq F(z)$, and it misses the other parts,
since $\beta$ maps them into their own sets $N(P')$, which miss $F(z)$. Let $C(z)$ be the union of
the vertices of $H_2$ other than $z$, the closures of the edges of $H_2$ not at $z$, and the images
under $\beta^{-1}$ of the closed pieces of the edges at $z$. It is a closed set, and it misses $P$
by the third step. Since $P$ is a tree that meets $bd(\Sigma)$ only in $z$, cutting along it turns
$P$ into an arc $\phi^{-1}(P)$ of $bd(\Sigma')$. So there is a homeomorphism $\chi$ from a rectangle
$[x_1, x_2] \times [0, \delta]$ into $\Sigma'$ that maps the bottom side into $bd(\Sigma')$, such
that $\phi^{-1}(P)$ is the image of a part of the bottom side that misses its ends, and such that
$\phi \circ \chi$ maps the rectangle into the open set $F'(z) - C(z)$, which includes $P$. Since
$l(z) \in \phi^{-1}(P)$, there is a point $(x_0, 0)$ of the bottom side with $\chi(x_0, 0) = l(z)$.
Each point of $P$ is an interior point of the image $Q$ of the rectangle under $\phi \circ \chi$,
since all its preimages are inside the bottom side. Each edge of $H_2$ at $z$ starts at $z$, and its
other end is not in $Q$, since it is in $C(z)$. So the edge leaves $Q$ for the last time at the
image of a point $w$ on the top or lateral sides of the rectangle (the image of the rest of the
bottom side lies in $P \cup bd(\Sigma)$). We replace the part of the edge up to this point by the
image of the straight segment from $(x_0, 0)$ to $w$. Two such segments meet only at $(x_0, 0)$,
since their ends $w$ are distinct points of the top and lateral sides, and each meets the bottom
side only at $(x_0, 0)$. So the new lines meet each other only at $z$, and they miss $P$ except at
$z$. They also miss the rest of the new forest, since inside $Q$ the drawing of $H_2$ consists of
$z$ and pieces of the edges at $z$, and the part of each such edge in $Q$ has been replaced. The
change takes place inside $F'(z)$, in which every closed curve is null-homotopic, and so the new
forest is homotopic to $H_2$. The changes at the points of $Z$ take place in the disjoint sets
$F'(z)$, and so they do not interfere.

Let $H$ be the final forest. Then $H$ is homotopic to $H_0$. A part $P$ with $\rho(P) \notin Z$
misses $U(H_2)$ and all the sets $F'(z)$, and so it misses $U(H)$. A part $P$ with
$\rho(P) = z \in Z$ meets $U(H)$ only in $z$. So $U(H)$ meets $U(L)$ only in points of $Z$, which
are in $bd(\Sigma)$. For $z \in Z$, every edge of $H$ at $z$ starts with the image of a segment from
$(x_0, 0)$, and so in $\phi^{-1}(H)$ every edge with an end in $\phi^{-1}(z)$ has this end at
$l(z)$. Hence every member of $\phi^{-1}(z) - \{l(z)\}$ is an isolated vertex of $\phi^{-1}(H)$. For
$z \in V(H_0) \cap bd(\Sigma)$ not in $U(L)$, the set $\phi^{-1}(z)$ has only the member $l(z)$.

## 9. Proof of (4.6), page 224: (4.3), not (4.2)

**Kind.** Misprint.

## 10. Proof of (4.6), page 225: the step from $F'$ to a contradiction

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** On pages 224 and 225 the proof says that "it is easy to see" that
$F' \cap r$ is connected for every region $r$ of $G'$, and that there is a $G$-normal $O$-arc $F$
homotopic to $F'$ with $V(G) \cap F \subseteq (V(G') \cap F') \cup ((V(G) \cap C) - \{z\})$.

**Proof.** We give the details of this step, without moving $F'$. Let $X = V(G') \cap F'$, let
$D = (V(G) \cap C) - \{z\}$, and let $Z$ be the union of $X \cup D$ and the set of points of
$\Sigma$ not in $U(G) \cup bd(\Sigma)$. Let $F \subseteq Z$ be an $O$-arc. Then $F$ is $G$-normal,
and $V(G) \cap F \subseteq X \cup D$, which gives the inclusion displayed above. Moreover
$|V(G) \cap F| \le |X| + |D| \le v$, and $z \notin F$ since $z \notin Z$. So the argument above
shows that $F$ is null-homotopic in $\Sigma$, for otherwise the cuff $C'$ given by (ii) could be
neither $C$ nor another cuff. By (11.2), it follows that $Z$ is flat, that is, every continuous map
from $S^1$ to $Z$ is null-homotopic in $\Sigma$. Now let $Z'$ be the union of $Z$ and the edges of
$G$ with an end in $D$. Then $Z'$ is also flat. For we may add these edges one at a time, and each
edge $e$ added has a thin closed disc around $\bar{e}$, whose boundary lies in the current set
except possibly at the other end of $e$, so that (11.3) applies. Finally, $F' \subseteq Z'$. For let
$x \in F'$. Then $x \notin bd(\Sigma)$. If $x \notin U(G)$ then $x \in Z$. If $x \in V(G)$ then

edge is not an edge of $G'$, since $F'$ is $G'$-normal, and so $e$ has an end in $D$, and

## 13. Proof of (7.3), page 239: deleting the inside of a small disc

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof says that "it is easy to see" that $\alpha(G') = \alpha(G)$, and that
the result for $G'$ gives the result for $G$.

**Proof.** We give the details. Let $\Delta$ be the closed disc with boundary $F$ that includes an
edge inside $F$. Then $|E(G')| < |E(G)|$. Every $G$-normal set is $G'$-normal, since
$U(G') \subseteq U(G)$ and every vertex of $G$ in $U(G')$ is a vertex of $G'$. Also
$|V(G') \cap Y| \le |V(G) \cap Y|$ for every $Y \subseteq \Sigma$, since $V(G') \subseteq V(G)$. In
particular $|V(G') \cap C| \le r - 1$ for every cuff $C$. We need only the inequality
$\alpha(G) \le \alpha(G')$, so that $r \le \frac{2}{9}\alpha(G')$ and the inductive hypothesis
applies to $G'$. Let $X'$ be a solid connected $G'$-normal set that is not near-planar, with
$|X' \cap V(G')| = \alpha(G')$. For each cuff $C$, keep an $O$-arc in $X'$ that is not
null-homotopic in $\Sigma + \hat{C}$, and keep one that is not null-homotopic in $\Sigma$. They
exist by (5.1), since $X'$ is not planar in any of these surfaces. Join these $O$-arcs by lines in
$X'$. This gives a solid connected set $X_0 \subseteq X'$, which is a finite union of lines, and so
it misses some point $p$ in the inside of $\Delta$. If $F$ contains a vertex of $G$ that is not in
$X_0$, we choose $p$ near this vertex, so that no point of $X_0$ is projected onto it below. Now
project from $p$ onto $F$ the points of $X_0$ in $\Delta$, and leave the other points of $X_0$
fixed. The image of each kept $O$-arc is a closed curve homotopic to it, and so by (11.2) it
includes an $O$-arc that is not null-homotopic in the same surface. Joining these $O$-arcs by lines
in the image of $X_0$ gives a solid connected set $X$, which is not near-planar by (5.1). Let
$x \in X \cap U(G)$. If $x$ is the image of a point of $X_0$ in $\Delta$, then $x \in F$, and so $x$
is the vertex of $G$ in $F$, since $F$ is $G$-normal. By the choice of $p$, then $x \in X_0$.
Otherwise $x \in X_0$ and $x$ is not in the inside of $\Delta$. In both cases $x \in U(G')$, and so
$x$ is a vertex of $G'$, since $X_0$ is $G'$-normal. So $X$ is $G$-normal and
$X \cap V(G) \subseteq X' \cap V(G')$. Hence $\alpha(G) \le \alpha(G')$.

Now let $e$ be an edge of $G'$ satisfying the theorem for $G'$. Then $e$ is an edge of $G$, it is
not a loop, and $\bar{e} \cap bd(\Sigma) = \emptyset$. Every set in $\mathscr{A}_{r-1}(C)$ for $G$
is in $\mathscr{A}_{r-1}(C)$ for $G'$, by the two facts above, and so $\Delta_{r-1}$ for $G$ is
included in $\Delta_{r-1}$ for $G'$. Hence $e \notin \Delta_{r-1}$ for $G$. Finally, a $G$-normal
null-homotopic $O$-arc with at most one vertex of $G$ and with $e$ inside it would be a $G'$-normal
$O$-arc with at most one vertex of $G'$ and with $e$ inside it, which is impossible. So $e$
satisfies the theorem for $G$.

`GraphMinor.GM07.exists_candidate_of_prune` in `GraphMinor/GM07/DiscPrune.lean`.

## 15. Remark in Section 8, page 242: "any schismatic set includes a schism"

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** The paper states
$\alpha(G) - |V(G) \cap bd(\Sigma)| \le \omega(G) \le \alpha(G)$, "because if $X$ is a schism then
$X \cup bd(\Sigma)$ includes a schismatic set, and any schismatic set includes a schism". The second
clause is false. Let $\Sigma$ be the closed unit disc with the open discs of radius $1/10$ around
$1/2$ and $-1/2$ removed, so $\Sigma \cong \Sigma(0, 0, 3)$. Let $C$ be the outer cuff, and let $A$
be the circle of radius $1/2$ around $1/2$. It meets $C$ only at the point $1$, and it has the first
removed disc inside it and not the second. Then $X = C \cup A$ is an $X_2$-arc.

- The set $X$ is not near-planar. In $\Sigma$, and in $\Sigma$ with $C$ capped, the circle $A$
  surrounds a hole, so $X$ lies in no disc. With either inner cuff capped, $C$ lies in no disc of
  the resulting cylinder.
- The only proper subsets of $X$ that are $O$-arcs are $C$ and $A$, and both are near-planar: $C$
  bounds the disc pasted on $C$, and $A$ bounds a disc once the first inner cuff is capped. So $X$
  is schismatic.
- $X$ includes no schism. Types (i) and (iv)–(vi) need an $O$-arc that is not near-planar or that
  misses $bd(\Sigma)$, and the only $O$-arcs in $X$ are $C$ and $A$, both near-planar and both
  meeting $C$. Types (ii) and (iii) need an $I$-arc that meets $bd(\Sigma)$ only in its ends. Such
  an arc in $X$ would have its interior in $A - \{1\}$ and both ends at $1$, which is impossible.

The bound $\omega(G) \le \alpha(G)$ is itself false. The correction to (18.1) in [GM08.md](GM08.md)
gives a graph $G$ in a surface with three cuffs that has a $G$-normal schismatic set with one
vertex, so that $\alpha(G) \le 1$, while every $G$-normal schism has at least $W$ vertices, for any
given $W$.

**Correction.** Delete "and any schismatic set includes a schism", and with it the bound
$\omega(G) \le \alpha(G)$. The bound $\alpha(G) - |V(G) \cap bd(\Sigma)| \le \omega(G)$ keeps its
proof. The next sentence of the paper, which replaces the condition $\alpha(G) \ge 42kv$ in (7.5) by
$\omega(G) \ge 42kv$, uses the deleted bound, so it is left without proof. No numbered result of
this paper uses the remark. Graph Minors VIII uses it in the proof of (18.1), as described in the
correction to (18.1) in [GM08.md](GM08.md).

**Lean.** Nothing in the formalization uses this remark. The corrected form of Graph Minors VIII,
(18.1), which avoids it, is `GraphMinor.GM08.theorem_18_1_leaf` in `GraphMinor/GM08/Results.lean`.

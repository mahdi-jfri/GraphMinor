# Graph Minors XX: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XX. Wagner's conjecture*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B
**92** (2004), 325–357.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM20/GM20.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM20/GM20.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Proof of 4.3, page 331: names of sets and maps

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** There are three slips. The proof says "let $C^r$ be the set of those
patchworks" and later "Then $P_1 = (G_1, \mu, \Delta) \in C_1$", but the rest of the proof calls
these sets $\mathcal{F}^r$ and $\mathcal{F}_1$. It says "let $\lambda = \Omega_2 \to \Omega_3$ be an
isomorphism", where $\lambda$ is a map. And it says "We define $\lambda(Q_2) = Q_1$, and claim that
$\gamma$ is an encoding", where the map being defined is $\gamma$.

**Correction.** The places read $\mathcal{F}^r$, $\mathcal{F}_1$, $\lambda \colon \Omega_2 \to \Omega_3$
and $\gamma(Q_2) = Q_1$. The statement is not affected.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_3_proof` and `GraphMinor.GM20.wellBehaved_addRootSet` in
`GraphMinor/GM20/Section4a.lean`. The set $\mathcal{F}^r$ is `GraphMinor.GM20.addRootSet`, in the
same file.

## 2. Proof of 4.9, pages 333–334: the place of $v$ among the roots

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof reduces to the case where each $P_2 \in \mathcal{F}_2$ has a vertex
$v$ with $P_2/\{v\} = P_1 \in \mathcal{F}_1$. It maps an $\Omega_2$-completion $Q_2$ of $P_2$ to an
$\Omega_1$-completion $Q_1 = \gamma(Q_2)$ of $P_1$, and says that "it is easy to see that $\gamma$
is an encoding". But $Q_1$ does not record whether $v$ is a root of $G_2$, or which term of
$\pi(G_2)$ it is, while an expansion of $Q_2$ must send the $i$th root of $G_2$ to the $i$th root of
$G_2'$. So $\gamma$ need not be an encoding. For example, let $\theta = 1$, and let $\mathcal{F}_1$
consist of one patchwork, with one vertex $u$, root march $(u)$ and no edges. Let $G_2$ and $G_2'$
both have vertices $u, v$ and no edges, with $\pi(G_2) = (u, v)$ and $\pi(G_2') = (u)$. Then $Q_2/\{v\}$ and
$Q_2'/\{v\}$ are the same patchwork, with one vertex $u$ and root march $(u)$, so $\gamma(Q_2)$ is
simulated in $\gamma(Q_2')$. But $Q_2$ is not simulated in $Q_2'$, because an expansion keeps the
length of the root march.

**Correction.** Since $\mathcal{F}_1$ is well-behaved and an expansion keeps the number of roots,
there is $K_0$ with $|\bar{\pi}(G)| \le K_0$ for every $(G, \mu, \Delta) \in \mathcal{F}_1$. So
$|\bar{\pi}(G_2)| \le K_0 + 1$. Split $\mathcal{F}_2$ into $K_0 + 2$ sets, by whether
$v \in \bar{\pi}(G_2)$ and, if so, which term of $\pi(G_2)$ it is. A union of finitely many
well-behaved sets is well-behaved, so it suffices to treat each set alone. Within one set, the place
of $v$ is fixed, and $\gamma$ is an encoding. 4.9 holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_9` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Results.lean`, proved by
`GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_9_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Section4d.lean`. The sets of the split are
`GraphMinor.GM20.oneSlash`, and each is well-behaved by `GraphMinor.GM20.wellBehaved_oneSlash`, in
the same file. The bound $K_0$ comes from `GraphMinor.GM18.WellBehaved.exists_roots_length_le` in
`GraphMinor/GM18/Section6Part2.lean`.

## 3. Proof of 4.10, page 334: $\Omega_1$ is a well-quasi-order

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof of 4.10 says "(That $\Omega_1$ is a well-quasi-order is proved in the
same way as theorem 8.4 of [1] and we omit the proof.)", where [1] is Graph Minors IV.

**Proof.** That $\Omega_1$ is a well-quasi-order follows from (8.4) of Graph Minors IV, applied to
stars with one vertex. Simulation is a quasi-order, by (7.1) of Graph Minors IV. Let
$P_i = (G_i, \mu_i, \Delta_i, \phi_i)$ $(i = 1, 2, \dots)$ be a countable sequence of members of
$E(\Omega_1)$. For each $i \ge 1$ let $S_i$ be the star with one vertex $o(S_i)$ and no edges, and
let $\sigma_i(o(S_i)) = G_i$. Then $(S_i, \sigma_i)$ is a star-decomposition of $G_i$: conditions
(i), (ii) and (v) in the definition of a tree-decomposition in Graph Minors IV hold since
$\sigma_i(o(S_i)) = G_i$, condition (iv) holds since $t_1 = t_2 = t_3$ there, and conditions (iii)
and (vi) hold vacuously, since they are about two distinct vertices or about an edge of $S_i$. Also
$|V(\sigma_i(o(S_i)))| = |V(G_i)| \le N$ and $P_i$ is removable, since $P_i \in E(\Omega_1)$. Since
$U(S_i) = \emptyset$, the set $\{P_i | \sigma_i(s) \colon i \ge 1, s \in U(S_i)\}$ is empty, and so
it is well-quasi-ordered by simulation. Hence by (8.4) of Graph Minors IV, with $\Omega = \Omega_2$
and $n = N$, there exist $i' > i \ge 1$ such that $P_i$ is simulated in $P_{i'}$; and so $\Omega_1$
is a well-quasi-order.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM20.wqo_boundedPatchwork` and `GraphMinor.GM20.wqo_omega₁` in
`GraphMinor/GM20/Section4eOmega.lean`, where $E(\Omega_1)$ is `GraphMinor.GM20.BoundedPatchwork`.
They use `GraphMinor.GM04.theorem_8_4_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM04/Patchwork/StarWQO.lean`, and
`GraphMinor.GM04.Simulated.refl` and `GraphMinor.GM04.Simulated.trans` in
`GraphMinor/GM04/Patchwork/Expansion.lean`.

## 4. Proof of 4.10, page 334: Theorem 5.7 of Graph Minors XVIII does not apply

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof sets $\phi_1(e) = Q_2|A_e$ for each $e \in E(G_1)$, and says that
"theorem 5.7 of [4] implies that $\gamma$ is an encoding", where [4] is Graph Minors XVIII. That
theorem is about a rooted location $\{A_e : e \in E(G_1)\}$ with one edge of the heart for each of its
members, and with the patches of $P_2|A_e$. Neither holds here. First, two parallel edges
$e \ne e'$ of $G_1$ with the same march and $E(A_e) = E(A_{e'}) = \emptyset$ have $A_e = A_{e'}$, so
the map $e \mapsto A_e$ need not be injective, and the heart has fewer edges than $G_1$. Second, a
condensation only asks that each grouping in $\Delta_1(e)$ be feasible in $P_2|A_e$, so the patches
$\Delta_1(e)$ can be smaller than those of the heart.

**Correction.** Check directly that $\gamma$ is an encoding. Every vertex of $A_e$ is a root of
$A_e$, since $V(A_e)$ is the set of ends of $e$ and $\pi(A_e) = \mu_1(e)$. Let $\eta$ be a
realizable expansion of $Q_1 = \gamma(Q_2)$ in $Q_1' = \gamma(Q_2')$. For each $e$, the inequality
$\phi_1(e) \le \phi_1'(\eta(e))$ says that $Q_2|A_e$ is simulated in $Q_2'|A'_{\eta(e)}$. Combining
these simulations gives an expansion of $Q_2$ in $Q_2'$ that sends each edge of $A_e$ into
$A'_{\eta(e)}$. A realization of it is put together from realizations of the pieces $A'_{e'}$ that
$\eta$ misses. 4.10 holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_10` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Results.lean`, proved by
`GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_10_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Section4e.lean`. The direct check is
`GraphMinor.GM20.simulated_of_pieces`, and the fact about roots is
`GraphMinor.GM20.Pieces.mem_roots`, both in the same file.

## 5. Proof of 4.12, page 336: the root march in $\mathcal{F}_3$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof builds $\mathcal{F}_3$ from $\mathcal{F}_1$ by changing the root march
(using 4.5), then $\mathcal{F}_4$ from $\mathcal{F}_3$ by 4.11, and $\mathcal{F}_5$ from
$\mathcal{F}_4$ by 4.6. It then says that $((G_1^-, \mu_2(f)), \mu_1, \Delta_1) \in \mathcal{F}_3$,
"and so $P_2 \setminus f \in \mathcal{F}_4$". But the members of $\mathcal{F}_4$ obtained in this way
from $((G_1^-, \mu_2(f)), \mu_1, \Delta_1)$ have root march $\mu_2(f)$, while $P_2 \setminus f$ has
root march $\pi(G_2) = \pi(G_1)$. These differ in general, for instance when $G_1$ has no roots and
$f$ has an end. Also, 4.6 needs every end of $f$ to be a root, which holds for the root march
$\mu_2(f)$ but not for $\pi(G_1)$.

**Correction.** Use a longer root march, and restore $\pi(G_1)$ at the end with 4.5. Since
$\mathcal{F}_1$ is well-behaved, there is $K_0$ with $|\bar{\pi}(G)| \le K_0$ for every
$(G, \mu, \Delta) \in \mathcal{F}_1$. In the definition of $\mathcal{F}_3$, replace the bound $k$ by
$K_0 + k$. Let $\pi'$ be $\pi(G_1)$ followed by the terms of $\mu_2(f)$ that are not in
$\bar{\pi}(G_1)$, and for a patchwork $P$ whose hypergraph contains every term of $\pi'$, let $(P)'$
be $P$ with root march $\pi'$. Then $((G_1^-, \pi'), \mu_1, \Delta_1) \in \mathcal{F}_3$, so
$(P_2 \setminus f)' \in \mathcal{F}_4$, and $(P_2)' \in \mathcal{F}_5$, because every end of $f$ is a
term of $\pi'$. The patchwork $(P_2)'$ is active, because $G_1$ is active and every vertex that
$G_1 \setminus A$ shares with $A$ is a root of $A$, that is, an end of $f$. Let $\mathcal{F}_6$ be
related to the set of active members of $\mathcal{F}_5$ as $\mathcal{F}_2$ is to $\mathcal{F}_1$ in
4.5, with $K_0$ in place of $k$. By 4.5, $\mathcal{F}_6$ is well-behaved, and
$P_2 \in \mathcal{F}_6$ since $\pi(G_2) = \pi(G_1)$. So $\mathcal{F}_2 \subseteq \mathcal{F}_6$, and
4.12 holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_12` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Results.lean`, proved by
`GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_12_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Section4g.lean`.

## 6. Statement of 4.13, page 336: the root march of $G_2$

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** The conditions of 4.13 relate $G_2$ to $G_1$ only through the hypergraphs
without roots: $G_2^- \setminus F = G_1^- \cap \bigcap((G_1 \setminus A)^- : A \in \mathcal{L})$ and
$P_2 \setminus F = P_1|(G_2 \setminus F)$. So nothing bounds the root march $\pi(G_2)$. With $w = 0$
and $F = \emptyset$, the patchworks in $\mathcal{F}_2$ can have the hypergraphs of members of
$\mathcal{F}_1$ with any root marches. When the members of $\mathcal{F}_1$ have unboundedly many
vertices, $\mathcal{F}_2$ can then contain patchworks $P_1', P_2', \dots$ where $P_i'$ has $i$ roots.
Such a set is not well-behaved: an expansion keeps the length of the root march, so no $P_i'$ is
simulated in a later $P_j'$. In 4.12, by contrast, the equation $G_2 \setminus f = G_1 \setminus A$
is one of rooted hypergraphs, and it gives $\pi(G_2) = \pi(G_1)$.

**Correction.** Add the condition $\pi(G_2) = \pi(G_1)$. With it, 4.13 follows from $w$ applications
of 4.12, as the paper says. The only use of 4.13 is in the proof of 5.5, where $G_1$ and $G_2$ have no
roots, so the condition holds there.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_13` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Results.lean`, proved by
`GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_4_13_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Section4i.lean`.

## 7. Proof of 10.5, page 356: the reference [1], not [2]

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** The proof ends "By the discussion in Section 7 of [2], it follows that $G_i$ is
isomorphic to a minor of $G_j$". Reference [2] of the paper is Graph Minors X, whose Section 7 does
not discuss patchworks. The discussion that turns a simulation of the patchworks of two graphs into
a minor is in Section 7 of Graph Minors IV, which is reference [1].

**Correction.** The text reads "By the discussion in Section 7 of [1]". The statement is not
affected.

`GraphMinor.GM20.theorem_10_5_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM20/Section10d.lean`. The step from a
simulation to a minor is `GraphMinor.GM20.isDirMinorModel_of_simulated` in
`GraphMinor/GM20/Directed.lean`.

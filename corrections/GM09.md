# Graph Minors IX: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. IX. Disjoint crossed paths*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B
**49** (1990), 40–77.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM09/GM09.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM09/GM09.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Proof of (3.4), page 48: a crooked subtransaction of each cardinality

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says only "The proof is easy."

**Proof.** We give the details. Let $(A, B)$ be a fence for $\mathscr{P}$, and let
$W = V(\mathscr{P})$. Since $\mathscr{P}$ is non-null, $W$ meets both $A$ and $B$. So, going once
around $\Omega$, we find $a' \in A \cap W$ and $b \in B \cap W$ with
$\Omega(a', b) \cap W = \emptyset$, and in the same way $b' \in B \cap W$ and $a \in A \cap W$ with
$\Omega(b', a) \cap W = \emptyset$.

(1) *Let $x \in A \cap W$ and $y \in B \cap W$. If $x \ne a'$ then $a' \in \Omega(x, y)$, and if
$x = a'$ and $y \ne b$ then $b \in \Omega(x, y)$. Similarly, if $y \ne b'$ then
$b' \in \Omega(y, x)$, and if $y = b'$ and $x \ne a$ then $a \in \Omega(y, x)$.*

For the first statement, let $x \ne a'$. Then $x \in \Omega(b, a')$, since $x \in W$ and $x \ne b$.
If $y = b$ this says that $a' \in \Omega(x, y)$. If $y \ne b$ then also $y \in \Omega(b, a')$, and
if $(b, x, y, a')$ were clockwise then $\{x, a'\} \subseteq A$ would cross $\{b, y\} \subseteq B$,
contrary to the definition of a fence. So $(b, y, x, a')$ is clockwise, and again
$a' \in \Omega(x, y)$. For the second statement, $y \in \Omega(b, a')$ since $y \in W$ and
$y \ne b$, and so $b \in \Omega(a', y)$. The last two statements follow in the same way. This proves
(1).

Suppose first that $p = 2$. Let $P_1, P_2 \in \mathscr{P}$ have ends $a'$ and $b$ respectively. If
$P_1 = P_2$ then $\Omega(a', b) \cap W = \emptyset$ shows that $P_1$ is peripheral in $\mathscr{P}$,
which is impossible. So $P_1 \ne P_2$, and we may write their ends as $a', b_1$ and $a_2, b$, where
$b_1 \ne b$ and $a_2 \ne a'$, since $P_1$ and $P_2$ are disjoint. By (1), $a' \in \Omega(a_2, b_1)$
and $b \in \Omega(a', b_1)$, and so $(a_2, a', b, b_1)$ is clockwise and $\{a', b_1\}$ crosses
$\{a_2, b\}$. Hence each of $\Omega(a', b_1)$, $\Omega(b_1, a')$ contains an end of $P_2$, and each
of $\Omega(a_2, b)$, $\Omega(b, a_2)$ contains an end of $P_1$. Thus $\{P_1, P_2\}$ is a crooked
transaction, with fence $(A, B)$.

Now suppose that $p \ge 4$. It is enough to show that if $|\mathscr{P}| \ge 5$ then
$\mathscr{P} - \{P\}$ is crooked for some $P \in \mathscr{P}$, for then we may remove members one at
a time until $p$ of them remain. At most four members of $\mathscr{P}$ have one of $a', b, b', a$ as
an end, and so some $P \in \mathscr{P}$ has none of them as an end. Let
$\mathscr{P}' = \mathscr{P} - \{P\}$, a transaction with fence $(A, B)$. Then $V(\mathscr{P}')$
contains $a', b, b', a$. Let $R \in \mathscr{P}'$ have ends $x \in A$ and $y \in B$. Suppose that
$\Omega(x, y) \cap V(\mathscr{P}') = \emptyset$. If $x \ne a'$ then $a' \in \Omega(x, y)$ by (1), a
contradiction. If $x = a'$ then $y \ne b$, for otherwise $\Omega(x, y) \cap W = \emptyset$ and $R$
would be peripheral in $\mathscr{P}$, and so $b \in \Omega(x, y)$ by (1), again a contradiction. In
the same way, using $b'$ and $a$, the set $\Omega(y, x) \cap V(\mathscr{P}')$ is not empty. So $R$
is not peripheral in $\mathscr{P}'$, and $\mathscr{P}'$ is crooked.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_3_4` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Results.lean`, proved as
`GraphMinor.GM09.Society.theorem_3_4_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Section3a.lean`, with
`GraphMinor.GM09.Society.exists_crooked_pair_of_nonempty` for $p = 2$ and
`GraphMinor.GM09.Society.exists_crooked_diff_singleton_of_ncard_ge_five` for the step that removes

## 2. Statement of (8.1), page 60: a society with $\bar{\Omega} = \emptyset$

**Kind.** Degenerate case.

**What goes wrong.** (8.1) states that every society with no transaction of cardinality $> k$ has a
linear decomposition of depth $\le k$. A linear decomposition of $(G, \Omega)$ is an enumeration
$\{t_1, \dots, t_n\}$ of $\bar{\Omega}$ with subgraphs $X_1, \dots, X_n$ of $G$, and condition (i)
asks that $X_1 \cup \dots \cup X_n = G$. Let $\bar{\Omega} = \emptyset$ and let $G$ have at least
one vertex. Every transaction is then empty, so there is no transaction of cardinality $> k$, for
any $k \ge 0$. But $n = |\bar{\Omega}| = 0$, so the union in (i) is the null graph, which is not
$G$.

**Correction.** Add the hypothesis that $\bar{\Omega} \ne \emptyset$ or $V(G) = \emptyset$. With it,
(8.1) holds. The Lean proofs of Graph Minors XV, (2.1), and Graph Minors XVI, (3.2), use (8.1) only
for societies with $\bar{\Omega} \ne \emptyset$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_8_1` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Results.lean`. The form used in Graph
Minors XV and XVI is `GraphMinor.GM15.GM09Theorem81Statement` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Cited.lean`,
proved by `GraphMinor.GM09.gm15_theorem_8_1` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Bridge.lean`.

## 3. Proof of (10.1), page 64: the uncrossing of the nest and the paths

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says that "the proof of (10.1) is very similar to the proof of [2,
Theorem (4.1)], and we omit it". Here [2] is Graph Minors III.

**Proof.** We give the proof, following the proof of [2, Theorem (4.1)]. Since $X$ is weakly-linked
there are $r$ mutually disjoint $X \to \bar{\Omega}_0$ paths, and by hypothesis there is an
$s$-nest. Choose an $s$-nest $(C_1, \dots, C_s)$ and mutually disjoint $X \to \bar{\Omega}_0$ paths
$P_1, \dots, P_r$ such that $|E(C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_s \cup P_1 \cup \dots \cup P_r)|$ is minimum.
For $1 \le j \le s$ let $\Delta_j$ be the closed disc bounded by the image of $C_j$, as in the
definition of an $s$-nest. Suppose that $P_i \cap C_j$ is not a path for some $i, j$, and choose
such a $j$ as large as possible. Each $P_i$ meets $C_j$, since it runs from a vertex in
$bd(\Delta)$, which is not in the interior of $\Delta_j$, to a vertex in
$\Delta_0 \subseteq \Delta_j$. We call a subpath $P$ of some $P_{i'}$ a *detour* if its ends are
distinct vertices of $C_j$ and no other vertex and no edge of $P$ is in $C_j$. A detour is drawn
either in $\Delta_j$, or outside $\Delta_j$ except for its ends.

(1) *No detour is drawn outside $\Delta_j$ except for its ends.*

For suppose that a detour $P \subseteq P_{i'}$ with ends $v, v'$ is drawn in this way. First, $P$ is
disjoint from $C_m$ for $m \ne j$. For $m < j$ this holds since $C_m$ is drawn in the interior of
$\Delta_j$ and $v, v' \notin V(C_m)$. Let $m > j$, and suppose that $P$ has a vertex $w$ in $C_m$.
The path $P_{i'}$ starts outside the interior of $\Delta_m$, and so it meets $C_m$ before it reaches
$v$ and $v'$, which are drawn in the interior of $\Delta_m$. By the choice of $j$, $P_{i'} \cap C_m$
is a path, and so it contains the part of $P_{i'}$ between this first vertex and $w$, which contains
$v$ or $v'$. This is impossible, since $v, v' \notin V(C_m)$. Now let $Q_1, Q_2$ be the two paths of
$C_j$ between $v$ and $v'$. Then $P \cup Q_1$ and $P \cup Q_2$ are circuits, and since $P$ is drawn
outside $\Delta_j$, one of them, say $P \cup Q_1$, bounds a closed disc that includes $\Delta_j$.
This disc is included in $\Delta_{j+1}$ if $j < s$, since $P$ misses $C_{j+1}$, and in $\Delta$ if
$j = s$. So replacing $C_j$ by $P \cup Q_1$ gives an $s$-nest. Some edge of $Q_2$ is in none of
$P_1, \dots, P_r$. For otherwise, since these paths are disjoint and $Q_2$ is connected, $Q_2$ would
be a subpath of $P_{i'}$ between $v$ and $v'$ other than $P$, which is impossible. Hence the
replacement makes the union smaller, contrary to the choice of the nest and the paths. This proves
(1).

(2) *For $1 \le i' \le r$ and each vertex $u$ of $P_{i'} \cap C_j$, the subpath of $P_{i'}$ from $u$
to its end in $\bar{\Omega}_0$ is drawn in $\Delta_j$.*

For if it leaves $\Delta_j$ then it comes back, since its end is in $\Delta_0 \subseteq \Delta_j$,
and so it contains a detour drawn outside $\Delta_j$ except for its ends, contrary to (1).

Now $P_i \cap C_j$ is non-null and not a path, and so some subpath of $P_i$ between two vertices of
$C_j$ is not included in $C_j$. A minimal such subpath $P$ is a detour, with ends $v, v'$ say, and
by (1) it is drawn in $\Delta_j$. Let $Q_1, Q_2$ be the two paths of $C_j$ between $v$ and $v'$.
Then $P$ splits $\Delta_j$ into two closed discs, bounded by $P \cup Q_1$ and $P \cup Q_2$. The
drawing uses no point in the interior of $\Delta_0$, and so we may choose $k \in \{1, 2\}$ such that
every connected subset of $\Delta_j$ that misses $P$ and meets $Q_k$ is disjoint from $\Delta_0$.
Then no $P_{i'}$ with $i' \ne i$ meets $Q_k$. For if $u$ is a common vertex, then by (2) the subpath
of $P_{i'}$ from $u$ to its end in $\bar{\Omega}_0$ is drawn in $\Delta_j$, and it misses $P$, since
$P_{i'}$ and $P_i$ are disjoint, and this contradicts the choice of $k$. Now replace $P$ by $Q_k$ in
$P_i$. The result contains an $X \to \bar{\Omega}_0$ path $P'_i$, with edges in
$(E(P_i) - E(P)) \cup E(Q_k)$, and $P'_i$ is disjoint from the other paths $P_{i'}$. The edge of $P$
incident with $v$ is in no circuit $C_m$, since it is not in $C_j$ and the other circuits do not
contain $v$, and it is in no $P_{i'}$ with $i' \ne i$. Hence replacing $P_i$ by $P'_i$ makes the
union smaller, again contrary to the choice. This proves (10.1).

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_10_1` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Results.lean`, proved as
`GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_10_1_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM09/NestUncrossing.lean`, by the minimum
counterexample argument `GraphMinor.GM09.nest_uncrossing_of_gt` in the same file.

## 4. Proof of (10.2), page 64: $Q_i$ runs along $C_{s+1-i}$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof takes $Q_i$ to be the union of the minimal subpaths of $P_i$ and
$P_{s+i}$ from $a_i$ and $b_i$ to $V(C_i)$ and a subpath of $C_i$. In an $s$-nest $C_1$ is the
innermost circuit, since $\Delta_0 \subseteq \Delta_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq \Delta_s$. Let
$s \ge 2$. The path $Q_1$ contains the minimal subpath of $P_1$ from $a_1$ to $V(C_1)$, and this
subpath passes through $C_s$, since $a_1$ is drawn in $bd(\Delta)$ and $C_1$ is drawn inside $C_s$.
The path $Q_s$ contains the subpath of $C_s$ that goes clockwise from where $P_s$ reaches $C_s$ to
where $P_{2s}$ reaches $C_s$. Since $(a_s, \dots, a_1, b_1, \dots, b_s)$ is clockwise and the paths
$P_1, \dots, P_{2s}$ are disjoint, their parts outside $C_s$ reach $C_s$ in the same cyclic order as
their ends lie in $bd(\Delta)$. So this subpath of $C_s$ passes through the vertices where $P_1$
meets $C_s$, and $Q_1$ and $Q_s$ meet. Hence $\{Q_1, \dots, Q_s\}$ is not a transaction.

**Correction.** Let $Q_i$ run along $C_{s+1-i}$: it is the union of the minimal subpath of $P_i$
from $a_i$ to $V(C_{s+1-i})$, the minimal subpath of $P_{s+i}$ from $b_i$ to $V(C_{s+1-i})$, and the
subpath of $C_{s+1-i}$ between their ends for which the disc bounded by $C_{s+1-i}$ lies on the same
side of $Q_i$ as $\Omega(b_i, a_i)$ (this is the subpath that goes clockwise). Then the path with
the shortest interval $\Omega(a_i, b_i)$ runs along the outermost circuit, the paths are disjoint,
and the rest of the proof is unchanged.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_10_2` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Results.lean`, proved as
`GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_10_2_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Section10d.lean`, with the arcs and sides
of `GraphMinor.GM09.exists_detour` in the same file.

## 5. Proof of (10.3), page 64: the neighborhood after $x$ is removed from $\Omega$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof takes $x \in \bar{\Omega} - X$, puts
$\Omega' = \Omega \mid (\bar{\Omega} - \{x\})$, and says that $(G, \Omega', \Omega_0)$ is a rural
neighborhood of breadth $\ge p + 4$. In a presentation of $(G, \Omega, \Omega_0)$ the vertex $x$ is
drawn in $bd(\Delta)$. A presentation of $(G, \Omega', \Omega_0)$ must draw in $bd(\Delta)$ only the
vertices of $\bar{\Omega}'$, and $x \notin \bar{\Omega}'$. So the presentation at hand is not a
presentation of $(G, \Omega', \Omega_0)$. A new presentation is needed, for instance with $\Delta$
enlarged near $x$, and the proof does not give one.

**Correction.** Keep the neighborhood $(G, \Omega, \Omega_0)$, and let only the set that holds the
ends shrink. Let $X \subseteq Y \subseteq \bar{\Omega}$, and let $\mathscr{P}$ be a crooked
transaction in $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$ with $|\mathscr{P}| \ge p$ and $V(\mathscr{P}) \subseteq Y$.
We show by induction on $|Y - X|$ that $\mathscr{P}$ may be chosen with
$V(\mathscr{P}) \subseteq X$. The theorem is the case $Y = \bar{\Omega}$. If $Y \ne X$, take
$x \in Y - X$. By the inductive hypothesis applied to $Y - \{x\}$, it suffices to find a crooked
transaction of cardinality $\ge p$ with all ends in $Y - \{x\}$. The paths $Q_1, \dots, Q_s$ are
found by (10.2) for $(G, \Omega, \Omega_0)$ as printed, and claims (1) and (2) are unchanged. In
claim (3), "$x \notin V(\mathscr{P})$" becomes "$V(\mathscr{P}) \subseteq Y - \{x\}$", and (5.2) is
applied to the society $(G \cup G_0, \Omega \mid Y)$. Its crosses and crooked transactions are those
of $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$ with all ends in $Y$, since $\Omega \mid Y$ orders $Y$ as $\Omega$ does.
So $\mathscr{P}$ is a crooked transaction in it, $\{Q_1, \dots, Q_s\}$ is a transaction in it, and
outcome (i) of (5.2) is excluded by (2). A crooked transaction given by outcome (ii) has no end in
$\Omega(a_1, b_1)$, which contains $x$, and so all its ends are in $Y - \{x\}$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_10_3_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM09/Section10e.lean`, with the step
`GraphMinor.GM09.theorem_10_3_step` in the same file.

## 6. Proof of (10.3), page 65: (5.2) for paths with internal vertices in $\bar{\Omega}$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** Claim (3) of the proof of (10.3) follows "from (5.2)", applied to the
transaction $\{Q_1, \dots, Q_s\}$ given by (10.2). But (5.2) asks that each $Q_i$ have no internal
vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$, and the paths of (10.2) may have such vertices. They are built from paths
$X \to \bar{\Omega}_0$, which may pass through vertices of $\bar{\Omega} - X$ (for instance through
$x$), and from parts of circuits of a nest, which may meet $\bar{\Omega}$.

**Correction.** Claim (3) follows from (5.2), applied to the society $(G \cup G_0, \Omega \mid Y)$
of the previous correction, in the following form. The paths $Q_i$ come from
(10.2), and they may have internal vertices in $\bar{\Omega}$, which (5.2) does not allow. But (5.2)
remains true without the hypothesis that $Q_1, \dots, Q_{p+4}$ have no internal vertex in
$\bar{\Omega}$. To see this, let $(G', \Omega')$ be the society obtained from $(G, \Omega)$ by
adding, for each $v \in \bar{\Omega}$, a new vertex $v'$ and a new edge joining $v$ and $v'$, where
$\bar{\Omega}'$ is the set of new vertices and $\Omega'(v') = u'$ when $\Omega(v) = u$. For an
$\Omega$-path $P$ with ends $a, b$, let $P'$ be the $\Omega'$-path with ends $a', b'$ obtained from
$P$ by adding the new edges at $a$ and $b$. Since the new vertices have degree one, every
$\Omega'$-path is $P'$ for exactly one $\Omega$-path $P$, and no $\Omega'$-path has an internal
vertex in $\bar{\Omega}'$. This correspondence keeps crossing, and so it maps separating paths to
separating paths, transactions to transactions, and crooked transactions to crooked transactions of
the same cardinality, and $\Omega(a, b) \cap V(\mathcal{P})$ is empty if and only if
$\Omega'(a', b') \cap V(\mathcal{P}')$ is empty. Given $(G_1, G_2)$ as in (5.2), let $G'_1$ be $G_1$
with the new edges at the vertices in $\Omega(a_{p+4}, b_{p+4}) \cup \{a_{p+4}, b_{p+4}\}$, and let
$G'_2$ be $G_2$ with the new edges at the vertices in $\Omega(b_{p+4}, a_{p+4})$ and with the
vertices $a'_{p+4}, b'_{p+4}$. Then $(G'_1, G'_2)$ is a separation of $G'$ with
$V(G'_1 \cap G'_2) = V(Q'_{p+4})$, $\Omega'(a'_{p+4}, b'_{p+4}) \subseteq V(G'_1)$ and
$\Omega'(b'_{p+4}, a'_{p+4}) \subseteq V(G'_2)$. So (5.2) applies to $(G', \Omega')$ and
$Q'_1, \dots, Q'_{p+4}$, and its outcome (i) or (ii) there gives outcome (i) or (ii) for
$(G, \Omega)$, since $P'_1 \cap G'_2$ is null exactly when $P_1 \cap G_2$ is null.

## 8. Proof of (10.4), page 66: the extended paths form a crooked transaction

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof extends each member $P$ of a crooked transaction of
$(G_1 \cup G_0, \Omega_1)$ to a path $P^+$ with ends in $X$, and says that the paths $P^+$ form "the
desired crooked transaction", with no reason.

**Proof.** We check this. The subpath of $P_i$ between $x_i$ and $y_i$ meets $\Delta_1$ only in
$y_i$, since $y_i$ is the first vertex of $P_i$ in $V(C_{p+4})$ and $x_i \in bd(\Delta)$ is not in
$\Delta_1$. So this subpath meets $G_1 \cup G_0$ only in $y_i$, because $G_1$ is drawn in $\Delta_1$
and $V(G \cap G_0) = \bar{\Omega}_0$ is drawn in $\Delta_0 \subseteq \Delta_1$. Since also the
members of $\mathscr{P}$ are disjoint and $P_1, \dots, P_{2p+8}$ are disjoint, the paths $P^+$
$(P \in \mathscr{P})$ are mutually disjoint. Let $H_2$ be the subgraph of $G$ drawn in the closure
of $\Delta - \Delta_1$. Then $(H_2, \Omega, \Omega_1)$ is a rural neighborhood, and the subpaths of
$P_1, \dots, P_{2p+8}$ between $x_i$ and $y_i$ are disjoint paths of $H_2$ from $\bar{\Omega}$ to
$\bar{\Omega}_1$. Hence for distinct $i_1, i_2, i_3, i_4$, $\{x_{i_1}, x_{i_3}\}$ crosses
$\{x_{i_2}, x_{i_4}\}$ in $\Omega$ if and only if $\{y_{i_1}, y_{i_3}\}$ crosses
$\{y_{i_2}, y_{i_4}\}$ in $\Omega_1$. A cyclic order of a finite set is determined, up to reversal,
by which of its pairs cross. So the ends of the paths $P^+$ lie around $\Omega$ in the same cyclic
order, up to reversal, as the corresponding ends of the members of $\mathscr{P}$ around $\Omega_1$.
Whether a set of disjoint paths is a transaction, and which of its members are peripheral, depends
only on this order. Hence $\{P^+ : P \in \mathscr{P}\}$ is a crooked transaction in
$(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$, of cardinality $|\mathscr{P}| \ge p$.

## 9. Proof of (11.1), page 67: two lawns meet only in common ends

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says that "the proof is clear, although tedious to write out, and we
omit it".

**Proof.** The definition of the discs $\Delta(P)$ presupposes that the drawing meets $bd(\Delta_0)$
only in the vertices of $\bar{\Omega}_0$, and we assume this. Then each path $P$ satisfying (i),
(ii) and (iii) is drawn as an arc with both ends in $bd(\Delta_0)$ and no other point in $\Delta_0$,
and we call it an *ear*. The set $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P)$ is a closed disc, whose boundary consists
of the drawing of $P$ and the portion of $bd(\Delta_0)$ not in $\Delta(P)$, and the interior of
$\Delta(P)$ misses $\Delta_0$. We use three facts about ears, and we prove them first. We use
several times that a connected set that misses the boundary of a closed disc lies in its interior or
outside it.

(a) *If the drawing of an ear $P'$, except its ends, lies in $\Delta(P)$ for an ear $P$, then
$\Delta(P') \subseteq \Delta(P)$.*

For the boundary of the closed disc $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P')$ lies in $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P)$,
and so, in the plane, $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P') \subseteq \Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P)$. Since the
interior of $\Delta(P')$ misses $\Delta_0$, it lies in $\Delta(P)$, and so does its closure
$\Delta(P')$.

(b) *If the drawings of two ears $P, P'$ meet only in common ends, then one of
$\Delta(P), \Delta(P')$ includes the other, or they meet only in images of common ends of $P$ and
$P'$.*

Suppose that neither disc includes the other. The drawing of $P'$ without its ends is connected and
misses the boundary of $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P)$. If it lay in the interior, it would lie in
$\Delta(P)$, since it misses $\Delta_0$, and then $\Delta(P') \subseteq \Delta(P)$ by (a). So it
lies outside $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P)$. Exchanging $P$ and $P'$, the drawing of $P$ without its ends
lies outside $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P')$. The interior of $\Delta(P)$ is connected and misses the
boundary of $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P')$. It does not lie in $\Delta(P')$, since otherwise
$\Delta(P)$, and so the drawing of $P$, would lie in $\Delta(P')$. Hence the interior of $\Delta(P)$
misses $\Delta(P')$, and exchanging $P$ and $P'$, the interior of $\Delta(P')$ misses $\Delta(P)$.
Now let $z \in \Delta(P) \cap \Delta(P')$. By what we have shown, $z$ is not in the drawing of $P$
or of $P'$ except as an end, and so $z \in bd(\Delta_0)$. Suppose that $z$ is not the image of an
end of $P'$. Then $z$ is in the interior of the closed disc $\Delta_0 \cup \Delta(P')$, and so the
points near $z$ outside $\Delta_0$ lie in $\Delta(P')$. But $z$ is a limit of points of the interior
of $\Delta(P)$, which are outside $\Delta_0$ and not in $\Delta(P')$, a contradiction. So $z$ is the
image of an end of $P'$, and in the same way of an end of $P$.

(c) *Let three arcs join a point $m \notin \Delta_0$ to distinct points $x, y, z$ of $bd(\Delta_0)$,
meeting each other only in $m$, and meeting $\Delta_0$ only in $x$, $y$, $z$ respectively. Then
either the arc to $z$ lies in the disc cut off by the arcs to $x$ and $y$, or this disc is properly
included in the disc cut off by the arcs to $x$ and $z$, or in the disc cut off by the arcs to $y$
and $z$.*

Here the disc cut off by two of the arcs is the closed disc bounded by their union and a portion of
$bd(\Delta_0)$ that does not include $\Delta_0$, as for ears. Let $D$ be the disc cut off by the
arcs to $x$ and $y$, and let $B$ be the portion of $bd(\Delta_0)$ between $x$ and $y$ not in $D$. If
$z \notin B$, then the arc to $z$ without its ends is connected and misses the boundary of the
closed disc $\Delta_0 \cup D$, and near $z$ it is in $D$, so it lies in $D$. Now let $z \in B$. Then
the arc to $z$ without its ends lies outside $\Delta_0 \cup D$, by the same fact, since near $z$ it
is outside $\Delta_0 \cup D$, and it splits the closure of the complement of $\Delta_0 \cup D$, in
the plane with a point at infinity added, into two closed discs. One of them, $D_1$, is bounded by
the arcs to $x$ and $z$ and the portion of $B$ between $x$ and $z$, and the other by the arcs to $y$
and $z$ and the rest of $B$. Suppose that $D_1$ does not contain the point at infinity. Then
$D \cup D_1$ is a closed disc in the plane bounded by the arcs to $y$ and $z$ and a portion of
$bd(\Delta_0)$, and it does not include $\Delta_0$. So it is the disc cut off by the arcs to $y$ and
$z$, and it properly includes $D$, since $D_1$ has points outside $D$. In the other case, the same
argument with $x$ and $y$ exchanged shows that $D$ is properly included in the disc cut off by the
arcs to $x$ and $z$.

Now let $P, P'$ be prominences with distinct lawns $L = \Delta(P)$ and $L' = \Delta(P')$. First we
show that $P$ and $P'$ have no common middle vertex. Suppose that $m$ is the middle vertex of both,
and let $x, y$ be the ends of $P$. We claim that every edge $g$ of $G$ joining $m$ to a vertex
$z \in \bar{\Omega}_0$ is drawn in $L$. If $z \in \{x, y\}$ then $g$ is an edge of $P$, since $G$ is
simple. Otherwise the two paths formed by $g$ and the edge of $P$ at $x$, and by $g$ and the edge of
$P$ at $y$, satisfy (i), (ii) and (iii). By (c), either $g$ is drawn in $L$, or $L$ is properly
included in $\Delta(Q)$ for one of these two paths $Q$, which is impossible by (iv) for $P$. This
proves the claim. Both edges of $P'$ join $m$ to vertices in $\bar{\Omega}_0$, and so by the claim
$P'$ is drawn in $L$. By (a), $L' \subseteq L$, and by (iv) for $P'$, $L' = L$, a contradiction.

So $P$ and $P'$ have no common middle vertex, and $P \ne P'$ since $L \ne L'$. Hence their drawings
meet only in common ends. For a common vertex that is not an end of one of them would be its middle
vertex, and so it would not be in $\bar{\Omega}_0$, and it would be the middle vertex of the other
too. And a common edge has an end that is the middle vertex of one of them, unless both $P$ and $P'$
consist of this edge alone. Now by (b), one of $L, L'$ includes the other, or they meet only in
images of common ends of $P$ and $P'$. In the first case, if say $L \subseteq L'$, then $L = L'$ by
(iv) for $P$, a contradiction. So every element of $L \cap L'$ is the image of a common end of $P$
and $P'$.

# Graph Minors XVI: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XVI. Excluding a non-planar graph*, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. B **89** (2003), 43–76.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM16/GM16.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM16/GM16.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

This paper numbers its results without parentheses, as in "1.3", and we do the same here. The
corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Section 1, page 45: $t_i \in X_i$ in the definition of an $r$-ring

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** The definition asks for sets $X_1, \dots, X_n$ of vertices with
$X_1 \cup \dots \cup X_n = V(G)$, "$t_i \in X_1$ for $1 \le i \le n$", $X_i \cap X_k \subseteq X_j$
for $1 \le i \le j \le k \le n$, and $|X_i| \le r$. As printed, the distinct vertices
$t_1, \dots, t_n$ all lie in $X_1$, which has at most $r$ vertices. So an $r$-ring would have at
most $r$ perimeter vertices.

**Correction.** Read $t_i \in X_i$. This matches the informal description just before the
definition, which glues graphs $G_1, \dots, G_n$ with at most $r$ vertices each and with
$t_i \in V(G_i)$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM16.IsRRing` in `GraphMinor/GM16/Defs.lean`.

## 3. Proof of 2.2, page 48: a tree-decomposition gives a tree-structure

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** Before 2.2 the paper says "It is easy to see the following." No proof of 2.2 is
given. The proof needs the complete graphs that turn the pieces of the tree-decomposition into
torsos, and an induction that splits off one leaf of the tree at a time.

**Proof.** *Proof.* For each edge $tt'$ of $T$, let $K_{tt'}$ be a complete graph with
$V(K_{tt'}) = V(\tau(t) \cap \tau(t'))$, chosen so that no two of these complete graphs have a
common edge, and none has an edge in common with $G$. For each subtree $S$ of $T$, let $G_S$ be the
graph with vertex set $\cup(V(\tau(t)) \colon t \in V(S))$ and with edge set
$$\cup(E(\tau(t)) \colon t \in V(S)) \cup \cup(E(K_{tt'}) \colon tt' \in E(T), t \in V(S), t' \notin V(S)).$$
Then $G_T = G$, since $\cup(\tau(t) \colon t \in V(T)) = G$. Hence it suffices to show that $G_S$ is
a tree-structure over $\mathscr{D}'$ for every subtree $S$ of $T$, and we prove this by induction on
$|V(S)|$.

Suppose first that $V(S) = \{t_0\}$, and let $t_1, \ldots, t_k$ be the neighbours of $t_0$ in $T$.
Then $V(G_S) = V(\tau(t_0))$, $\tau(t_0)$ is a subgraph of $G_S$, and every other edge of $G_S$ is
an edge of some $K_{t_0 t_i}$, and so its ends belong to $V(\tau(t_0) \cap \tau(t_i))$. Thus $G_S$
is a torso of the design of $t_0$ in $(T, \tau)$, and so $G_S \in \mathscr{D}'$.

Now suppose that $|V(S)| \ge 2$. Let $t_0$ be a vertex of $S$ with degree one in $S$, let $t_1$ be
its neighbour in $S$, and let $S' = S \setminus t_0$. Let $H_1$ be the graph obtained from
$G_{\{t_0\}}$ by deleting the edges of $K_{t_0 t_1}$, and let $H_2$ be obtained from $G_{S'}$ in the
same way. We claim that $(H_1, H_2)$ is a separation of $G_S$ with
$V(H_1 \cap H_2) = V(K_{t_0 t_1})$. Every neighbour $t'$ of $t_0$ in $T$ with $t' \ne t_1$ is not in
$V(S)$, since $S$ is a subtree and $t_0$ has degree one in $S$. Hence the edges $tt'$ of $T$ with
$t \in V(S)$ and $t' \notin V(S)$ are the edges $t_0 t'$ with $t' \ne t_1$, and the edges $tt'$ with
$t \in V(S')$, $t' \notin V(S')$ and $t' \ne t_0$. It follows that $H_1 \cup H_2 = G_S$. No edge of
$\tau(t_0)$ belongs to $\tau(t)$ with $t \ne t_0$, and the complete graphs $K_{t_0 t'}$
$(t' \ne t_1)$ appear only in $H_1$, so $H_1$ and $H_2$ have no common edges. Finally, let
$t \in V(S')$. The path of $T$ between $t_0$ and $t$ lies in $S$, and so it passes through $t_1$.
Hence $\tau(t_0) \cap \tau(t) \subseteq \tau(t_1)$, and so
$V(\tau(t_0)) \cap V(\tau(t)) \subseteq V(\tau(t_0) \cap \tau(t_1))$. Since
$V(\tau(t_1)) \subseteq V(H_2)$, it follows that
$V(H_1 \cap H_2) = V(\tau(t_0) \cap \tau(t_1)) = V(K_{t_0 t_1})$. This proves our claim. Since
$K_{t_0 t_1}$ has no edges in common with $H_1$ and $H_2$, and $H_1 \cup K_{t_0 t_1} = G_{\{t_0\}}$
and $H_2 \cup K_{t_0 t_1} = G_{S'}$, it follows that $G_S$ is the clique-sum of $G_{\{t_0\}}$ and
$G_{S'}$. From the inductive hypothesis, $G_{\{t_0\}}$ and $G_{S'}$ are tree-structures over
$\mathscr{D}'$, and hence so is $G_S$. This proves 2.2. $\square$

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM16.theorem_2_2_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM16/Section2.lean`, and
`GraphMinor.GM16.theorem_2_2` in `GraphMinor/GM16/Results.lean`.

## 4. Section 2, page 48: the sentence before 2.3

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** The sentence says that for $n \ge 0$ and a class $\mathscr{D}$ of designs,
"every torso of a member of $\mathscr{D}^n$ is a $(\le n)$-vertex extension of a torso of a member
of $\mathscr{D}$". The paper uses it to deduce 2.3 from 2.1 and 2.2, and again at the end of the
proof of 1.3. Recall from page 47 that $(H', M') \in \mathscr{D}^n$ means that $(H', M')$ is an
$n$-enlargement of some $(H, M) \in \mathscr{D}$, with a set $Z \subseteq V(H')$ of at most $n$
vertices. The definition allows $Z$ to meet $V(H)$, and then the sentence can fail, because a torso
of $(H', M')$ can have an edge with both ends in $Z \cap V(H)$. For example, let $H$ have two
vertices $a, b$ and no edges, and let $\mathscr{D} = \{(H, \emptyset)\}$. With $Z = \{a, b\}$, the
design $(H, \{\{a, b\}\})$ is a $2$-enlargement of $(H, \emptyset)$, and the graph with one edge
$ab$ is a torso of it. The only torso of $(H, \emptyset)$ is $H$. Deleting vertices from the edge
$ab$ never gives $H$, since $H$ has both vertices and no edge.

**Correction.** Let $G$ be a torso of $(H', M')$, and let $G'$ be the union of $H$ and
$G \setminus Z$. Then $G'$ is a torso of $(H, M)$. Its vertex set is $V(H)$, since
$V(H') \setminus V(H) \subseteq Z$. An edge of $G \setminus Z$ that is not in $H$ is not in $H'$,
since every edge of $H'$ is an edge of $H$. So its ends lie in some $X \in M'$, and $X \ne Z$
because the ends are not in $Z$. Its ends are in $V(H)$, so they lie in $X \cap V(H)$, which is in
$M$ by the definition of an enlargement. Also $G \setminus Z = G' \setminus Z$. So the sentence
holds in the weaker form: $G$ contains a torso $G'$ of a member of $\mathscr{D}$, and
$G \setminus X = G' \setminus X$ for some $X \subseteq V(G)$ with $|X| \le n$. When
$Z \cap V(H) = \emptyset$, the graph $G'$ is $G \setminus Z$, so in that case the sentence holds as
printed. The corrections to 2.3 and to page 52 of the proof of 1.3 below carry the weaker form
through.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM16.IsLooseVertexExtension` in `GraphMinor/GM16/Defs.lean` (the weaker form),
and `GraphMinor.GM16.exists_isTorsoOfDesign_isLooseVertexExtension_of_isGraphEnlargement` in
`GraphMinor/GM16/Section2.lean` (the sentence in that form). The example is
`GraphMinor.GM16.Examples.not_exists_torso_isVertexExtension_completeGraph_two` in
`Checks/GM16Examples.lean`.

## 5. Statement of 2.3, page 48: the second case

**Kind.** Proof gap.

extension of a torso of a member of $\mathscr{D}$". The paper deduces 2.3 from 2.1 and 2.2 by the

holds as printed. Its only use is in the proof of 1.3, and the correction to page 52 of that proof
below shows that the weaker form is enough there.

## 7. Proof of 1.3, page 51: $\mathcal{L}$ is a location with its design in $\mathcal{D}$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof says "Then since $\mathcal{S}$ is a segregation of $G$, and
$(A_1^j, B_1^j), \dots, (A_{n_j}^j, B_{n_j}^j)$ satisfy 3.2, it is straightforward to verify (we
omit the details) that $\mathcal{L}$ is a location in $G \setminus Z$ and every torso of the design
of $\mathcal{L}$ is an outgrowth by $\le d$ $r$-rings of a graph in one of
$\Sigma_1, \dots, \Sigma_s$". The verification is not given. It needs a splitting of each torso into
torsos of the local locations, one for each member of $\mathcal{S}$, and a drawing of the part that
comes from the small members of $\mathcal{S}$.

**Proof.** We give the details. For $(A, \Omega) \in \mathcal{S}$ with $|\bar{\Omega}| \le 3$ let
$\mathcal{L}(A, \Omega) = \{(A, B_0)\}$, where $B_0$ is the graph with vertex set $\bar{\Omega}$ and
no edges, and for $1 \le j \le k$ let
$\mathcal{L}(A_j, \Omega_j) = \{(A_i^j, B_i^j) : 1 \le i \le n_j\}$. In each case
$\mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$ is a location in $A$ (by 3.2 for the vortices), and
$\bar{\Omega} \subseteq V(B)$ for each $(A', B) \in \mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$ (by statement 1 of 3.2).
Thus $\mathcal{L}$ is the set of all $(A', B \cup C(A, \Omega))$ with $(A, \Omega) \in \mathcal{S}$
and $(A', B) \in \mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$. Each of these is a separation of $G \setminus Z$, since
$(A', B)$ is a separation of $A$ and $V(A \cap C(A, \Omega)) = \bar{\Omega} \subseteq V(B)$. Since
$\mathcal{S}$ is a segregation, $C(A, \Omega)$ includes every other member of $\mathcal{S}$. Hence
if two members of $\mathcal{L}$ come from distinct $(A, \Omega), (A^*, \Omega^*) \in \mathcal{S}$,
the first side of the first is included in $A \subseteq C(A^*, \Omega^*)$; and if they come from the
same $(A, \Omega)$, this holds since $\mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$ is a location. So $\mathcal{L}$ is a
location in $G \setminus Z$.

Now let $H$ be a torso of the design $(H_0, M)$ of $\mathcal{L}$. For
$(A', B) \in \mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$ we have $V(A' \cap (B \cup C(A, \Omega))) = V(A' \cap B)$,
since $\bar{\Omega} \subseteq V(B)$. Assign each edge of $H$ not in $H_0$ to some
$(A, \Omega) \in \mathcal{S}$ such that its ends lie in $V(A' \cap B)$ for some
$(A', B) \in \mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$, and each edge of $H_0$ to the member of $\mathcal{S}$ that
contains it. For $(A, \Omega) \in \mathcal{S}$, let $P(A, \Omega)$ be the subgraph of $H$ with
vertex set $V(H) \cap V(A)$ and with the edges assigned to $(A, \Omega)$. Since $A$ is included in
every $C(A^*, \Omega^*)$ with $(A^*, \Omega^*) \ne (A, \Omega)$, a vertex of $A$ is in $H$ if and
only if it is in $V(B)$ for every $(A', B) \in \mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$, and the same holds for edges
of $A$ and $H_0$. Hence $P(A, \Omega)$ is a torso of the design of $\mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$. So
$V(P(A, \Omega)) = \bar{\Omega}$ if $|\bar{\Omega}| \le 3$, and $P(A_j, \Omega_j)$ is a
$(2\rho + 1)$-ring with perimeter $t_1^j, \dots, t_{n_j}^j$ by statement 5 of 3.2. Since
$\mathcal{S}$ is a segregation, the graphs $P(A, \Omega)$ have union $H$, no two have a common edge,
and $V(P(A, \Omega) \cap P(A^*, \Omega^*)) \subseteq \bar{\Omega} \cap \bar{\Omega}^*$. Also
$V(\mathcal{S}) \subseteq V(H)$, since $\bar{\Omega} \subseteq V(B)$ for every
$(A', B) \in \mathcal{L}(A, \Omega)$, and so $V(P(A, \Omega)) \cap V(\mathcal{S}) = \bar{\Omega}$.

Let $\alpha$ be the proper arrangement of $\mathcal{S}$ in
$\Sigma \in \{\Sigma_1, \dots, \Sigma_s\}$, and let $G_0$ be the subgraph of $H$ with vertex set
$V(\mathcal{S})$ and with the edges of all $P(A, \Omega)$ with $|\bar{\Omega}| \le 3$. Then
$H = G_0 \cup P(A_1, \Omega_1) \cup \dots \cup P(A_k, \Omega_k)$. We draw $G_0$ in $\Sigma$ with
each $x \in V(\mathcal{S})$ at $\alpha(x)$; for $|\bar{\Omega}| \le 3$ the edges of $P(A, \Omega)$
have ends in $\bar{\Omega}$, and since $|\bar{\Omega}| \le 3$ they may be drawn in
$\alpha(A, \Omega)$ without crossings, meeting $bd(\alpha(A, \Omega))$ only in their ends. This is a
drawing, since two of the discs meet only in points $\alpha(v)$ with $v \in V(\mathcal{S})$. Let
$\Delta_j = \alpha(A_j, \Omega_j)$ $(1 \le j \le k)$. These discs are pairwise disjoint, since
$\alpha$ is proper. If $x \in V(\mathcal{S})$ and $\alpha(x) \in \Delta_j$, then
$x \in \bar{\Omega}_j$ by the second condition in the definition of an arrangement, and so
$\alpha(x) \in bd(\Delta_j)$. Hence $\Delta_j$ meets the drawing of $G_0$ only in the points
$\alpha(t_i^j)$, which lie on $bd(\Delta_j)$ in the order $t_1^j, \dots, t_{n_j}^j$. Finally
$P(A_j, \Omega_j)$ meets $G_0$ just in $\bar{\Omega}_j$, since
$V(P(A_j, \Omega_j)) \cap V(\mathcal{S}) = \bar{\Omega}_j$, and it is disjoint from
$P(A_{j'}, \Omega_{j'})$ for $j' \ne j$, since $\Delta_j \cap \Delta_{j'} = \emptyset$. Thus $H$ is
an outgrowth by $k \le \kappa = d$ $r$-rings of a graph in $\Sigma$.

## 8. Proof of 1.3, page 51: edges at $Z$ in the lifted location

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** In the subproof of Claim (1), $\mathcal{L}$ is a location in $G \setminus Z$,
and each $(A, B) \in \mathcal{L}$ is lifted to a separation $(A^+, B^+)$ of $G$ with
$Z \subseteq V(A^+ \cap B^+)$, $A^+ \setminus Z = A$, $B^+ \setminus Z = B$, and with every edge of
$E(G) \setminus E(A)$ that has both ends in $V(B^+)$ in $E(B^+)$. The proof says that the design of
$\mathcal{L}^+$ belongs to $\mathscr{D}^\zeta$, as an enlargement of the design of $\mathcal{L}$.
Call the graph of the design of a location its core. By the definition of an enlargement (page 47),
every edge of the core of $\mathcal{L}^+$ must be an edge of the core of $\mathcal{L}$. Let $e$ be
an edge of $G$ with an end in $Z$ and all its ends in $Z$ or in the core of $\mathcal{L}$, such as
an edge with both ends in $Z$ or a loop at a vertex of $Z$. For each $(A, B) \in \mathcal{L}$, the
edge $e$ is not in $A$, since $A \subseteq G \setminus Z$, and both ends of $e$ are in $V(B^+)$. So
$e$ is in every $B^+$, and hence in the core of $\mathcal{L}^+$. But $e$ is not in the core of
$\mathcal{L}$, which lies in $G \setminus Z$.

**Correction.** Add to $\mathcal{L}^+$, for each such edge $e$, the separation
$(K_e, G \setminus e)$, where $K_e$ consists of $e$ and its ends. The result is still a location in
$G$, because $e$ lies in every $B^+$ and in no $A^+$. Each new separation has order at most
$2 < \theta$, so it is in $\mathscr{T}$ by (2.7) of Graph Minors X. The new core is the core of
$\mathcal{L}^+$ without these edges, so it has the same edges as the core of $\mathcal{L}$. An
enlargement also asks that each new set $V(K_e)$ meet the vertex set of the core of $\mathcal{L}$ in
a set of the design of $\mathcal{L}$. This intersection has at most one vertex $u$, since one end of
$e$ is in $Z$, and $\{u\}$ need not be a set of the design. But $u$ lies in one. The graphs $A$ with
$(A, \Omega) \in \mathcal{S}$ cover $G \setminus Z$, and for each vortex $(A_j, \Omega_j)$ the
graphs $A_1^j, \dots, A_{n_j}^j$ cover $A_j$, by (a) in the proof of 3.2. So $u \in V(A)$ for some
$(A, B) \in \mathcal{L}$. Also $u \in V(B)$, since $u$ is in the core, and so $u \in V(A \cap B)$.
So it is enough to close the sets of the design of $\mathcal{L}$ under taking subsets, and to add
the empty set. This does not change its torsos, and $\mathscr{D}$ is defined by the torsos of its
members, so the new design is still in $\mathscr{D}$. The design of the new location is a
$|Z|$-enlargement of it. So Claim (1) holds as printed.

## 9. Proof of 1.3, page 52: the last step and the value of $r$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof of 1.3 fixes $r = 2\rho + 1$, $d = \kappa$ and
$w = \zeta + 4\theta - 3$ on page 51. On page 52 it applies 2.3 to $\mathscr{D}^\zeta$, and then the
sentence before 2.3 with $n = \zeta$, to conclude that each graph $G'$ in the construction is a
$(\le w)$-vertex extension of a torso of a member of $\mathscr{D}$. Both steps use the printed
forms, which the corrections to the sentence before 2.3 and to 2.3 above replace by weaker ones.

**Correction.** Use the weaker forms. In the second case of 2.3, the graph $G'$ contains a torso of
a member of $\mathscr{D}^\zeta$ that agrees with $G'$ outside a set of at most $3\theta - 2$
vertices, and that torso contains a torso $G''$ of a member of $\mathscr{D}$ that agrees with it
outside a set of at most $\zeta$ vertices. Taking $X$ to be the union of the two sets, we get
$G' \setminus X = G'' \setminus X$ with $|X| \le 3\theta - 2 + \zeta \le w$. In the first case of
2.3, where $|V(G')| \le 4\theta - 3 \le w$, the same holds with $G''$ null and $X = V(G')$. Up to
isomorphism, $G''$ is an outgrowth by $\le d$ $r$-rings of a graph in one of
$\Sigma_1, \dots, \Sigma_s$. Deleting at most $n$ vertices from an outgrowth by $\le d$ $r$-rings
gives an outgrowth by $\le d$ $(n + 1)r$-rings. So $G' \setminus X$ is an outgrowth by $\le d$
$(w + 1)r$-rings, and $G'$ is a $(\le w)$-vertex extension of it. So the numbers
$(w + 1)(2\rho + 1)$, $d$ and $w$ satisfy 1.3, and 1.3 holds as printed. The class $\mathscr{D}$ is
still defined with $r = 2\rho + 1$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM16.IsOutgrowthByRings.deleteVertices` in
`GraphMinor/GM16/OutgrowthDeletion.lean` (the deletion step), and `GraphMinor.GM16.theorem_1_3` in
`GraphMinor/GM16/Results.lean`, whose proof uses these three numbers.

# Graph Minors XIV: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XIV. Extending an embedding*, J. Combin. Theory Ser.
B **65** (1995), 23–50.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM14/GM14.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM14/GM14.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Proof of (3.1), page 28: atoms inside $ins(W)$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper states (3.1) and says only "The proof is clear." The three kinds of
atoms correspond to atoms of $K$ in different ways, and the case of a region of $H$ needs an
argument.

**Proof.** We give the details. Let $K'$ be the subgraph of $K$ formed by the vertices and edges of
$W$, so that $ins(W)$ is the union of $U(K')$ and the discs $ins(C)$ for the circuits $C$ of $K'$.
Since $x \subseteq x'$ unless $x$ is a region of $H$, and $x' \subseteq x$ if $x$ is a region, the
condition $x' \subseteq ins(W)$ implies $x \cap ins(W) \ne \emptyset$. For the converse, let
$y \in x \cap ins(W)$. If $x = \{v\}$ with $v \in V(H)$, then $x' = x \subseteq ins(W)$. Suppose
that $x \in E(H)$. Then $y \notin U(K)$, since $U(H) \cap U(K) = V(H)$, and so $y \in ins(C)$ for
some circuit $C$ of $K'$. Now $x'$ is a region of $K$, and so it is connected and disjoint from
$U(C)$, which is the boundary of $ins(C)$. Since $y \in x' \cap ins(C)$, it follows that
$x' \subseteq ins(C) \subseteq ins(W)$. Finally, suppose that $x \in R(H)$, and let $x' = \{w\}$.
The only points of $U(K)$ in $x$ are $w$ and the points of the edges of $K$ incident with $w$. Hence
if $y \in U(K')$, then $w \in V(K') \subseteq ins(W)$. Otherwise $y \in ins(C)$ for some circuit $C$
of $K'$. If $w \in V(C)$, then $w \in ins(C)$. If not, then $x$ is disjoint from $U(C)$, since $C$
has no edge incident with $w$. Since $x$ is connected and meets $ins(C)$, it follows that
$x \subseteq ins(C)$, and so $w \in ins(C) \subseteq ins(W)$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM14.theorem_3_1_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM14/Section3.lean`, and
`GraphMinor.GM14.theorem_3_1` in `GraphMinor/GM14/Results.lean`.

## 2. Section 3, page 28: the definition of a $\lambda$-zone

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** A $\lambda$-zone around $z$ is defined as an open disc $\Lambda$ with
$z \subseteq \Lambda$, "such that $bd(\Lambda)$ is an $O$-arc and hence $\overline{\Lambda}$ is a
closed disc", together with further conditions. The word "hence" claims that the second property
follows from the first, and it does not. Let $\Sigma$ be the projective plane, obtained from a
closed disc by identifying opposite points of its boundary, and let $\Lambda$ be the image of the
interior of the disc. Then $\Lambda$ is an open disc, and $bd(\Lambda)$ is the image of the boundary
circle, which is an $O$-arc. But $\overline{\Lambda}$ is the whole projective plane, not a closed
disc.

**Correction.** Delete "hence", so that "$\overline{\Lambda}$ is a closed disc" is a condition in
the definition. Every $\lambda$-zone built in Section 9 is bounded by a circuit inside a closed
disc, so it satisfies the condition, and the results about $\lambda$-zones hold as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM14.IsLambdaZoneAround` in `GraphMinor/GM14/Defs.lean`.

## 3. Proof of (5.1), page 31: the circuit around $\mathscr{R}$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says that (5.1) "is closely related to the elementary result that the
minimal edge-cutsets of the dual graph of a planar graph correspond to the circuits of the graph,
and may be proved similarly. We omit further details." No proof is given. The formalization proves
(5.1) for surfaces without boundary. The first paragraph of the proof below reduces the general case
to that one.

**Proof.** We give the details, using some results of [5] (Graph Minors XI).

*Proof of (5.1).* We may assume that $\text{bd}(\Sigma) = \emptyset$. For otherwise, let $\Sigma'$
be the surface obtained from $\Sigma$ by pasting a closed disc onto each component of
$\text{bd}(\Sigma)$. Then $H$ is a drawing in $\Sigma'$, and $\Delta$ is a closed disc in $\Sigma'$.
Let $r \in \mathscr{R}$. Then $r$ is connected, and it is open in $\Sigma'$, since it is open in
$\Sigma$ and disjoint from $\text{bd}(\Sigma)$ (because $r \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$).
Moreover every point $x \in \bar{r} - r$ is in $U(H)$, for otherwise the region of $H$ in $\Sigma$
that contains $x$ is an open set meeting $r$, and so equals $r$, which is impossible since
$x \notin r$. Hence $r$ is also a region of $H$ in $\Sigma'$. The incidences between the edges of
$H$ and the members of $\mathscr{R}$ are the same in $\Sigma$ and in $\Sigma'$, and so (5.1) for
$\Sigma'$ implies (5.1) for $\Sigma$.

Thus we assume that $\text{bd}(\Sigma) = \emptyset$. Let $N$ be the subdrawing of $H$ formed by the
vertices and edges of $H$ that are incident both with a member of $\mathscr{R}$ and with a region of
$H$ not in $\mathscr{R}$. (So $N = N_H(\mathscr{R})$ in the notation of [5].) Every vertex and every
edge of $N$ is a subset of $\bar{r}$ for some $r \in \mathscr{R}$, and $\bar{r} \subseteq \Delta$
since $\Delta$ is closed. Hence $U(N) \subseteq \Delta$. For each circuit $C$ of $N$, $U(C)$ is an
O-arc in the closed disc $\Delta$, and so it bounds a unique closed disc
$\text{ins}(C) \subseteq \Delta$. Let $\theta = |E(N)|/2 + 1$. Every circuit of $N$ has length at
most $|E(N)| < 2\theta$. We claim that $\text{ins}$ is a slope in $N$ of order $\theta$, in the
sense of [5]. For the first slope axiom, let $C_1, C_2$ be circuits of $N$ with
$U(C_1) \subseteq \text{ins}(C_2)$. Then $U(C_1)$ bounds a closed disc included in
$\text{ins}(C_2)$, and this disc is $\text{ins}(C_1)$ by uniqueness, since
$\text{ins}(C_2) \subseteq \Delta$. For the second, let $P_0, P_1, P_2$ be as in that axiom. If
$U(P_0) \subseteq \text{ins}(P_1 \cup P_2)$, then $\text{ins}(P_1 \cup P_2)$ includes
$U(P_2 \cup P_0)$ and $U(P_0 \cup P_1)$, and hence includes $\text{ins}(P_2 \cup P_0)$ and
$\text{ins}(P_0 \cup P_1)$, by the first axiom. The same holds if
$U(P_1) \subseteq \text{ins}(P_2 \cup P_0)$ or $U(P_2) \subseteq \text{ins}(P_0 \cup P_1)$, and so
we may assume that none of these three inclusions holds. Since
$U(P_0) \not\subseteq \text{ins}(P_1 \cup P_2)$ and $U(P_1) \not\subseteq \text{ins}(P_2 \cup P_0)$,
the closed discs $\text{ins}(P_1 \cup P_2)$ and $\text{ins}(P_2 \cup P_0)$ meet only in $U(P_2)$,
and their union is a closed disc bounded by $U(P_0 \cup P_1)$. This union is a subset of $\Delta$,
and so it equals $\text{ins}(P_0 \cup P_1)$ by uniqueness. But it includes $U(P_2)$, a
contradiction. This proves our claim. We apply the results of [5, Section 4] with
$\Gamma = \Gamma' = N$. Since every circuit of $N$ has length $< 2\theta$, $N$ is confined.

(1) *All the members of $\mathscr{R}$ are included in the same region of $N$.*

For let $r_1, r_2 \in \mathscr{R}$ be distinct, and both incident with an edge $e$ of $H$. Since $e$
is incident with at most two regions of $H$, it is incident with no region outside $\mathscr{R}$,
and so $e \notin E(N)$. Choose $x \in e$. Then $x \notin U(N)$, since $e \notin E(N)$, and so $x$
belongs to a region $r'$ of $N$. Now $x \in \bar{r}_1 \cap \bar{r}_2$, and so $r'$ meets both $r_1$
and $r_2$. Since $r_1, r_2$ are connected and disjoint from $U(N)$, they are both included in $r'$.
Since $\mathscr{R}$ is connected, (1) follows.

Let $r'$ be the region of $N$ that includes all the members of $\mathscr{R}$. Let $\text{ins}(N)$ be
the union of $U(N)$ and of the discs $\text{ins}(C)$ for all circuits $C$ of $N$. By [5, (4.4)],
$r_0 = \Sigma - \text{ins}(N)$ is a region of $N$. Since $\text{ins}(N) \subseteq \Delta$, it
follows that $\Sigma - \Delta \subseteq r_0$. Now $\Sigma - \Delta$ is open, and it is non-empty,
for otherwise $\Delta = \Sigma$, which is impossible since $\text{bd}(\Delta) \ne \emptyset$ and
$\text{bd}(\Sigma) = \emptyset$. Since $U(H)$ includes no non-empty open subset of $\Sigma$, there
is a point $y \in \Sigma - \Delta$ with $y \notin U(H)$. Let $r_2$ be the region of $H$ that
contains $y$. Then $r_2 \notin \mathscr{R}$, since $y \notin \Delta$, and $r_2 \subseteq r_0$, since
$r_2$ is connected, disjoint from $U(N)$, and contains $y \in r_0$.

(2) *$r' \ne r_0$.*

For suppose that $r' = r_0$, and choose $r_1 \in \mathscr{R}$. Then $r_1, r_2 \subseteq r_0$, and so
by [5, (5.1)], applied with $\Gamma = H$, $\Gamma' = N$ and $Z = \mathscr{R}$ (its proof does not
use the slope), there are regions $r_3 \in \mathscr{R}$ and $r_4 \notin \mathscr{R}$ of $H$, both
incident with some edge $e$ of $H$ with $e \notin E(N)$. But then $e \in E(N)$, from the definition
of $N$, a contradiction. This proves (2).

Now $N$ does not capture $r_0$, since $y \in r_0$ and $y \notin \text{ins}(N)$. In the proof of [5,
(4.4)] it is shown that a confined subdrawing captures at least one of any two distinct regions of
it. By (2), it follows that $N$ captures $r'$, that is, there is a circuit $C$ of $N$ with
$r' \subseteq \text{ins}(C)$. Then $U(C) \subseteq U(N) \subseteq \Delta$, and $U(C)$ bounds the
disc $\text{ins}(C) \subseteq \Delta$, which includes each member of $\mathscr{R}$ by (1). Every
edge of $C$ is an edge of $N$, and so is incident with a member of $\mathscr{R}$. This proves (5.1).
$\square$

## 4. Proof of (5.2), page 31: $\mathscr{R}$ is connected

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof says "By (5.1) it suffices to show that $\mathscr{R}$ is connected,
and this follows easily from the connectedness of $Z$." It does not show that $\mathscr{R}$ is
connected, nor that the disc given by (5.1), which includes each member of $\mathscr{R}$, includes
$Z$ in its interior.

**Proof.** We give the details. Since $U(H)$ includes no non-empty open subset of $\Sigma$, every
point of $\Sigma$ is in the closure of some region of $H$. In particular
$\mathscr{R} \ne \emptyset$, since $Z \ne \emptyset$. To show that $\mathscr{R}$ is connected,
choose $r_0 \in \mathscr{R}$, let $\mathscr{R}_1$ be the set of all $r \in \mathscr{R}$ for which
there is a sequence from $r_0$ to $r$ as in the definition of connectedness, and let
$\mathscr{R}_2 = \mathscr{R} - \mathscr{R}_1$. Suppose that $\mathscr{R}_2 \ne \emptyset$. Let
$K_1$, $K_2$ and $K_3$ be the unions of the closures of the members of $\mathscr{R}_1$, of the
members of $\mathscr{R}_2$, and of the regions of $H$ not in $\mathscr{R}$, respectively. These sets
are closed, since $H$ has only finitely many regions. Every point of $\Sigma$ not in $K_3$ is in
$K_1 \cup K_2$, and $Z \cap K_3 = \emptyset$ by the definition of $\mathscr{R}$. We claim that
$K_1 \cap K_2 \subseteq V(H)$. For let $x \in \bar{r}_1 \cap \bar{r}_2$, where
$r_1 \in \mathscr{R}_1$ and $r_2 \in \mathscr{R}_2$. If $x \notin U(H)$, then the region of $H$ that
contains $x$ is an open set meeting $r_1$ and $r_2$, and so $r_1 = r_2$, which is impossible. If $x$
belongs to an edge $e$ of $H$, then $e$ is incident with $r_1$ and $r_2$, and so
$r_2 \in \mathscr{R}_1$, which is impossible. This proves our claim.

Next we show that $Z \cap K_1 \cap K_2 = \emptyset$. For suppose that $v \in Z \cap K_1 \cap K_2$.
Then $v \in V(H)$ by our claim, and $v \notin K_3$. Since $v \in \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$, $v$ is
not in $\text{bd}(\Sigma)$, and so there is an open disc $W \subseteq \Sigma$ containing $v$ and
disjoint from $K_3$ and from $V(H) - \{v\}$. Then $W - \{v\}$ is connected. Each point of
$W - \{v\}$ is in $K_1 \cup K_2$, since it is not in $K_3$, and it is not in both $K_1$ and $K_2$,
by our claim, since it is not in $V(H)$. Since $K_1, K_2$ are closed, it follows that $W - \{v\}$ is
disjoint from one of $K_1, K_2$, say from $K_i$. But $v \in K_i$, and so $v \in \bar{r}$ for some
region $r$ with $\bar{r} \subseteq K_i$. Then $r$ meets $W$, and $r \cap W \subseteq W - \{v\}$
since $v \in U(H)$. Hence $W - \{v\}$ meets $K_i$, a contradiction. Thus
$Z \cap K_1 \cap K_2 = \emptyset$.

Now $Z \subseteq K_1 \cup K_2$, since $Z \cap K_3 = \emptyset$, and $Z$ meets both $K_1$ and $K_2$,
since $\mathscr{R}_1$ and $\mathscr{R}_2$ are non-empty subsets of $\mathscr{R}$. Hence $Z$ is the
union of the disjoint non-empty sets $Z \cap K_1$ and $Z \cap K_2$, which are closed in $Z$. This is
contrary to the connectedness of $Z$, since $Z$ is arc-wise connected. Thus
$\mathscr{R}_2 = \emptyset$, and $\mathscr{R}$ is connected.

By (5.1), there is a circuit $C$ of $H$ with $U(C) \subseteq \Delta$, bounding a closed disc
$D \subseteq \Delta$ that includes each member of $\mathscr{R}$, such that every edge of $C$ is
incident with a member of $\mathscr{R}$. It remains to show that $Z \subseteq D - U(C)$, the open
disc bounded by $U(C)$. The set $\Sigma - K_3$ is open, and it includes $Z$. Moreover
$\Sigma - K_3 \subseteq D$. For otherwise $(\Sigma - K_3) - D$ is a non-empty open set, and so it
contains a point $y \notin U(H)$. Let $r$ be the region of $H$ that contains $y$. Then
$r \notin \mathscr{R}$, since $y \notin D$ and every member of $\mathscr{R}$ is a subset of $D$, and
so $y \in K_3$, a contradiction. Hence $Z$ is included in the interior of $D$, which is $D - U(C)$.
This proves (5.2).

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM14.theorem_5_2_full` and `GraphMinor.GM14.theorem_5_2_proof`, with the
private lemmas `isConnectedRegionSet_of_isPreconnected` (that $\mathscr{R}$ is connected) and
`subset_interior_of_regions_subset` (that $Z$ is in the interior), in
`GraphMinor/GM14/Section5.lean`, and `GraphMinor.GM14.theorem_5_2` in
`GraphMinor/GM14/Results.lean`.

## 5. Proof of (5.5), page 32: the nested circuits

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says "A similar proof (we omit the details) yields" (5.5). Unlike
(5.3) and (5.4), (5.5) measures the distance to $\text{bd}(\Delta)$ and asks that the circuits avoid
$\text{bd}(\Delta)$, so the proof of (5.3) does not apply word for word.

**Proof.** *Proof of (5.5).* We first show the following analogue of (5.3).

(1) *If $k \ge 2$, there is a circuit $C$ of $H$ with $U(C) \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$,
bounding an open disc $\Lambda$ including $Z$, such that
$\bar{\Lambda} \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$ and the regional distance between
$\bar{\Lambda}$ and $\text{bd}(\Delta)$ is $\ge k - 1$.*

For let $r$ be a region of $H$ with $\bar{r} \cap Z \ne \emptyset$. Then
$\bar{r} \cap \text{bd}(\Delta) = \emptyset$, for otherwise the regional distance between $Z$ and
$\text{bd}(\Delta)$ is $\le 1$. In particular $r$ is disjoint from $\text{bd}(\Delta)$. Moreover $r$
meets $\Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$, since $\bar{r}$ meets $Z \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$
and $\Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$ is open. Since $r$ is connected, it follows that
$r \subseteq \Delta$, and so $\bar{r} \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$. In particular (5.2) may
be applied. Let $C$ be as in (5.2), bounding an open disc $\Lambda \subseteq \Delta$ including $Z$.
Every edge $e$ of $C$ is incident with a region $r$ of $H$ with $\bar{r} \cap Z \ne \emptyset$, and
so $\bar{e} \subseteq \bar{r} \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$. Since every vertex of $C$ is an
end of an edge of $C$, it follows that $U(C) \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$. Since $\Lambda$
is open and $\Lambda \subseteq \Delta$, we have $\Lambda \cap \text{bd}(\Delta) = \emptyset$, and so
$\bar{\Lambda} = \Lambda \cup U(C) \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$. Now let
$v_0, r_1, v_1, \dots, r_{k'}, v_{k'}$ be a chain with $v_0 \in \bar{\Lambda}$ and
$v_{k'} \in \text{bd}(\Delta)$, with $k'$ minimum. Then $k' \ge 1$, since
$\bar{\Lambda} \cap \text{bd}(\Delta) = \emptyset$. Also $v_1 \notin \bar{\Lambda}$, by the
minimality of $k'$ if $k' \ge 2$, and since $v_1 \in \text{bd}(\Delta)$ if $k' = 1$. Hence
$r_1 \not\subseteq \Lambda$, for otherwise $v_1 \in \bar{r}_1 \subseteq \bar{\Lambda}$. Since $r_1$
is connected and disjoint from $U(C)$, it follows that $r_1 \cap \Lambda = \emptyset$, and so
$\bar{r}_1 \cap \Lambda = \emptyset$, since $\Lambda$ is open. Thus $v_0 \notin \Lambda$, since
$v_0 \in \bar{r}_1$, and therefore $v_0 \in U(C)$. Let $e$ be an edge of $C$ with $v_0 \in \bar{e}$,
and let $r$ be a region of $H$ incident with $e$ such that $\bar{r} \cap Z \ne \emptyset$. Choose
$v \in \bar{r} \cap Z$. Then $v_0 \in \bar{e} \subseteq \bar{r}$, and so
$$v, r, v_0, r_1, v_1, \dots, r_{k'}, v_{k'}$$
is a chain of length $k' + 1$ between $Z$ and $\text{bd}(\Delta)$. Hence $k' + 1 \ge k$. This proves
(1).

We prove (5.5) by induction on $k$. If $k = 1$ there is nothing to prove, and so we assume that
$k \ge 2$. Let $C$ and $\Lambda$ be as in (1). Then $\bar{\Lambda}$ is a closed disc, and so it is
non-empty and arc-wise connected. Moreover $\bar{\Lambda} \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$, and
the regional distance between $\bar{\Lambda}$ and $\text{bd}(\Delta)$ is $\ge k - 1 \ge 1$. From our
inductive hypothesis, applied with $\bar{\Lambda}$ in place of $Z$, there are $k - 2$ mutually
disjoint circuits $C_2, \dots, C_{k-1}$ of $H$ with
$U(C_2), \dots, U(C_{k-1}) \subseteq \Delta - \text{bd}(\Delta)$, bounding open discs
$\Lambda_2, \dots, \Lambda_{k-1}$ that include $\bar{\Lambda}$, and hence include $Z$. For
$2 \le i \le k - 1$, $U(C) \subseteq \bar{\Lambda} \subseteq \Lambda_i$ and
$U(C_i) \cap \Lambda_i = \emptyset$, and so $U(C) \cap U(C_i) = \emptyset$. Setting $C_1 = C$, the
circuits $C_1, \dots, C_{k-1}$ satisfy (5.5). $\square$

## 7. Proof of (6.5), page 35: vertices drawn on $bd(\Delta)$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof takes the union of the drawings of $A \cap H$ in the discs
$\alpha(A, \Omega)$ to get a drawing $H'$ in $\Delta$ with
$V(H') \cap bd(\Delta) = V(H) \cap bd(\Delta)$ and an isomorphism $\phi \colon H \to H'$ such that
(i) $\alpha(v) = \phi(v)$ for all $v \in V(H) \cap V(\mathscr{S})$. But the definition of an
arrangement (page 30) allows $\alpha(v) \in bd(\Delta)$ for a vertex
$v \in V(H) \cap V(\mathscr{S})$ that is not in $bd(\Delta)$. Then (i) puts $\phi(v)$ in
$bd(\Delta)$, so $H'$ has a vertex on $bd(\Delta)$ that $H$ does not have there, contrary to
$V(H') \cap bd(\Delta) = V(H) \cap bd(\Delta)$.

**Correction.** Before taking the union, move the discs $\alpha(A, \Omega)$ away from $bd(\Delta)$
at every vertex of $V(\mathscr{S})$ not in $bd(\Delta)$, and change $\alpha$ to match. The
hypothesis $\alpha(v) = v$ for $v \in V(H) \cap bd(\Delta)$ is kept, and now only these vertices are
mapped into $bd(\Delta)$, so the union of the drawings has the stated properties. The conclusion of
(6.5) asks only for some arrangement $\alpha'$ compatible with $H$, so (6.5) holds as printed.

## 8. Proof of (7.3), page 36: the step $1 \ge d(z, v)$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** In the case $z = f$, where $f$ is an edge of $H$ with an end $v$, the proof
writes "$d(z, g) \ge 1 \ge d(z, v) \ge \theta - |V(A \cap B)|$". The step $1 \ge d(z, v)$ is false
in general. Let $K$ be a radial drawing of $H$. The atom of $f$ is a region of $K$ bounded by a
circuit of length $4$, and a closed walk of $K$ of length $2$ has inside only its own edge. So when
$\theta \ge 2$, no closed walk of length less than $4$ has both the atom of $f$ and $v$ inside, so
$d(f, v) \ge 2$.

**Correction.** Replace the chain by $d(z, g) \ge d(z, v) \ge \theta - |V(A \cap B)|$. The first
inequality holds because $v$ is in the closure of the atom of $z$, and the inside $ins(W)$ of a
closed walk $W$ is closed. So every closed walk whose inside contains the atoms of $z$ and $g$ also
has $v$ inside, and $d(z, v) \le d(z, g)$. With this step, (7.3) holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM14.tangleMetric_edge_vertex_le` in `GraphMinor/GM14/Section7a.lean`, and
`GraphMinor.GM14.theorem_7_3` in `GraphMinor/GM14/Results.lean`.

of $\mathscr{P}$ has an internal vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$.*

This gives (8.3), applied to the neighbourhood $(G_1, \Omega_1, \Omega_2)$, the society
$(G_2, \Omega_2)$, and the set $X'$ of initial vertices of the four given paths. Indeed
$X' \subseteq X$ and $|X'| = 4$, since the paths are disjoint, and $X'$ is linked, by the four
paths. Now (1) is the case $p = 2$ of [3, (10.4)], with breadth $\ge 3$ in place of $\ge p + 5$ and
$|X| \ge 4$ in place of $|X| \ge 2p + 8$. The proof of [3, (10.4)] uses [3, (10.3)], whose proof
uses [3, (5.2)], and only [3, (5.2)] needs $p + 4$ separating paths. We show first that for crosses
two separating paths are enough, and then we follow the proofs of [3, (10.3)] and [3, (10.4)].

We use the following fact several times. Let $(H_1, H_2)$ be a separation of a graph, and let $R$ be
a connected subgraph with a vertex in $V(H_1)$ and no vertex in $V(H_1 \cap H_2)$. Then
$R \subseteq H_1$. For an edge of $R$ with an end in $V(H_1) - V(H_2)$ is not an edge of $H_2$, and
so it is an edge of $H_1$, and its other end is in $V(H_1)$ and hence in $V(H_1) - V(H_2)$; since
$R$ is connected, every edge and every vertex of $R$ is in $H_1$.

(2) *Let $(G, \Omega)$ be a society with a cross. Let $\{Q_1, Q_2\}$ be a transaction in
$(G, \Omega)$ such that $Q_1, Q_2$ are separating and $Q_i$ has ends $a_i, b_i$ $(i = 1, 2)$, where
$(a_2, a_1, b_1, b_2)$ is clockwise. Let $(H_1, H_2)$ be a separation of $G$ with
$V(H_1 \cap H_2) = V(Q_2)$, $Q_2 \subseteq H_1$, $\Omega(a_2, b_2) \subseteq V(H_1)$ and
$\Omega(b_2, a_2) \subseteq V(H_2)$. Then either there is a cross $\{P, P'\}$ of $(G, \Omega)$ with
$P, P' \subseteq H_1$, or there is a cross $\mathscr{P}$ of $(G, \Omega)$ with
$\Omega(a_1, b_1) \cap V(\mathscr{P}) = \emptyset$.*

Since $(a_2, a_1, b_1, b_2)$ is clockwise, the vertices $a_2$ and $b_2$ are in $\Omega(b_1, a_1)$,
and $\Omega(a_1, b_1) \subseteq \Omega(a_2, b_2)$. Suppose first that $Q_1, Q_2$ have no internal
vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$. A cross is a crooked transaction of cardinality $2$, and so we may choose
$\mathscr{P}$ and a fence for it such that the statement **S** of [3, Section 4] holds with
$\mathscr{Q} = \{Q_1, Q_2\}$ and $p = 2$. Then $|\mathscr{P}| = 2$. For otherwise, by [3, (3.4)],
some subset of $\mathscr{P}$ with two members is a crooked transaction; its union with
$\bigcup \mathscr{Q}$ is included in $\bigcup \mathscr{P} \cup \bigcup \mathscr{Q}$, and so it is
equal to it by (i) of **S**, and then (ii) of **S** is false. Hence $\mathscr{P}$ is a
cross, and $|V(\mathscr{P})| = 4$. If $V(\mathscr{P}) \cap \Omega(a_1, b_1) = \emptyset$ the second
outcome of (2) holds, and so we may assume that there is a vertex
$x_0 \in V(\mathscr{P}) \cap \Omega(a_1, b_1)$.

(3) *For $j = 1, 2$, if $P$ is an $\Omega$-path with both ends in $\Omega(a_j, b_j)$ and no vertex
in $V(Q_j)$, then $P \subseteq H_1$.*

The ends of $P$ are in $\Omega(a_2, b_2) \subseteq V(H_1)$. We claim that $P$ has no vertex in
$V(Q_2)$. For $j = 2$ this is a hypothesis. Let $j = 1$, and suppose that $P$ meets $V(Q_2)$. Let
$R_1$ be a minimal subpath of $P$ between an end $u$ of $P$ and a vertex $w \in V(Q_2)$, and let
$R_2$ be the subpath of $Q_2$ between $w$ and $a_2$. Then $R_1 \cup R_2$ is a path between $u$ and
$a_2$, since $R_1$ meets $V(Q_2)$ only in $w$. It has no vertex in $V(Q_1)$, since $P$ has none and
$Q_1, Q_2$ are disjoint. But $u \in \Omega(a_1, b_1)$ and $a_2 \in \Omega(b_1, a_1)$, and so
$R_1 \cup R_2$ is an $\Omega$-path that crosses $Q_1$, which is impossible since $Q_1$ is
separating. This proves our claim. Since $V(H_1 \cap H_2) = V(Q_2)$, the fact above shows that
$P \subseteq H_1$. This proves (3).

Suppose that $Q_j \cap \bigcup \mathscr{P}$ is null for some $j \in \{1, 2\}$. Since $Q_j$ is
separating and $x_0 \in \Omega(a_1, b_1) \subseteq \Omega(a_j, b_j)$, [3, (5.1)] shows that
$\mathscr{P}$ has a subset which is a cross with all four ends in $\Omega(a_j, b_j)$. This subset is
$\mathscr{P}$ itself, and by (3) both members of $\mathscr{P}$ are subgraphs of $H_1$, so the first
outcome of (2) holds. We may therefore assume that $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ both meet $\bigcup \mathscr{P}$.
Moreover $\mathscr{P}$ has no overpass, since removing a member of $\mathscr{P}$ leaves a
transaction with one member, which does not have two peripheral members. So by [3, (4.3)], each of
$Q_1, Q_2$ either has both ends in $V(\mathscr{P})$ or is half-covered.

(4) *If $Q_j$ is half-covered, where $j \in \{1, 2\}$, then $\mathscr{P} = \{P, P'\}$, where $P$ and
$Q_j$ have a common end $s$, $P \cap Q_j$ is a path between $s$ and some vertex $c$, $P'$ has no
vertex in $V(Q_j)$, and the three vertices in $V(\mathscr{P}) - \{s\}$ are either all in
$\Omega(a_j, b_j)$ or all in $\Omega(b_j, a_j)$.*

For let $P$ be the member of $\mathscr{P}$ that meets $Q_j$, as in [3, (4.3)(iii)], let $s$ be the
common end of $P$, $Q_j$ and $P \cap Q_j$, and let $P'$ be the other member of $\mathscr{P}$. Then
$P'$ has no vertex in $V(Q_j)$, since $Q_j$ meets only one member of $\mathscr{P}$. Since $Q_j$ is
separating, $P'$ does not cross $Q_j$, and so both ends of $P'$ are in $\Omega(a_j, b_j)$ or both
are in $\Omega(b_j, a_j)$. We assume the first; the second case is the same with the two sides
exchanged. Let the ends of $P'$ be $u, w$, where $(a_j, u, w, b_j)$ is clockwise, and let $t$ be the
end of $P$ other than $s$. Since $\mathscr{P}$ is crooked, $P'$ is not peripheral, and so
$\Omega(u, w)$ contains $s$ or $t$. It does not contain $s$, since $s \in \{a_j, b_j\}$. Hence
$t \in \Omega(u, w) \subseteq \Omega(a_j, b_j)$, and so $u, w, t$ are all in $\Omega(a_j, b_j)$.
This proves (4).

Since $Q_2$ meets $\bigcup \mathscr{P}$, it has an end in $V(\mathscr{P})$, by (4) if $Q_2$ is
half-covered. This end is in $\Omega(b_1, a_1)$, while $x_0 \in \Omega(a_1, b_1)$. So for
$s \in \{a_1, b_1\}$ the set $V(\mathscr{P}) - \{s\}$ meets both $\Omega(a_1, b_1)$ and
$\Omega(b_1, a_1)$, and hence $Q_1$ is not half-covered, by (4). Thus $a_1, b_1 \in V(\mathscr{P})$.
If also $a_2, b_2 \in V(\mathscr{P})$, then $x_0, a_1, b_1, a_2, b_2$ are five distinct members of
$V(\mathscr{P})$, which is impossible. Hence $Q_2$ is half-covered. Let $P, P', s, c$ be as in (4)
with $j = 2$, and let $t$ be the end of $P$ other than $s$. Since $x_0 \in \Omega(a_2, b_2)$ and
$x_0 \ne s$, the three vertices in $V(\mathscr{P}) - \{s\}$ are in $\Omega(a_2, b_2)$, by (4). In
particular $P' \subseteq H_1$, by (3). Since $P \cap Q_2$ is the subpath of $P$ between $s$ and $c$,
the subpath $R$ of $P$ between $c$ and $t$ meets $V(Q_2)$ only in $c$. Now $t \ne c$, since
$t \in \Omega(a_2, b_2)$, while the only vertices of $Q_2$ in $\bar{\Omega}$ are $a_2$ and $b_2$. So
$R \setminus c$ is connected, has no vertex in $V(Q_2) = V(H_1 \cap H_2)$, and contains
$t \in V(H_1)$, and by the fact above $R \setminus c \subseteq H_1$. The edge of $R$ at $c$ is then
in $H_1$, since its other end is in $V(H_1) - V(H_2)$, and $P \cap Q_2 \subseteq Q_2 \subseteq H_1$.
Hence $P \subseteq H_1$, and the first outcome of (2) holds.

Now we drop the assumption that $Q_1, Q_2$ have no internal vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$. Let $G'$ be
obtained from $G$ by adding, for each $v \in \bar{\Omega}$, a new vertex $v'$ and a new edge $vv'$,
and let $\Omega'$ be the cyclic permutation with $\bar{\Omega}' = \{v' : v \in \bar{\Omega}\}$ that
maps $v'$ to $w'$ whenever $\Omega$ maps $v$ to $w$. For an $\Omega$-path $P$ with ends $u, w$, let
$P'$ be $P$ together with the edges $uu'$ and $ww'$. Then $P'$ is an $\Omega'$-path with no internal
vertex in $\bar{\Omega}'$, and every $\Omega'$-path is $P'$ for exactly one $\Omega$-path $P$, since
the new vertices have valency one. Two $\Omega$-paths are disjoint if and only if the corresponding
$\Omega'$-paths are, and $\{u, w\}$ crosses $\{u_1, w_1\}$ in $\Omega$ if and only if $\{u', w'\}$
crosses $\{u_1', w_1'\}$ in $\Omega'$. Hence $P \mapsto P'$ and its inverse map crosses to crosses,
transactions to transactions, and separating paths to separating paths. Let $H_1'$ be obtained from
$H_1$ by adding the new edges at the vertices in $\Omega(a_2, b_2) \cup \{a_2, b_2\}$, with their
ends, and let $H_2'$ be obtained from $H_2$ by adding the other new edges, with their ends, and the
vertices $a_2', b_2'$. Then $(H_1', H_2')$ is a separation of $G'$ with
$V(H_1' \cap H_2') = V(H_1 \cap H_2) \cup \{a_2', b_2'\} = V(Q_2')$ and $Q_2' \subseteq H_1'$, and
$\Omega'(a_2', b_2') \subseteq V(H_1')$ and $\Omega'(b_2', a_2') \subseteq V(H_2')$. Since
$(G', \Omega')$ has a cross, and $\{Q_1', Q_2'\}$ is a transaction of separating paths with no
internal vertex in $\bar{\Omega}'$, the case already proved applies to $(G', \Omega')$,
$\{Q_1', Q_2'\}$ and $(H_1', H_2')$. If it gives a cross of $(G', \Omega')$ whose members are
subgraphs of $H_1'$, then the corresponding cross of $(G, \Omega)$ has members that are subgraphs of
$H_1$, since $H_1'$ with the new vertices deleted is $H_1$. If it gives a cross $\mathscr{P}'$ with
$\Omega'(a_1', b_1') \cap V(\mathscr{P}') = \emptyset$, the corresponding cross $\mathscr{P}$ of
$(G, \Omega)$ has $\Omega(a_1, b_1) \cap V(\mathscr{P}) = \emptyset$. This proves (2).

Using (2) in place of [3, (5.2)], we follow the proof of [3, (10.3)] with two separating paths.

(5) *Let $(G, \Omega, \Omega_0)$ be a rural neighbourhood of breadth $\ge 2$, let
$X \subseteq \bar{\Omega}$ be weakly-linked with $|X| \ge 4$, and let $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$ be the
composition of a society $(G_0, \Omega_0)$ with $(G, \Omega, \Omega_0)$. If $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$
has a cross, then it has a cross $\mathscr{P}$ with $V(\mathscr{P}) \subseteq X$.*

We show, by induction on $|Y - X|$, that for every $Y$ with $X \subseteq Y \subseteq \bar{\Omega}$,
if $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$ has a cross with all ends in $Y$ then it has one with all ends in $X$. The
case $Y = \bar{\Omega}$ is (5). If $Y = X$ there is nothing to prove, and so we assume that there
exists $x \in Y - X$. By the inductive hypothesis, applied to $Y - \{x\}$, it suffices to find a
cross with all ends in $Y - \{x\}$. List the members of $X$ in clockwise order, starting just after
$x$. Since $|X| \ge 4$, we may take $b_1, b_2$ to be the first two and $a_1, a_2$ to be the last
two, and then $(a_2, a_1, x, b_1, b_2)$ is clockwise. The set $\{a_1, a_2, b_1, b_2\}$ is
weakly-linked, since it is a subset of $X$, and so by [3, (10.2)] with $s = 2$ there is a
transaction $\{Q_1, Q_2\}$ in $(G, \Omega)$ such that $Q_i$ has ends $a_i, b_i$ and meets every path
of $G$ from $\Omega(a_i, b_i)$ to $\Omega(b_i, a_i) \cup \bar{\Omega}_0$ $(i = 1, 2)$.

Let $i \in \{1, 2\}$. Let $J$ be the union of the components of $G \setminus V(Q_i)$ that contain a
vertex in $\Omega(a_i, b_i)$. Let $K_1$ consist of $J$, $Q_i$, and the edges of $G$ between $V(J)$
and $V(Q_i)$, and let $K_2$ consist of the vertices of $G$ not in $V(J)$ and the edges of $G$ not in
$K_1$. An edge of $G$ with an end in $V(J)$ has its other end in $V(J) \cup V(Q_i)$, and so it is in
$K_1$. Hence $(K_1, K_2)$ is a separation of $G$ with $V(K_1 \cap K_2) = V(Q_i)$,
$Q_i \subseteq K_1$ and $\Omega(a_i, b_i) \subseteq V(K_1)$. Moreover
$\Omega(b_i, a_i) \cup \bar{\Omega}_0 \subseteq V(K_2)$, since a vertex of
$\Omega(b_i, a_i) \cup \bar{\Omega}_0$ in $V(J)$ would be joined to $\Omega(a_i, b_i)$ by a path of
$G$ with no vertex in $V(Q_i)$. Since
$V(K_1) \cap V(G_0) \subseteq \bar{\Omega}_0 \subseteq V(K_2)$, and $G_0$ has no edge in $G$, it
follows that $(K_1, K_2 \cup G_0)$ is a separation of $G \cup G_0$ with
$V(K_1 \cap (K_2 \cup G_0)) = V(Q_i)$. We call it the separation for $Q_i$.

Let $\Omega_Y$ be the cyclic permutation with $\bar{\Omega}_Y = Y$ that orders $Y$ as $\Omega$ does.
The crosses of $(G \cup G_0, \Omega_Y)$ are the crosses of $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$ with all ends in
$Y$. Also $\{Q_1, Q_2\}$ is a transaction in $(G \cup G_0, \Omega_Y)$, since its ends are in
$X \subseteq Y$, and $(a_2, a_1, b_1, b_2)$ is clockwise in $\Omega_Y$. For $i = 1, 2$ we have
$\Omega_Y(a_i, b_i) \subseteq \Omega(a_i, b_i)$ and $\Omega_Y(b_i, a_i) \subseteq \Omega(b_i, a_i)$,
and $Q_i$ is separating in $(G \cup G_0, \Omega_Y)$. For suppose that an $\Omega_Y$-path $R$ crosses
$Q_i$, and let $(K_1, K_2 \cup G_0)$ be the separation for $Q_i$. Then $R$ has no vertex in
$V(Q_i)$. It has one end in $\Omega(a_i, b_i) \subseteq V(K_1)$, and so $R \subseteq K_1$ by the
fact above. But its other end is in $\Omega(b_i, a_i) - V(Q_i)$, which is disjoint from $V(K_1)$, a
contradiction.

Now we apply (2) to $(G \cup G_0, \Omega_Y)$ and $\{Q_1, Q_2\}$, with $(H_1, H_2)$ the separation
$(K_1, K_2 \cup G_0)$ for $Q_2$. Suppose first that (2) gives a cross $\{P, P'\}$ with
$P, P' \subseteq K_1$. Then $\{P, P'\}$ is a cross of $(G, \Omega)$, since $K_1 \subseteq G$ and its
ends are in $Y \subseteq \bar{\Omega}$. But $(G, \Omega)$ has no cross, since it is rural, as in the
proof of [3, (10.3)], a contradiction. Hence (2) gives a cross $\mathscr{P}$ of
$(G \cup G_0, \Omega_Y)$ with no end in $\Omega_Y(a_1, b_1)$. Its ends are in $Y$, and $x$ is not
one of them since $x \in \Omega_Y(a_1, b_1)$. So $\mathscr{P}$ is a cross of $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$
with all ends in $Y - \{x\}$. This proves (5).

Now we prove (1), following the proof of [3, (10.4)] with a $3$-nest in place of the $(p + 5)$-nest
and (5) in place of [3, (10.3)]. Take a presentation $(\Sigma, \Gamma, \Delta, \Delta_0)$ of
$(G, \Omega, \Omega_0)$ with a $3$-nest $(C_1', C_2', C_3')$, and let $\Delta_j'$ be the closed disc
bounded by the drawing of $C_j'$ $(j = 1, 2, 3)$. The drawing of $C_2'$ is disjoint from that of
$C_3'$, and so $\Delta_2'$ lies in the interior of $\Delta_3'$ and contains no point of
$bd(\Delta)$. Hence no vertex of $C_1'$ or $C_2'$ is in $\bar{\Omega}$, and $\bar{\Omega}_0$, which
is drawn in $\Delta_0 \subseteq \Delta_1'$, is disjoint from $\bar{\Omega}$. Let $n = |X|$. Since
$X$ is linked, there are mutually disjoint $X \to \bar{\Omega}_0$ paths $P_1', \dots, P_n'$ of $G$,
each with only its initial vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$. Let $G'$ be obtained from $G$ by deleting every
edge of $G$ with an end in $\bar{\Omega}$ except the first edges of $P_1', \dots, P_n'$. Then
$C_1', C_2'$ and $P_1', \dots, P_n'$ are subgraphs of $G'$, and
$(\Sigma, \Gamma', \Delta, \Delta_0)$ is a presentation of $(G', \Omega, \Omega_0)$ with the
$2$-nest $(C_1', C_2')$, where $\Gamma'$ is the drawing of $G'$ given by $\Gamma$. By [3, (10.1)]
applied to $(G', \Omega, \Omega_0)$, there is a $2$-nest $(C_1, C_2)$ and there are disjoint
$X \to \bar{\Omega}_0$ paths $P_1, \dots, P_n$ of $G'$, such that $P_i \cap C_j$ is a path for
$1 \le i \le n$ and $j = 1, 2$. Each vertex $v \in \bar{\Omega}$ has valency at most $1$ in $G'$.
For the edges of $G'$ at $v$ are first edges of the paths $P_k'$, the second vertex of each $P_k'$
is an internal vertex or is in $\bar{\Omega}_0$, and so it is not in $\bar{\Omega}$, and only one of
these paths starts at $v$. Hence no vertex of $C_1$ or $C_2$ is in $\bar{\Omega}$, since circuits
have no vertex of valency $1$. For the same reason no internal vertex of $P_i$ is in $\bar{\Omega}$,
and so the only vertex of $P_i$ in $\bar{\Omega}$ is its initial vertex $x_i$, since its other end
is in $\bar{\Omega}_0$.

Let $\Delta_1$ be the closed disc bounded by the drawing of $C_2$. As before, $\Delta_0$ lies in the
interior of $\Delta_1$. No vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$ is drawn in $\Delta_1$. For such a vertex is
drawn in $bd(\Delta)$, and it is not drawn in the drawing of $C_2$, since it is not a vertex of
$C_2$, and so it would be in the interior of $\Delta_1$, which is included in the interior of
$\Delta$. The drawing of $P_i$ runs from $x_i$, which is not in $\Delta_1$, to its end in
$\bar{\Omega}_0$, which is in $\Delta_1$, and so it meets the drawing of $C_2$. Since an edge not in
$C_2$ meets the drawing of $C_2$ only in its ends, $P_i$ has a vertex in $V(C_2)$; let $y_i$ be the
first one, and put $Y = \{y_1, \dots, y_n\}$. Let $G_1$ be the subgraph of $G$ drawn in $\Delta_1$,
and let $H_2$ be the subgraph of $G$ drawn in the closure of $\Delta - \Delta_1$, without the edges
of $C_2$. An edge of $G$ not in $C_2$ is drawn in $\Delta_1$ or in the closure of
$\Delta - \Delta_1$, and the vertices drawn in $bd(\Delta_1)$ are those of $C_2$. So $(G_1, H_2)$ is
a separation of $G$ with $V(G_1 \cap H_2) = V(C_2)$. Since $V(G_0) \cap V(G) = \bar{\Omega}_0$,
which is drawn in the interior of $\Delta_1$, it follows that $(G_1 \cup G_0, H_2)$ is a separation
of $G \cup G_0$ with $V((G_1 \cup G_0) \cap H_2) = V(C_2)$, and $\bar{\Omega} \subseteq V(H_2)$. Let
$\Omega_1$ be the cyclic permutation with $\bar{\Omega}_1 = V(C_2)$ given by the clockwise
orientation of $bd(\Delta_1)$. Then $(G_1, \Omega_1, \Omega_0)$ and $(H_2, \Omega, \Omega_1)$ are
rural neighbourhoods, the first with the $2$-nest $(C_1, C_2)$, and $(G \cup G_0, \Omega)$ is the
composition of $(G_1 \cup G_0, \Omega_1)$ with $(H_2, \Omega, \Omega_1)$. By [3, (9.1)] and [3,
(3.4)], $(G_1 \cup G_0, \Omega_1)$ has a cross.

The set $Y$ is weakly-linked in $(G_1, \Omega_1, \Omega_0)$, and $|Y| = n \ge 4$. For let
$1 \le i \le n$. The path $P_i \cap C_2$ contains $y_i$, and it is a subpath of $P_i$, and so $y_i$
is one of its ends, since $y_i$ is the first vertex of $P_i$ in $V(C_2)$. Let $z_i$ be its other
end, and let $T_i$ be the subpath of $P_i$ from $z_i$ to the end $t_i$ of $P_i$ in $\bar{\Omega}_0$.
Then $T_i$ meets $V(C_2)$ only in $z_i$, and $t_i \ne z_i$, since $t_i$ is drawn in the interior of
$\Delta_1$. So $T_i \setminus z_i$ is a connected subgraph of $G$ with no vertex in $V(C_2)$ and
with $t_i \in V(G_1)$, and it is a subgraph of $G_1$ by the fact above, applied to $(G_1, H_2)$. The
edge of $T_i$ at $z_i$ is in $G_1$ as well, since its other end is in $V(G_1) - V(C_2)$. Hence the
subpath of $P_i$ from $y_i$ to $t_i$, which is the union of $P_i \cap C_2$ and $T_i$, is a path of
$G_1$. These paths are disjoint, since $P_1, \dots, P_n$ are, and so $Y$ is weakly-linked.

By (5), applied to $(G_1, \Omega_1, \Omega_0)$, $Y$ and $(G_1 \cup G_0, \Omega_1)$, there is a cross
$\{P, P'\}$ of $(G_1 \cup G_0, \Omega_1)$ with all ends in $Y$. Let $y_{i_1}, y_{i_2}$ be the ends
of $P$ and $y_{i_3}, y_{i_4}$ those of $P'$, and for $m = 1, \dots, 4$ let $R_m$ be the subpath of
$P_{i_m}$ between $x_{i_m}$ and $y_{i_m}$. Then $R_m$ meets $V(C_2)$ only in $y_{i_m}$, by the
choice of $y_{i_m}$, and so its drawing meets the drawing of $C_2$ only in $y_{i_m}$. Since
$x_{i_m}$ is not drawn in $\Delta_1$, the rest of $R_m$ is drawn outside $\Delta_1$. So $R_m$ is a
path of $H_2$ from $\bar{\Omega}$ to $\bar{\Omega}_1$, and it meets $G_1 \cup G_0$ only in
$y_{i_m}$, since $V(G_0) \cap V(G) = \bar{\Omega}_0 \subseteq V(G_1)$. Let
$P^+ = P \cup R_1 \cup R_2$ and $P'^+ = P' \cup R_3 \cup R_4$. These are disjoint paths of
$G \cup G_0$, with ends $x_{i_1}, x_{i_2}$ and $x_{i_3}, x_{i_4}$ in $X$, since $P, P'$ are disjoint
and $R_1, \dots, R_4$ are disjoint. No internal vertex of $P^+$ or $P'^+$ is in $\bar{\Omega}$,
since no vertex of $G_1 \cup G_0$ is in $\bar{\Omega}$ and $x_{i_m}$ is the only vertex of $R_m$ in
$\bar{\Omega}$. The paths $R_1, \dots, R_4$ are mutually disjoint, and they are drawn in the closed
annulus which is the closure of $\Delta - \Delta_1$, each from one of its boundary circles to the
other. So the ends $x_{i_1}, \dots, x_{i_4}$ occur around $bd(\Delta)$ in the same cyclic order as
the ends $y_{i_1}, \dots, y_{i_4}$ occur around $bd(\Delta_1)$. Since $\{y_{i_1}, y_{i_2}\}$ crosses
$\{y_{i_3}, y_{i_4}\}$ in $\Omega_1$, it follows that $\{x_{i_1}, x_{i_2}\}$ crosses
$\{x_{i_3}, x_{i_4}\}$ in $\Omega$. Thus $\{P^+, P'^+\}$ is a cross as required in (1). This proves
(1), and hence (8.3).

## 10. Claim (1) in the proof of (8.4), page 41: replacing the path $P$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof of Claim (1) says "it is easy to check that, from the assumptions
about $A'$ in (1), there is a path $Q'$ of $A'$ with the same ends as $Q$ and with
$\bar{\Omega} \cap V(Q') \subseteq \bar{\Omega} \cap V(Q)$". The assumption gives a path of $A'$
only between two vertices of $\bar{\Omega}$ joined by a path of $A$ with no internal vertex in
$\bar{\Omega}$, while $Q$ may pass through the third vertex of $\bar{\Omega}$. So paths must be
combined, and the result must still be disjoint from the other members of $\mathscr{P}$.

**Proof.** We give the details, replacing $P$ as a whole. Let $B$ be the subgraph of $G$ such that
$(A, B)$ is a separation of $G$ with $V(A \cap B) = \bar{\Omega}$. Then
$V(\mathscr{P}) \subseteq V(B)$, since $V(A) \cap V(\mathscr{S}) = \bar{\Omega}$. We claim that for
every path $R$ of $G$ with both ends in $V(B)$, there is a path $R'$ of $A' \cup B$ with the same
ends such that $V(R') \cap V(B) \subseteq V(R) \cap V(B)$. We prove this by induction on $|V(R)|$.
If $R \subseteq B$ we take $R' = R$. If some internal vertex $w$ of $R$ is in $\bar{\Omega}$, we
apply induction to the two subpaths of $R$ between its ends and $w$, and take for $R'$ a path
between the ends of $R$ in the union of the two paths obtained. Otherwise $R \not\subseteq B$ and no
internal vertex of $R$ is in $V(A \cap B)$, and so $R \subseteq A$ and both ends of $R$ are in
$\bar{\Omega}$. By the assumption about $A'$ in (1), there is a path $R'$ of $A'$ with the same ends
as $R$ and no internal vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$, and then
$V(R') \cap V(B) \subseteq V(R) \cap V(B)$, as required. This proves our claim. Now every member of
$\mathscr{P}$ that is not a subgraph of $B$ has at least two vertices in $\bar{\Omega}$, and so $P$
is the only such member, since $|\bar{\Omega}| \le 3$. Let $P'$ be a path of
$A' \cup B \subseteq G'$ obtained from $P$ by our claim. Then $P'$ is disjoint from the other
members of $\mathscr{P}$, since they are subgraphs of $B$ and $V(P') \cap V(B) \subseteq V(P)$.
Hence replacing $P$ by $P'$ gives a crooked transaction in $(G', \Omega_1)$ with the same ends.

## 11. Claim (2) in the proof of (8.4), page 42: the edge added to the forest $B$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** Here $H \cap A$ is a forest, $B$ is a maximal forest of $A$ with
$H \cap A \subseteq B$, and $u, v \in \bar{\Omega}$ are joined by a path of $A$ with no internal
vertex in $\bar{\Omega}$, but not by such a path of $B$. The path $P$ of $B$ between $u$ and $v$ has
an internal vertex $w$, and $\bar{\Omega} = \{u, v, w\}$. The proof splits $B$ at $w$ into $B_1$ and
$B_2$, and says: "Since there is a path of $A$ between $u$ and $v$ in $B \setminus w$, there is an
edge $e$ of $A$ with one end in $B_1 \setminus w$ and the other in $B_2 \setminus w$." First, "in
$B \setminus w$" is a misprint for "in $A \setminus w$". Second, $B \setminus w$ may have more than
two components. If $w$ has three neighbours in $B$, with components $T_u, T_v, T_3$ of
$B \setminus w$ and $B_2 = T_v \cup T_3$, the edge $e$ may join $T_u$ to $T_3$. Then $B + e$ does
not join $u$ and $v$ by a path that avoids $w$, and Claim (1) cannot be applied to $B + e$.

**Correction.** Read "in $A \setminus w$", and choose $B$ with more care: among the maximal forests
of $A$ that contain $H \cap A$ and $P$, take one in which the component $T_u$ of $B \setminus w$
that contains $u$ is as large as possible. Suppose an edge $g$ of $A$ joins $T_u$ to a component $T$
of $B \setminus w$ that contains neither $u$ nor $v$. Then $T$ has no vertex of $\bar{\Omega}$. The
forest $B$ has exactly one edge between $T$ and $w$: at least one, since otherwise adding $g$ to $B$
would give a larger forest, and at most one, since $B$ has no circuit. This edge is not in $H$.
Otherwise the edges of $H \cap A$ inside $T$ or between $T$ and $w$ form a forest with at least one
edge, which has a leaf in $T$. That vertex is a leaf of $H \cap A$ outside $\bar{\Omega}$, while the
rigidity of $H$ puts every leaf of $H \cap A$ in $\bar{\Omega}$. So removing this edge and adding
$g$ gives a forest of the same kind with a larger component of $u$, which is impossible. Hence every
edge of $A$ that leaves $T_u$, other than at $w$, ends in the component $T_v$ of $v$. A path of
$A \setminus w$ from $u$ to $v$ leaves $T_u$, so some edge $e$ of $A$ joins $T_u$ to $T_v$. Then
$B + e$ joins $u$ and $v$ by a path that avoids $w$, and the rest of the subproof applies. Claim (2)
holds as printed.

## 13. Proof of (9.3), page 45: the maximality of $\Lambda$

$r$ of $H$ with $r \cap \Lambda = \emptyset$, distinct $v_1, v_2 \in V(C)$, and a line $F_2$ with

$U(P_i) \subseteq \bar{r}$. Then $r \subseteq \Lambda_2$ as above, and so $F_2 \subseteq \Lambda_2$.
For $i = 1, 2$, let $D_i \subseteq \bar{\Lambda}_2$ be the closed disc bounded by $F_2 \cup U(P_i)$.
Now $F_2 \not\subseteq \bar{\Lambda}$, since $\bar{r} \cap \Lambda = \emptyset$ and
$r \cap U(C) = \emptyset$. Suppose that $U(P_1) \not\subseteq D_2$ and $U(P_2) \not\subseteq D_1$.
Since also $F_2 \not\subseteq \bar{\Lambda}$, the closed discs $\bar{\Lambda}$ and $D_2$ meet only
in $U(P_2)$, and their union is a closed disc in $\bar{\Lambda}_2$ bounded by $F_2 \cup U(P_1)$. So

that $U(P_1) \subseteq D_2$. Then $U(C) \subseteq D_2$, and so $\bar{\Lambda} \subseteq D_2$, since

# Graph Minors XV: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XV. Giant steps*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B **68**
(1996), 112–148.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM15/GM15.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM15/GM15.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Proof of (1.1), page 116: the definition of $\theta_{\kappa+1}$ and $\lambda_{\kappa+1}$

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** The proof says "Define $\theta_{k+1} = \max(\phi, \mu + 16)$ and
$\lambda_{k+1} = 4$". No $k$ is bound at this point.

defines $\theta_k, \lambda_k, \rho_k$ for $k = \kappa, \kappa - 1, \dots, 0$, starting from these

## 3. Proof of (2.1), page 116: the case $\bar{\Omega} = \emptyset$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** For $(C, \Omega) \in \mathscr{S}$, the proof applies Graph Minors IX, (8.1), to
get a linear decomposition of $(C, \Omega)$ with $\bar{\Omega} = \{t_1, \dots, t_n\}$. If
$\bar{\Omega} = \emptyset$ and $C$ is not null, there is no such decomposition, since the union of
the empty family $X_1, \dots, X_n$ is not $C$. See the correction to (8.1) in [GM09.md](GM09.md).

**Correction.** Treat $\bar{\Omega} = \emptyset$ first. The proof supposes, for a contradiction,
that $(A, B) \in \mathscr{T}$ and $B \subseteq C$. If $\bar{\Omega} = \emptyset$, then $C$ shares no
vertex with the other members of $\mathscr{S}$, so $G$ has a separation $(D, C)$ of order $0$. Since
$0 < ord(\mathscr{T})$, one of $(D, C)$ and $(C, D)$ is in $\mathscr{T}$. The first is excluded by
the hypothesis of (2.1), since $0 \le 2\rho + 1$. The second is excluded by the tangle axioms, since
$A \cup C \supseteq A \cup B = G$. So we may assume that $\bar{\Omega} \ne \emptyset$, and then IX
(8.1) applies. (2.1) holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM15.theorem_2_1` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Results.lean`, with the form of IX (8.1)
that it uses, `GraphMinor.GM15.GM09Theorem81Statement` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Cited.lean`.

## 6. Statement of (3.4), page 122: the paths $P_i$

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** (3.4) takes $H' = H \cup P_1 \cup \dots \cup P_{k-1}$ with
$H' \cap (\Sigma - \Lambda) = H$, where $P_i$ is a path with ends $v_i, v_{2k-i}$, and concludes
among other things that $d'(r_1, r_2) \ge 2k - 6$, where $r_1, r_2 \subseteq \Lambda$ are the
regions of $H'$ incident with $v_k, v_{2k}$. It does not say that the paths are disjoint or that
$P_i$ meets $H$ only in its ends, and the proof uses both: "$H' \cap \Sigma_2$ is the union of
$H \cap bd(\Lambda)$ and the disjoint paths $P_1, \dots, P_{k-1}$". Without them the bound fails.

- Let each $P_i$ be a path of the circuit $H \cap bd(\Lambda)$ from $v_i$ to $v_{2k-i}$, and let
  $\mathscr{T}' = \mathscr{T}$. Then $H' = H$, so $r_1 = r_2 = \Lambda$ and $d'(r_1, r_2) = 0$,
  which is less than $2k - 6$ for $k \ge 4$.
- Let the paths meet $H$ only in their ends but share a vertex: put a vertex $x$ in $\Lambda$, and
  let $P_i$ consist of the two edges $v_i x$ and $x v_{2k-i}$. Then $r_1$ is bounded by $P_{k-1}$
  and the part of the circuit through $v_k$, and $r_2$ by $P_1$ and the part through $v_{2k}$, so
  both are incident with $x$. A vertex and an incident region are at distance at most $1$, since the
  closed walk of length $2$ of the radial drawing along the edge between them has both inside. So if
  $\mathscr{T}'$ is respectful, then $d'(r_1, r_2) \le 2$, which is less than $2k - 6$ for
  $k \ge 5$. If $\mathscr{T}'$ is not respectful, then $H', \mathscr{T}'$ is not a $\Sigma$-span,
  and the first conclusion of (3.4) fails.

**Correction.** Add the hypotheses that $P_1, \dots, P_{k-1}$ are mutually disjoint and that
$U(P_i) \cap U(H) = \{v_i, v_{2k-i}\}$ for $1 \le i \le k - 1$. With them the printed proof works.
The two uses of (3.4), in (7.10) and (7.11), apply it to disjoint $\eta(H)$-paths, which meet $H$
only in their ends, so they satisfy the new hypotheses.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM15.GM15Theorem34Statement` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Section3.lean` (the corrected
statement), proved by `GraphMinor.GM15.theorem_3_4_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Section3Given.lean`,
and `GraphMinor.GM15.theorem_3_4` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Results.lean`.

## 7. Proof of (3.4), page 123: the proof that (3.2)(iv) holds

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** For (3.2)(iv) the proof says "If $F \subseteq r_1 \cup \{u, v\}$ or
$F \subseteq r_2 \cup \{u, v\}$, then (3.2)(iv) is satisfied, and so we may assume that
$F \subseteq r_3 \cup \{u, v\}$ for some region $r_3 \ne r_1, r_2$ of $H'$ with
$r_3 \subseteq \Lambda$. Let the four vertices in $X$ incident with $r_3$ be
$v_i, v_{i+1}, v_{2k-i-1}, v_{2k-i}$, where $1 \le i \le k - 2$." It does not prove that the regions
of $H'$ in $\Lambda$ are as described, and it does not say why (3.2)(iv) holds in the first two
cases.

**Proof.** We give the details. First, for $1 \le j \le k-1$ we show that
$U(P_j) \subseteq \Lambda \cup \{v_j, v_{2k-j}\}$. Let $e$ be an edge of $P_j$. Since
$U(P_j) \cap U(H) = \{v_j, v_{2k-j}\}$ and these are vertices, $e \cap U(H) = \emptyset$, and so
$e \cap bd(\Lambda) = \emptyset$. Since $e$ is connected, either $e \subseteq \Lambda$ or
$e \subseteq \Sigma - \bar{\Lambda}$; and the second is impossible, since then $e$ would be an edge
of $H' \cap (\Sigma - \Lambda) = H$. Thus every edge of $P_j$ is a subset of $\Lambda$, and so every
vertex of $P_j$ is in $\bar{\Lambda}$. Every vertex of $P_j$ other than $v_j, v_{2k-j}$ is not in
$U(H)$, and hence not in $bd(\Lambda)$, and so it is in $\Lambda$. This proves that
$U(P_j) \subseteq \Lambda \cup \{v_j, v_{2k-j}\}$. Since $U(H) \cap \Lambda = \emptyset$, it follows
that $\Sigma_2 \cap U(H') = bd(\Lambda) \cup U(P_1) \cup \dots \cup U(P_{k-1})$, and that
$H' \cap bd(\Lambda) = H \cap bd(\Lambda)$.

Now let $u, v, F, P$ be as in (3.2)(iv). We show first that for $1 \le j \le k-1$, $P$ does not
contain both $v_j$ and $v_{2k-j}$. The circuit $H \cap bd(\Lambda)$ is the union of two paths with
ends $v_j, v_{2k-j}$, and since $v_1, \dots, v_{2k}$ are in order on this circuit, one of these
paths contains $v_k$ and the other contains $v_{2k}$. If $v_j, v_{2k-j} \in V(P)$, then since $P$ is
a path of this circuit, $P$ includes one of these two paths, and so $|V(P) \cap X| \ge 3$, a
contradiction.

Since $F$ meets $U(H')$ only in $u$ and $v$, the set $U(P) \cup F$ is an $O$-arc in the closed disc
$\Sigma_2$, and so it bounds a closed disc $D \subseteq \Sigma_2$. Let $D^0 = D - (U(P) \cup F)$.
Then $D^0$ is an open disc, and $D^0 \subseteq \Lambda$, since $D^0$ is open in $\Sigma$ and
$D^0 \subseteq \bar{\Lambda}$. We claim that $D^0 \cap U(H') = \emptyset$. For suppose that
$x \in D^0 \cap U(H')$. Since $x \in \Lambda$, the first paragraph implies that
$x \in U(P_j) - \{v_j, v_{2k-j}\}$ for some $j$. This set is connected, and it is disjoint from
$U(P) \cup F$, since $U(P) \cup (F \cap U(H')) \subseteq bd(\Lambda)$. Since it meets $D^0$ and
$\Sigma - D$ is open, it is a subset of $D^0$, and so $v_j, v_{2k-j} \in D$. But
$v_j, v_{2k-j} \in bd(\Lambda)$, and so they are not in $D^0$. Hence they belong to $U(P) \cup F$,
and therefore to $U(P)$, since $F \cap U(H') = \{u, v\} \subseteq U(P)$. Thus
$v_j, v_{2k-j} \in V(P)$, contrary to what we showed above. This proves that
$D^0 \cap U(H') = \emptyset$, and so (3.2)(iv) is satisfied.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM15.theorem_3_4_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Section3Given.lean` (the first
paragraph), `GraphMinor.GM15.no_both_ends_in_bd_path` (the second paragraph) and
`GraphMinor.GM15.theorem_3_4_of_chart` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Section3Parallel.lean`, and
`GraphMinor.GM15.openDisc_of_chord_in_striped_disc` (the third paragraph) in

## 8. Proof of (3.4), page 123: the application of Graph Minors XII, (6.1)

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof says "Now $ord(\mathscr{T}') \ge 2k > 2(k - 1)$, and so by [4,
Theorem (6.1)], $d'(e_1, e_2) \ge 2k - 2$." Here [4] is Graph Minors XII. Its (6.1) is about a
closed disc $\Delta$ whose boundary meets the drawing only in $2k-2$ vertices, such that the drawing
meets $\Delta$ in $k-1$ disjoint paths joining these vertices in pairs. The paper does not give such
a disc, and it does not check the other hypotheses of (6.1). The rest of the proof also uses,
without proof, that $r_1$ and $r_2$ are incident with the edges of $P_{k-1}$ and of $P_1$
respectively.

**Proof.** We give the details. By the first paragraph of the proof in correction 7 above,
$U(P_1), \dots, U(P_{k-1})$ are mutually disjoint lines in the closed disc $\bar{\Lambda}$, and
$U(P_j) \cap bd(\Lambda) = \{v_j, v_{2k-j}\}$ for $1 \le j \le k-1$. Let $D_0$ be the closed unit
disc in the plane, and for $1 \le j \le k-1$ let $C_j$ be the vertical chord of $D_0$ with ends
$(\cos(\pi j/k), \pm \sin(\pi j/k))$. Thus $C_1, \dots, C_{k-1}$ are disjoint, and they occur in
this order from right to left.

We claim that there is a homeomorphism $\phi \colon \bar{\Lambda} \to D_0$ that maps $v_i$ to
$(\cos(\pi i/k), \sin(\pi i/k))$ for $1 \le i \le 2k$, and maps $U(P_j)$ onto $C_j$ for
$1 \le j \le k-1$. Since $v_1, \dots, v_{2k}$ are in order on the $O$-arc $bd(\Lambda)$, there is a
homeomorphism $\phi$ with the first property. Suppose that $\phi(U(P_j)) = C_j$ for $1 \le j < n$,
where $1 \le n \le k-1$. Let $D_1 = D_0$ if $n = 1$, and otherwise let $D_1$ be the part of $D_0$ on
the left of $C_{n-1}$, including $C_{n-1}$. The line $L = \phi(U(P_n))$ is disjoint from $C_{n-1}$,
and its ends are on the left of $C_{n-1}$, so $L \subseteq D_1$. Both $L$ and $C_n$ are lines in the
closed disc $D_1$ with the same ends, meeting $bd(D_1)$ only in their ends. Each of them divides
$D_1$ into two closed discs, bounded by the line and by the two arcs of $bd(D_1)$ between its ends.
Since a homeomorphism between the boundaries of two closed discs extends to a homeomorphism between
the discs, there is a homeomorphism of $D_1$ onto itself that fixes $bd(D_1)$ and maps $L$ onto
$C_n$. We extend it by the identity on $D_0 - D_1$, and compose $\phi$ with the result. The new
$\phi$ still maps $U(P_j)$ onto $C_j$ for $j < n$, since $C_j \subseteq (D_0 - D_1) \cup bd(D_1)$,
and now also for $j = n$. The claim follows by induction on $n$.

The image under $\phi^{-1}$ of the part of $D_0 - bd(D_0)$ on the left of $C_{k-1}$ is connected and
open, it is disjoint from $U(H')$, and its boundary is included in $U(H')$. Hence it is a region of
$H'$ in $\Lambda$. It is incident with $v_k$, and so it is $r_1$, and it is incident with every edge
of $P_{k-1}$. Similarly $r_2$ is the image of the part of $D_0 - bd(D_0)$ on the right of $C_1$, and
it is incident with $v_{2k}$ and with every edge of $P_1$.

Now let $\psi$ be a homeomorphism of the plane with $\psi(D_0) \subseteq D_0$ that fixes every point
of $C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_{k-1}$ and maps every other point of $bd(D_0)$ into $D_0 - bd(D_0)$. (For
instance, let $c = \cos(\pi/k)$, let $h(x) = x$ if $|x| \le c$ and $h(x) = (x + c)/2$ if $x > c$ and
$h(x) = (x - c)/2$ if $x < -c$, let $s(x) = 1 - \min(1/2, \prod_{j=1}^{k-1} (x - \cos(\pi j/k))^2)$,
and let $\psi(x, y) = (h(x), s(x)y)$.) Let $\Delta = \phi^{-1}(\psi(D_0))$ and $F = bd(\Delta)$.
Then $\Delta \subseteq \bar{\Lambda}$ is a closed disc, $\Delta \cap bd(\Lambda)$ is the set $Y$ of
the $2k-2$ ends of $P_1, \dots, P_{k-1}$, and every other point of
$U(P_1) \cup \dots \cup U(P_{k-1})$ is in $\Delta - F$. (For $\psi(D_0) \cap bd(D_0)$ is the set of
ends of $C_1, \dots, C_{k-1}$, and $\psi$ fixes each $C_j$ and maps $D_0 - bd(D_0)$ onto
$\psi(D_0) - bd(\psi(D_0))$.) Since $U(H) \cap \bar{\Lambda} \subseteq bd(\Lambda)$, it follows that
$H' \cap \Delta = P_1 \cup \dots \cup P_{k-1}$, that $H' \cap \overline{\Sigma - \Delta} = H$, and
that $F \cap U(H') = Y$, so $F$ is $H'$-normal. The members of $Y$ occur on $F$ in the order
$v_1, \dots, v_{k-1}, v_{k+1}, \dots, v_{2k-1}$, since $\psi$ fixes them and maps $bd(D_0)$ onto
$bd(\psi(D_0))$; and $P_j$ has ends the $j$th and the $(2k-1-j)$th of them. Moreover $H'$ and $H$
are 2-cell, since they are rigid; $\mathscr{T}$ is a respectful tangle in $H$ of order
$\theta \ge 2k > 2k-2$, where $\theta \ge 2k$ since $X$ is free with respect to $\mathscr{T}$;
$\mathscr{T}'$ is the tangle in $H'$ induced by $\mathscr{T}$; and $Y \subseteq X$ is free with
respect to $\mathscr{T}$, as in the proof of (2) in the proof of (3.3). Since $2k - 2 \ge 4$, [4,
Theorem (6.1)] applies with $\Gamma = H'$, with $2k-2$ in place of $\theta$, and with $\Gamma_1 = H$
and $\mathscr{T}_1 = \mathscr{T}$, and it gives $d'(e_1, e_2) \ge 2k-2$.

## 10. Statement of (3.5), page 123: a hypothesis of XII (5.1) is missing

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** In (3.5), $\Lambda_1, \Lambda_2$ are regions of $H$ bounded by circuits
$C_1, C_2$, the vertices $a_1, \dots, a_\phi$ of $C_1$ and $b_1, \dots, b_\phi$ of $C_2$ are in
order, $\{a_1, \dots, a_\phi\}$ and $\{b_1, \dots, b_\phi\}$ are free, and $P_1, \dots, P_\phi$ are
disjoint $\eta(H)$-paths, with $P_i$ from $\eta(a_i)$ to $\eta(b_i)$. The conclusion is that there
is a $\Sigma'$-span of order $\phi$, where $\Sigma'$ is $\Sigma$ with a handle added. The proof, on
page 124, applies Graph Minors XII, (5.1). That result assumes that every $H$-normal line in
$\Sigma - (\Lambda_1 \cup \Lambda_2)$ from $bd(\Lambda_1)$ to $bd(\Lambda_2)$ meets at least $\phi$
vertices of $H$, and (3.5) does not assume this. Without it the conclusion can fail. Let $\Sigma$ be
the sphere and $\phi \ge 5$. Let $H$ contain a cylindrical grid with four concentric circuits
$C_1, D_1, D_2, C_2$ of length at least $\phi$, and a large grid elsewhere, so that $H$ has a
respectful tangle of large order in which the sets of $a_i$ and of $b_i$ are free. Some line in
$\Sigma - (\Lambda_1 \cup \Lambda_2)$ from $a_1$ to $b_1$ meets only four vertices of $H$, one on
each of the four circuits. Add a handle at $\Lambda_1$ and $\Lambda_2$ and draw the paths
$P_1, \dots, P_\phi$ along it. In the torus $\Sigma'$, this line together with a line along $P_1$
forms an $O$-arc that does not separate $\Sigma'$, so it is not null-homotopic, but it meets only
four vertices of the new drawing. So no tangle of order $\phi$ in that drawing is respectful, and it
gives no $\Sigma'$-span of order $\phi$.

**Correction.** Add the hypothesis: every $H$-normal line
$F \subseteq \Sigma - (\Lambda_1 \cup \Lambda_2)$ with one end in $bd(\Lambda_1)$ and the other in
$bd(\Lambda_2)$ satisfies $|F \cap V(H)| \ge \phi$. With it the printed proof works. The only use of
(3.5) is in Claim (4) in the proof of 8.1 of Graph Minors XVI, page 72. There $\Lambda_1$ and
$\Lambda_2$ come from cleared zones around two vertices that are far apart in the metric of the
tangle, and this gives the new hypothesis.

## 12. Statement of (5.2), page 129: the case $\mu = 0$

**Kind.** Degenerate case.

**What goes wrong.** (5.2) allows $\mu \ge 0$. A $(\lambda, \mu)$-flat $\Sigma$-span has order
$\theta \ge 4\lambda + \mu + 2$. In Claim (3) of the proof, page 130, the proof takes the
$(\theta - 4\lambda - 2)$-truncation $\mathscr{T}^0$ of $\mathscr{T}''$ and says that it exists
since $\theta - 4\lambda - 2 \ge 1$. When $\mu = 0$, only $\theta - 4\lambda - 2 \ge 0$ follows, and
for $\theta = 4\lambda + 2$ there is no truncation of order $0$. The case $\mu = 0$ is also
degenerate in another way: $d(u, v) \ge 0$ holds for every pair $u, v$, so a $\Sigma$-span is
$0$-stepped as soon as there is one $\eta(H)$-path.

**Correction.** Assume $\mu \ge 1$. Then $\theta - 4\lambda - 2 \ge \mu \ge 1$ and the printed proof
works. Every use of (5.2), in Sections 1 and 7, has $\mu \ge 12$.

statement), and `GraphMinor.GM15.theorem_5_2` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Results.lean`.

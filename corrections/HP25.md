# On the two paths theorem and the two disjoint paths problem: corrections

S. Humeau and D. Pous, *On the two paths theorem and the two disjoint paths problem*,
arXiv:2505.16431v2, 14 August 2025.

The formalization uses this paper for its two paths theorem, Theorem 1.3 (page 2), proved through
Lemma 4.3. The crossing extractor of [`docs/crossing/`](../docs/crossing/) uses it as Fact T, and
Graph Minors XIII (8.1) uses the crossing extractor. The algorithm of Section 5 is not used for the
results of the Graph Minors series, so the open question in correction 5 below does not affect
them.

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Proof of Lemma 4.3, page 15: $PzCx$ is crossless in $H$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** In the second case of the proof, $x, y, z$ are consecutive on $C$, and $P$ is
an $x$–$z$ path with no inner vertex on $C$ that minimises the measure $\mu$ (page 14). Let $H$ be
the subgraph of $G$ induced by $V(P)$ and the component of $G - V(P)$ that contains
$C - \{x, y, z\}$, and let $PzCx$ be the cycle made of $P$ and the path of $C - y$ from $z$ to $x$.
The proof says "It is easily proved that $PzCx$ is crossed in any edge extension of $H$, hence $H$
is a web with frame $PzCx$ by induction." The induction hypothesis is Lemma 4.3 for $H$, so it needs
$H$ to be maximally $PzCx$-crossless. This has two parts: $PzCx$ is crossless in $H$, and it is
crossed in every edge extension of $H$. The proof argues only the second part.

The first part needs the maximality of $G$ once more, and not only the choice of $P$. For example,
let $C = xyzl$ and $P = xp_1p_2p_3z$, and let the other edges be $sp_1$, $sp_3$, $st$, $tp_2$ and
$tl$. Then $C$ is crossless in $G$, since $y$ has no neighbour off $C$, and $P$ minimises $\mu$. But
$PzCx$ is crossed in $H$, by the paths $p_1sp_3$ and $p_2tl$. This $G$ is not maximally
$C$-crossless, since $C$ is still crossless in $G + xz$.

**Correction.** Add the missing step: $PzCx$ is crossless in $H$. Theorem 1.3 of the paper cannot be
used for it, since its proof uses Lemma 4.3. We prove the step with the form of the two paths
theorem in (8.3) and (8.5) of N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XIII. The disjoint
paths problem*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B **63** (1995), 65–110, pages 93–95. In outline:

- View $G$, with the cyclic order of $C$, as a society. It has no cross, because $C$ is crossless in
  $G$. By (8.3) the society has a core. Its vertex set is a set $Z$ with $V(C) \subseteq Z$ such
  that each vertex outside $Z$ reaches at most three vertices of $Z$ by paths with no other vertex in
  $Z$. Two vertices of $Z$ are adjacent in the core when a path of $G$ joins them with no inner
  vertex in $Z$. Since there is no cross, (8.5) shows that the core can be drawn in a closed disc
  with $C$ on the boundary, in order.
- Two vertices $u, w$ of $P$ that are adjacent in the core are adjacent in $G$. Otherwise, by the
  maximality of $G$, the graph $G + uw$ has a crossing of $C$. In it, replace the edge $uw$ by the
  path of $G$ from $u$ to $w$ with no inner vertex in $Z$. The other path of the crossing misses this
  path. Indeed, a common vertex is outside $Z$, and it would reach four vertices of $Z$ by paths with
  no other vertex in $Z$: $u$, $w$, and the first vertex of $Z$ in each direction along the other
  path. The choice of $Z$ does not allow this. So $C$ would be crossed in
  $G$, a contradiction. Since $P$ minimises $\mu$, it is induced, so $u$ and $w$ are consecutive on
  $P$. It follows that every vertex of $P$ is in $Z$. Otherwise the last vertex of $Z$ before it on
  $P$ and the first one after it would be adjacent in the core but not consecutive on $P$.
- In the drawing, $P$ meets the boundary of the disc only at $x$ and $z$, since its inner vertices
  are not on $C$ and none of its edges joins two vertices that are consecutive on $C$. So $P$ cuts
  the disc into two closed discs, and the one that contains $C - \{x, y, z\}$ is bounded by $PzCx$.
- A crossing of $PzCx$ in $H$ gives two disjoint paths of the core with alternating ends on $PzCx$.
  Each of their edges has an end off $P$: an edge with both ends on $P$ joins two vertices that are
  consecutive on $P$, so it would be a whole path of the crossing with ends consecutive on $PzCx$,
  which a crossing does not allow. Every vertex of the core off $P$ used here lies in the component
  of $G - V(P)$ that contains $C - \{x, y, z\}$, so it is joined to $C - \{x, y, z\}$ away from $P$.
  So both paths are drawn in the closed disc bounded by $PzCx$. But two disjoint paths in a closed
  disc cannot have alternating ends on its boundary.

Lemma 4.3 holds as printed. It is the classical two paths theorem.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.HP25.Case2.gap` in `GraphMinor/HP25/Gap43.lean` proves that $PzCx$ is
crossless in $H$. The steps above are `GraphMinor.HP25.adj_of_coreGraph_adj` in
`GraphMinor/HP25/Gap43Core.lean`, `GraphMinor.HP25.exists_planeEmbedding` in
`GraphMinor/HP25/Gap43Rural.lean` and `GraphMinor.HP25.PlaneEmbedding.plane_gap` in
`GraphMinor/HP25/Gap43Plane.lean`. (8.3) and (8.5) of Graph Minors XIII are
`GraphMinor.GM13.theorem_8_3_proof` and `GraphMinor.GM13.theorem_8_5_proof` in

`GraphMinor.HP25.theorem_1_3`, both in `GraphMinor/HP25/Lemma43.lean`.

## 2. Section 5.1, definition of $P$-sides, page 17: the paths meant

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** The definition reads "we say that a vertex $y \in P$ is on $P$-side $i$ when
$y$ and $\mathring{P_i}$ are $P$-connected". By the definitions on page 5, a set $U$ is
$H$-connected to $K$ when there is an $H$-path from $U$ to $K$, and an $H$-path has both ends in
$H$. Read this way, the definition never holds, since $\mathring{P_i}$ has no vertex of $P$. The
proofs use another meaning: "a $\mathring{P_1}$-$y_r$ path $Q$ inner disjoint with $P \cup C$"
(proof of Lemma 5.4, page 19) and "a $C \cup P$-path from $z$ to $\mathring{P_i}$" (Claim 5.6,
page 19).

**Correction.** The definition reads: $y$ is on $P$-side $i$ when some path from $y$ to
$\mathring{P_i}$ has no inner vertex in $C \cup P$. The results are not affected.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.HP25.IsOnSide` in `GraphMinor/HP25/Defs.lean`.

## 3. Statement of Lemma 5.4, page 19: the edge $y_jy_s$ need not be safe

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** Here $P = y_1 \dots y_l$ is a $C$-path between non-consecutive vertices of the
cycle $C$, and $P_1$, $P_2$ are the two paths of $C$ between the ends of $P$. A set $F$ of edges is
safe (page 15) if $C$ is crossed in $G$ whenever it is crossed in $G + F$. A crossing of $C$ is a
pair of disjoint paths with their ends on $C$, no inner vertex on $C$, and ends that alternate
around $C$ (Definition 2.4, page 7). Lemma 5.4 defines indices $j < r \le s$ from the $P$-sides of
the vertices of $P$, and claims: "Then the edge $y_jy_s$ is safe, and in the graph $G+y_jy_s$, all
vertices of $Py_jy_s$ lie on both $Py_jy_sP$-sides." The first conclusion is false.

For example, take seven vertices $a, b, c, d, p, q, r$, the cycle $C = abcd$ and the $C$-path
$P = cpqra$, and let the other edges be $bp$, $dq$, $dr$, $aq$ and $pr$. The two paths of $C$
between $c$ and $a$ have interiors $\{b\}$ and $\{d\}$, and $P$ separates them. Every vertex is on
$C$ or on $P$, so the sides can be read off the neighbours: $c$ and $a$ are on both sides, $p$ is
only on the side of $b$, and $q$ and $r$ are only on the side of $d$. So $j = 1$, $r = 2$, $s = 3$,
and the edge is $cq$.

- The cycle $C$ is crossless in $G$. A crossing would join $a$ to $c$ and $b$ to $d$ by disjoint
  paths with no inner vertex on $C$. The only neighbour of $c$ off $C$ is $p$, and so is the only
  neighbour of $b$ off $C$, so both paths would pass through $p$.
- In $G + cq$ the paths $aqc$ and $bprd$ form a crossing of $C$.

So Algorithm 5.1 (page 18) can give a wrong answer. Line 5 asks only for "a C-path between
non-consecutive vertices of C", so it may pick this $P$. Algorithm 5.2 then adds $cq$, after which
every vertex of the new path $cqra$ is on both sides, and $cqra$ still separates $b$ from $d$. From
then on Algorithm 5.1 works in $G + cq$, where $C$ is crossed, so by Proposition 5.2 one of the two
subinstances is crossed, and Algorithm 5.1 answers True.

A first try at a correction is to ask that $P$ is induced. This is not enough. Take nine vertices
$0, \dots, 8$, the cycle $C = 0123$ and the $C$-path $P = 14673$, and let the other edges be $06$,
$07$, $24$, $38$, $45$, $57$, $58$, $68$ and $78$. Then $P$ is induced and is a shortest $C$-path
between $1$ and $3$, and it separates $\{0\}$ from $\{2\}$. The only vertices off $C \cup P$ are $5$
and $8$, and neither is adjacent to $0$ or $2$. So $1$ and $3$ are on both sides, $4$ is only on the
side of $2$, and $6$ and $7$ are only on the side of $0$. So $j = 1$, $r = 2$, $s = 3$, and the
edge is $16$. The only neighbour of $2$ off $C$, and the only neighbour of $1$ off $C$, is $4$, so
$C$ is crossless in $G$. But in $G + 16$ the paths $07542$ and $1683$ form a crossing.

**Correction.** The second conclusion holds as printed, for every $P$. For the first one we ask more
of $P$. Call $P$ *tight* if every component of $G - (C \cup P)$ with no neighbour in
$\mathring{P_1} \cup \mathring{P_2}$ has all its neighbours on $P$ among two consecutive vertices of
$P$. In the second example above, the component $\{5, 8\}$ has the neighbours $4, 6, 7, 3$ on $P$, so
$P$ is not tight.

Lemma 5.4 holds when $P$ is induced and tight. We prove more. Let $P$ be induced and tight, let two
vertices $y_a$ and $y_b$ of $P$ with $a < b$ be on side $i$, and let no vertex strictly between them
on $P$ be on side $i$. Then $y_ay_b$ is safe. Lemma 5.4 is the case $a = j$ and $b = s$, with $i$
the side of $y_s$ that $y_r$ is not on (either side when $r = s$). By the choice of $r$ and $s$, no
vertex strictly between $y_j$ and $y_s$ is on that side. The proof does not use the fans of the
paper's proof. In outline, suppose that $C$ is crossless in $G$. Then $G$ is contained in a web with
frame $C$, by Theorem 1.3. In the drawing of this web, the triangles on side $i$ along $y_aPy_b$
form a connected strip. Take a crossing of $C$ in $G + y_ay_b$. In the path that uses the new edge,
replace the edge by a curve through the strip. The other path cannot enter the strip, because $P$ is
induced and tight and no vertex strictly between $y_a$ and $y_b$ is on side $i$. This gives two
disjoint connected sets in a disc that join alternating points of its boundary, which is impossible.

Line 5 of Algorithm 5.1 lets us choose $P$, and two steps before the loop of Algorithm 5.2 make it
tight. Start from an induced $C$-path between non-consecutive vertices of $C$ that separates
$\mathring{P_1}$ from $\mathring{P_2}$.

- First, as long as some component $D$ of $G - (C \cup P)$ with no neighbour in
  $\mathring{P_1} \cup \mathring{P_2}$ has, strictly between its first and last neighbour on $P$, a
  vertex that is on some side, do the following. If there is a vertex on each side there, then $C$
  is crossed. Otherwise, replace that part of $P$ by an induced path through $D$. This adds no edge.
  The set of vertices of $G - V(P)$ that are joined to $\mathring{P_1} \cup \mathring{P_2}$ in
  $G - V(P)$ grows, so this stops.
- Second, for each maximal part $yNz$ of $P$ such that $y$ and $z$ are on some side and the vertices
  of $N$ are on no side, add the edge $yz$ and replace $yNz$ by $yz$ in $P$. After the first step,
  $\{y, z\}$ separates $N$ from $C$, so the argument of Claim 5.5 shows that $yz$ is safe.

After these two steps, $P$ is induced, tight and still separating, and every vertex of $P$ is on
some side. Then the loop of Algorithm 5.2 runs as printed, and each edge it adds is safe by the
result above. One call of this completion makes at most $n = |V(G)|$ steps of the first kind. A run
of Algorithm 5.1 makes many calls, and we do not know whether the total number of these steps in a
run is $O(n)$. We found a family of graphs on which, for some choices of the paths, a run makes of
order $n^2$ of them. So with these steps we only know the running time $O(n^2(n + m))$, not the
$O(nm)$ of Proposition 5.9 (page 23).

The same lemma is Lemma 6.15.1, page 158, of S. Humeau, *Structures and equations of sourced graphs:
small treewidth and disjoint paths*, PhD thesis, ENS de Lyon, defended 24 October 2025, HAL
tel-05362382v1. The examples above apply to it too.

**Lean.** The two examples are `GraphMinor.HP25.Ex54.lemma_5_4_counterexample` and

the stronger result is `GraphMinor.HP25.strip_safe` in the same file. Tightness is

`GraphMinor.HP25.reroute_all` in `GraphMinor/HP25/Sec5Tighten.lean` and

## 4. Claim 5.5 in the proof of Lemma 5.4, page 19: $\{y_j, y_r\}$ need not separate

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** Claim 5.5 says that the edge $y_jy_r$ is safe. Its proof says "As vertices
$y_t$ with $j<t<r$ have no $P$-sides, $\{y_j,y_r\}$ separates them from $C$." But such a vertex
$y_t$ may reach $C$ through a chord of $P$, or through a component of $G - (C \cup P)$ that has no
neighbour in $\mathring{P_1} \cup \mathring{P_2}$ but has a neighbour on $P$ after $y_r$. For
example, take the cycle $C = 0123$ and the $C$-path $P = 0452$, and let the other edges be $53$,
$47$ and $72$. Then $P$ is induced and separates $\{1\}$ from $\{3\}$. The vertex $4$ is on no side
and $5$ is only on the side of $3$, so $j = 1$ and $y_r = 5$. But the path $472$ joins $4$ to $C$ and
misses $0$ and $5$. Claim 5.5 holds in this example, since $1$ has no neighbour off $C$, and we know
no counterexample to the claim.

**Correction.** Assume that $P$ is induced and tight, as in the correction to Lemma 5.4 above. Let
$j < t < r$. By the choice of $r$, the vertex $y_t$ is on no side, so it has no neighbour in
$\mathring{P_1} \cup \mathring{P_2}$. Since $P$ is induced, its only neighbours on $P$ are
$y_{t-1}$ and $y_{t+1}$. A component of $G - (C \cup P)$ with a neighbour $y_t$ has no neighbour in
$\mathring{P_1} \cup \mathring{P_2}$, since $y_t$ is on no side. So by tightness its neighbours on
$P$ are among $y_{t-1}$, $y_t$ and $y_{t+1}$. Hence the vertices $y_t$ with $j < t < r$, together
with these components, have all their neighbours among themselves and $y_j$, $y_r$. So
$\{y_j, y_r\}$ separates them from $C$, and the rest of the proof of Claim 5.5 is unchanged.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.HP25.tightZone_closed` and `GraphMinor.HP25.claim_5_5_tight` in
`GraphMinor/HP25/Sec5Tight.lean`.

## 5. Section 5.3, pages 22–23: a crossing of $G + F$, not of $G$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** When Algorithm 5.1 answers True, Section 5.3 builds a crossing as follows: "if
some recursive call $\mathbf{2DP}(G_i,C_i)$ returns True, and hence returns a crossing $(Q,R)$ of
$C_i$ in $G_i$, its shape must fall into one of the five cases of Figure 11. The solutions presented
there to build a crossing of $C$ in $G$ still work here, thanks to vertices of $P$ being all on both
$P$-sides." But line 9 of Algorithm 5.1 replaces $G$ by $G + F$, where $F$ is the set of edges that
Algorithm 5.2 adds, and $G_1$, $G_2$ are subgraphs of $G + F$. So this gives a crossing of $C$ in
$G + F$, which may use edges of $F$. The paper does not turn it into a crossing of $C$ in the input
graph. That $F$ is safe says only that $C$ is crossed in $G$ too. The proof of Lemma 5.4 shows this
by contradiction, through web completions, so it gives no way to find the paths.

Two simple ways to undo an added edge both fail. Take the vertices $0, \dots, 10$, the cycle
$C = 0123$ and the $C$-path $P = 1\,4\,5\,6\,7\,3$, and let the other edges be $04$, $05$, $1\,10$,
$27$, $29$, $2\,10$, $38$, $48$, $59$, $68$, $6\,10$, $8\,10$ and $9\,10$. Then $P$ is induced and
tight, and it separates $\{2\}$ from $\{0\}$. The vertices $1, 4, 5, 3$ are on both sides and $6, 7$
are only on the side of $2$, so the edge of Lemma 5.4 is $53$.

- The paths $0\,4\,8\,6\,7\,2$ and $1\,10\,9\,5\,3$ form a crossing of $C$ in $G + 53$, and the
  second one uses the new edge.
- Every path of $G$ from $1$ to $3$ meets the first path, so no crossing of $C$ in $G$ contains it.
- Replacing the edge $53$ in the second path by the part $5\,6\,7\,3$ of $P$ gives the path
  $1\,10\,9\,5\,6\,7\,3$. Every path of $G$ from $2$ to $0$ meets it, so no crossing of $C$ in
  $G$ contains it either.
- Yet $C$ is crossed in $G$, by $0\,5\,6\,7\,2$ and $1\,10\,8\,3$.

**Correction.** We can repair this only in part.

- In the completion of the correction to Lemma 5.4 above, the edges of the second step that a later
  edge of the loop skips can be left out, and the completion keeps all its properties. Then every
  added edge joins two consecutive vertices of the final path $P'$, so it is an edge of both cycles
  $C_1$ and $C_2$, and a crossing of a subinstance uses no added edge. Every vertex of $P'$ is on
  both sides in $G$ itself, so the constructions of Figure 11 turn a crossing of a subinstance into
  a crossing of $C$ in $G + F$.
- Then we undo the added edges one at a time, in the reverse order. For each of them we have four
  rules that turn a crossing of $C$ in the graph with the edge into a crossing in the graph without
  it, each with a bounded number of searches. The first two rules are the two ways above. The other
  two use a part of the other path of the crossing that joins the skipped part of $P$ to the rest
  of $P$. Each rule is proved correct when its condition holds.
- We do not know whether one of the four rules always applies. We know no case where none does.

So, with the completion of the correction to Lemma 5.4, the yes-or-no answer of Algorithm 5.1 is
right. What is missing is a proof that a crossing of the input graph can always be found in this
way, within the time that Proposition 5.9 (page 23) and the remark after its proof (page 24) claim.
Section 6.16, pages 163–164, of the thesis cited in the correction to Lemma 5.4 above repeats the
text of Section 5.3.

# Graph Minors XVII: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XVII. Taming a vortex*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B
**77** (1999), 162–210.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM17/GM17.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM17/GM17.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Statement of (1.1)(ii), page 163: the coefficients of $a$ and $b$

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** (1.1)(ii) says that $\Sigma(a, b, c) \simeq \Sigma(a', b', c')$ if and only if
$c = c'$, $a + 2b = a' + 2b'$, and both or neither of $b, b'$ are zero. Here $a$ counts handles and
$b$ counts crosscaps, and the two coefficients are swapped. For example,
$\Sigma(1, 1, 0) \simeq \Sigma(0, 3, 0)$ by Dyck's theorem, but $a + 2b = 3$ and $a' + 2b' = 6$.
Condition (ii) in the definition of "simpler than" on page 164 uses $2a + b$, as it should.

**Correction.** The equation reads $2a + b = 2a' + b'$. With this change (1.1)(ii) holds: the
surface $\Sigma(a, b, c)$ has Euler characteristic $2 - 2a - b - c$ and $c$ cuffs, and it is
orientable exactly when $b = 0$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.Surface.ModelCriterionStatement` in
`GraphMinor/Surface/TopologyStatements.lean` states (1.1)(ii) with $2a + b = 2a' + b'$. It is
proved as `GraphMinor.Surface.modelCriterion` in `GraphMinor/Surface/ModelCriterion.lean`.

## 2. Definition of "simpler than", page 164: the two forms are not equivalent

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** Let $\Sigma \simeq \Sigma(a, b, c)$ and $\Sigma' \simeq \Sigma(a', b', c')$
be connected. Page 164 defines "$\Sigma$ is simpler than $\Sigma'$" by three conditions: (i) if
$b' = 0$ then $b = 0$; (ii) $2a + b \le 2a' + b'$; (iii) $4a + 2b + c < 4a' + 2b' + c'$. It then
says that, equivalently, there are such $a, b, c, a', b', c'$ with $a \le a'$, $b \le b'$ and
$4a + 2b + c < 4a' + 2b' + c'$. The two forms are not equivalent. The torus $\Sigma(1, 0, 0)$ and
the Klein bottle with one cuff $\Sigma(0, 2, 1)$ satisfy (i)–(iii), since $b' = 2 \ne 0$,
$2 \le 2$ and $4 < 5$. By (1.1)(ii), as corrected above, $(1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 2, 1)$ are the only
triples for these two surfaces, so $a \le a'$ fails.

The difference matters on page 207. The proof of (13.4) ends with "$H$ cannot be drawn in $\Sigma$,
since $H$ cannot be drawn in $\Sigma_0$ and $\Sigma$ is simpler than or homeomorphic to
$\Sigma_0$". Under the first form this step fails: $K_7$ can be drawn in the torus (Heawood) but not
in the Klein bottle (Franklin, 1934), and so not in $\Sigma(0, 2, 1)$.

**Correction.** "Simpler than" is defined by the second form: there exist $a, b, c, a', b', c'$ with
$\Sigma \simeq \Sigma(a, b, c)$, $\Sigma' \simeq \Sigma(a', b', c')$, $a \le a'$, $b \le b'$ and
$4a + 2b + c < 4a' + 2b' + c'$. The second form implies (i)–(iii), because $b \le b'$ gives (i), and
$a \le a'$ and $b \le b'$ give (ii). With the second form, a graph that can be drawn in $\Sigma$ can
be drawn in $\Sigma'$: a drawing in $\Sigma(a, b, c)$ survives when discs are pasted onto cuffs, and
when handles, crosscaps and holes are added away from it. This is what page 207 uses. All numbered
results of the paper hold with this definition.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.Surface.IsSimplerThan` in `GraphMinor/Surface/TopologyStatements.lean` is the
second form, and `GraphMinor.Surface.IsSimplerThan.conditions` in the same file derives (i)–(iii)
from it. The theorem `GraphMinor.Surface.IsSimplerThan.canBeDrawn` in
`GraphMinor/Surface/DrawCarry.lean` proves that a graph that can be drawn in $\Sigma$ can be drawn
in $\Sigma'$. It is used in the proof of (13.4), `GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_13_4_proof` in
`GraphMinor/GM17/Section13Proved.lean`.

## 4. Definition of the warp of a portrayal, page 168: the warp is at least 1

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** Page 168 defines the warp of a portrayal $\pi$ as the maximum of the warps of
$\pi$ at its cuffs, "or 1 if $bd(\Sigma) = \emptyset$". With this definition, (5.1) on page 171 is
false. (5.1) says that a $0$-redundant $\mathscr{T}$-central portrayal has warp at least 1 at every
cuff. Its proof pastes a disc onto a cuff $\Theta$ of warp 0, and claims that the new portrayal is
simpler than $\pi$. If $\Theta$ is the only cuff, the new surface has no boundary, so the new
portrayal has warp 1, while $\pi$ has warp 0.

Here is a counterexample. Let $\pi$ be a $\mathscr{T}$-central portrayal of $G$ in the disc
$\Sigma(0, 0, 1)$, with $\beta(n) = \emptyset$ for every node $n$ on its cuff $\Theta$. (For
example, draw a large grid $G$ inside the disc, with one cell for each edge, and let $\mathscr{T}$
be the tangle of the grid.) Every cell that borders $\Theta$ has warp 0, so $\pi$ has warp 0 at
$\Theta$, and warp 0. A portrayal simpler than $\pi$ would need either a surface simpler than the
disc, that is, the sphere, where its warp is $1 > 0$, or a warp below 0 at some cuff. Neither is
possible, so $\pi$ is $0$-redundant. But $\pi$ has warp 0 at $\Theta$.

**Correction.** The warp of $\pi$ is the maximum of 1 and the warps of $\pi$ at its cuffs. When
$bd(\Sigma) = \emptyset$ this is 1, as printed. For $p \ge 1$, "warp $\le p$" means the same under
both definitions, so the change matters only for warp 0. With it, pasting a disc onto a cuff of
warp 0 does not raise the warp, and the proof of (5.1) works. So (5.1) holds as printed, and so does
the remark after it that every $0$-redundant $\mathscr{T}$-central portrayal has warp at least 1.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM17.Portrayal.warp` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Defs.lean` takes the maximum with 1.
(5.1) is `GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_5_1` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Results.lean`.

## 9. Proof of (7.4), page 180: the verification of (P1)–(P5) and (P7)

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The proof says "**(P1)**–**(P5)** and **(P7)** are easily verified, and we omit
them." The verification needs the cells and nodes of $\Gamma'$, and border cells of $\Gamma$ inside
$\Delta$ at the ends of $F$.

**Proof.** We give the details. Every cell of $\Gamma$ is disjoint from $F$, since $F$ is
$\Gamma$-normal; and the frontier of $\Delta$ in $\Sigma$ is included in $F$, since
$bd(\Delta) \subseteq F \cup bd(\Sigma)$. So every cell of $\Gamma$ is a subset of $\Delta$ or of
$\Sigma - \Delta$, and we let $I_1, I_2$ be the sets of cells of the first and the second kind. Then
$\alpha(\Delta) = \cup(\alpha(c) \colon c \in I_1)$. From the definition of $\Gamma'$, its cells are
$c'$ and the members of $I_2$, its nodes are the nodes of $\Gamma$ not in $\Delta - \{n_1, n_2\}$,
$\tilde{c}' = \{n_1, n_2\}$, and $\tilde{c}$ is the same in $\Gamma'$ as in $\Gamma$ for
$c \in I_2$. Moreover $c'$ is a border cell, since $bd(\Delta) - F$ is a nonempty subset of
$bd(\Sigma)$; and for $i = 1, 2$ the cell of $\Gamma$ that contains the points of $bd(\Delta) - F$
near $n_i$ is a border cell $c_i \in I_1$ with $n_i \in \tilde{c}_i$. Now **(P1)** holds, since
$\alpha'(c') \cup \cup(\alpha'(c) \colon c \in I_2) = \cup(\alpha(c) \colon c \in C(\Gamma)) = G$,
and the hypergraphs $\alpha(c)$ $(c \in C(\Gamma))$ have no common edges. **(P2)** holds for $c'$,
since if $n_i \in dom(\gamma)$ then $\gamma(n_i) \in V(\alpha(c_i)) \subseteq V(\alpha(\Delta))$ by
**(P2)** for $\pi$; and **(P3)** holds for $c'$, since
$\beta(n_i) \subseteq V(\alpha(c_i)) \subseteq V(\alpha(\Delta))$ by **(P3)** for $\pi$, as $c_i$ is
a border cell. For the cells in $I_2$, these conditions and **(P4)** are the same as for $\pi$,
since $\beta'$ and $\gamma'$ are restrictions of $\beta$ and $\gamma$ and the cuffs are unchanged.
For **(P5)**, let $c$ be an internal cell of $\Gamma'$. Then $c \in I_2$, and
$\alpha'(-c) \subseteq \alpha(-c)$ since $\alpha'(c') = \alpha(\Delta)$; so **(P5)** for $c$ follows
from **(P5)** for $\pi$. For **(P7)**, the nodes of $\Gamma'$ bordering a cuff are nodes of $\Gamma$
bordering it, in the same order, and $\beta'$ agrees with $\beta$ on them.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_7_4_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Section7Proved.lean`, which gets
the cells and nodes of $\Gamma'$ from `GraphMinor.GM17.normalIArcReplaceDisc` in

## 11. Proof of (10.1), page 188: the proof

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** After (10.1) the paper says: "The proof (using (6.6) with $|X| = 1$ and
**(P6)** and **(P7)**) is clear but lengthy, and we omit it." No step of the proof is given.

**Proof.** We give the proof. By (9.8)(i), $|\beta(n_i)| = q$ for $0 \le i \le k$. So for
$1 \le i \le k$, every vertex in $\beta(n_{i-1})$ is the initial vertex of exactly one path of the
standard linkage in $\alpha(c_i)$, and every vertex in $\beta(n_i)$ is the terminal vertex of
exactly one. For $u \in \beta(n_{i-1})$, let $P(i, u)$ be the path of this linkage with initial
vertex $u$, and let $P^-(i, u) = V(P(i, u)) - \{u\}$. Then $P_i = P(i, v_{i-1})$, and we write
$U(v) = P^-(1, v_0) \cup \dots \cup P^-(k, v_{k-1})$, so that $V(L(v, n_0)) = \{v\} \cup U(v)$.

(1) *Let $1 \le i \le k$ and $u \in \beta(n_{i-1})$, and let $x \in P^-(i, u)$ with
$x \in V(\alpha(-c_i)) \cup \beta(n_{i-1}) \cup \beta(n_i)$. Then $x$ is the terminal vertex of
$P(i, u)$, and $x \in \beta(n_i +) - \beta(n_{i-1} +)$.*

*Subproof.* If $x \in \beta(n_{i-1})$, then $x$ is the initial vertex of a path of the standard
linkage in $\alpha(c_i)$, which is $P(i, u)$ since the paths are disjoint, and so $x = u$, which is
impossible. Since also $x \ne \gamma(n_{i-1})$ by the choice of the standard linkage,
$x \notin \beta(n_{i-1} +)$. If $x \in V(\alpha(-c_i))$, then
$x \in \beta(n_{i-1} +) \cup \beta(n_i +)$ by **(P6)**, so $x \in \beta(n_i +)$, and
$x \in \beta(n_i)$ since $x \ne \gamma(n_i)$. So in all cases $x \in \beta(n_i)$, and in the same
way $x$ is the terminal vertex of $P(i, u)$. This proves (1).

(2) *Let $1 \le i, j \le k$, $u \in \beta(n_{i-1})$ and $u' \in \beta(n_{j-1})$, with
$(i, u) \ne (j, u')$. Then $P^-(i, u) \cap P^-(j, u') = \emptyset$.*

*Subproof.* If $i = j$ then $u \ne u'$, and the claim holds since $P(i, u)$ and $P(i, u')$ are
disjoint. Suppose that $i \ne j$ and $x \in P^-(i, u) \cap P^-(j, u')$. Then
$x \in V(\alpha(c_j)) \subseteq V(\alpha(-c_i))$, and so $x \in \beta(n_i +) - \beta(n_{i-1} +)$ by
(1); and in the same way $x \in \beta(n_j +) - \beta(n_{j-1} +)$. But $n_i, n_{j-1}, n_j, n_{i-1}$
are in order, and so $x \in \beta(n_{j-1} +) \cup \beta(n_{i-1} +)$ by **(P7)**, a
contradiction. This proves (2).

For $0 \le i \le k$, the map $v \mapsto v_i$ is an injection from $\beta(n_0)$ into $\beta(n_i)$,
since distinct paths of a standard linkage have distinct terminal vertices. Since $n_k = n_0$ and
$\beta(n_0)$ is finite, it follows that $v \mapsto v_k$ is a permutation of $\beta(n_0)$.

(3) *For each $v \in \beta(n_0)$, the sets $P^-(1, v_0), \dots, P^-(k, v_{k-1})$ are mutually
disjoint; and for distinct $v, v' \in \beta(n_0)$, $U(v) \cap U(v') = \emptyset$.*

*Subproof.* This follows from (2), since the pairs $(i, v_{i-1})$ $(1 \le i \le k)$ are distinct,
and $v_{i-1} \ne v'_{i-1}$ for $1 \le i \le k$ by the injectivity of $v \mapsto v_{i-1}$. This
proves (3).

(4) *For each $v \in \beta(n_0)$, $U(v) \ne \emptyset$.*

*Subproof.* Suppose that $U(v) = \emptyset$. Then each $P_i$ has only one vertex, and so
$v_i = v_{i-1}$ for $1 \le i \le k$. Hence $v_i = v$ for $0 \le i \le k$, and so $v \in \beta(n)$
for every node $n$ bordering $\Theta$. But this is contrary to (6.6) with $X = \{v\}$, since
$z \ge 3$. This proves (4).

(5) *For all $v, u \in \beta(n_0)$, $u \in U(v)$ if and only if $u = v_k$.*

*Subproof.* By (4), there is a maximum $i$ such that $P^-(i, v_{i-1}) \ne \emptyset$. Then
$v_i \in P^-(i, v_{i-1})$, and $v_h = v_i$ for $i \le h \le k$; so $v_k \in U(v)$. Conversely,
suppose that $u \in U(v)$. Since $v \mapsto v_k$ is a permutation of $\beta(n_0)$, there exists
$v' \in \beta(n_0)$ with $v'_k = u$, and then $u \in U(v')$ by what we just proved. By (3),
$v' = v$, and so $u = v_k$. This proves (5).

Now let $v \in \beta(n_0)$, and traverse $P_1, \dots, P_k$ in turn, starting from $v$. By (3), and
since each $P_i$ is a path, no vertex of $U(v)$ is met twice. The edges traversed are distinct,
since paths in different cells have no common edge by **(P1)**. If $v_k \ne v$, then
$v \notin U(v)$ by (5), so no vertex is met twice, and $L(v, n_0)$ is a path from $v$ to $v_k$. If
$v_k = v$, the traversal is a closed walk, with at least one edge by (4), in which no vertex is met
twice except $v$, which is met only at the start and the end; so $L(v, n_0)$ is a circuit.

Now let $v, v' \in \beta(n_0)$ be distinct. Suppose that some edge $e$ belongs to both $L(v, n_0)$
and $L(v', n_0)$, and choose $i, j$ such that $e$ is an edge of $P(i, v_{i-1})$ and of
$P(j, v'_{j-1})$. Then $i = j$ by **(P1)**; but $v_{i-1} \ne v'_{i-1}$, so $P(i, v_{i-1})$ and
$P(i, v'_{i-1})$ are distinct paths of the standard linkage in $\alpha(c_i)$, and they are disjoint,
a contradiction. Thus $L(v, n_0)$ and $L(v', n_0)$ are edge-disjoint. Now let $u$ be a vertex of
both. Since $U(v) \cap U(v') = \emptyset$ by (3), either $u = v$ or $u = v'$, and from the symmetry
we may assume that $u = v$. Since $v \ne v'$, it follows that $v \in U(v')$, and so $v = v'_k$ by
(5). Hence $v'_k \ne v'$; and $v_k \ne v$, since $v'_k = v$ and $v \mapsto v_k$ is injective. So
both $L(v, n_0)$ and $L(v', n_0)$ are paths, and $u$ is the initial vertex of $L(v, n_0)$ and the
terminal vertex of $L(v', n_0)$. This proves (10.1).

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_10_1_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Section10Proved.lean`, restated
as `GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_10_1` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Results.lean`. Claim (1) is

`GraphMinor.GM17.CuffStandardLinkages.disjoint_support_tail_stepWalk`, and its use of **(P7)** is
`GraphMinor.GM17.unique_entry_index` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Section10.lean`. The injectivity of
$v \mapsto v_i$ is `GraphMinor.GM17.CuffStandardLinkages.traceVertex_injOn` in
`GraphMinor/GM17/Section10.lean`. Claim (3) is
`GraphMinor.GM17.CuffStandardLinkages.nodup_support_tail_circWalk` and

## 12. Proof of (12.6), page 200: deleting the edges of $G^*$ keeps the warps

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof puts $G' = G \cup G^*$ and says: "If there is a
$\mathscr{T}'$-central portrayal of $G'$ resembling $\pi$, then there is a $\mathscr{T}$-central
portrayal of $G$ resembling $\pi$." The natural way is to delete the edges of $G^*$ from each cell.
But this can delete edges of $\alpha(c)^*$: an edge $uv$ of $G^*$ may lie in $\alpha(c)$, while the
edge of $G$ that joins $u$ and $v$ lies in another cell. The warp of a cell is defined by linkages
in $\alpha(c)^*$, so it can go down. The paper gives no reason why the new portrayal still resembles
$\pi$, rather than having a smaller warp at some cuff.

**Correction.** Add the following step. Let $\pi'$ be a $\mathscr{T}'$-central portrayal of $G'$
resembling $\pi$, and let $\pi_1$, with cells $\alpha_1(c)$, be obtained from $\pi'$ by deleting the
edges of $G^*$. For each cell $c$, $\alpha_1(c)^*$ is a subgraph of $\alpha'(c)^*$ with the same
vertices. So $\pi_1$ is a $\mathscr{T}$-central portrayal of $G$, since $\mathscr{T}$ and
$\mathscr{T}'$ are both embodiments of $\mathscr{T}^*$. Also, at each cuff the warp of $\pi_1$ is at
most that of $\pi'$, which equals that of $\pi$. Suppose that it is smaller at some cuff. Each edge
$uv$ of $G^*$ has an edge of $G$ with ends $u$ and $v$, and this edge lies in some $\alpha_1(c)$.
Put $uv$ into one such cell, and then delete the edges of $G$. This gives a portrayal $\pi_2$ of
$G^*$ with the same painting and $\alpha_2(c)^* \subseteq \alpha_1(c)^*$ for every cell $c$, so its
warps are at most those of $\pi_1$. It is $\mathscr{T}^*$-central, because each $\alpha_2(c)$ has
the same vertices as $\alpha_1(c)$ and lies in $\alpha_1(c)^*$, which is small relative to
$\mathscr{T}^*$ by the definition of the embodiment. By (ii) in the definition of "simpler" on page
171, $\pi_2$ is simpler than $\pi$. This contradicts the $(2p+7)$-redundancy of $\pi$, used with
$Z = \emptyset$. So $\pi_1$ resembles $\pi$, and (12.6) holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_12_6` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Results.lean`, proved by
`GraphMinor.GM17.theorem_12_6_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM17/Section12d.lean`. The step above is
`GraphMinor.GM17.resembles_of_warpLE` in the same file, and the move of a portrayal to $G^*$ is

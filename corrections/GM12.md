# Graph Minors XII: corrections

N. Robertson and P. D. Seymour, *Graph Minors. XII. Distance on a surface*, J. Combin. Theory Ser. B
**64** (1995), 240–272.

A corrected copy of the paper, with each correction below marked in place, is
[GM12/GM12.pdf](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers/blob/main/GM12/GM12.pdf) in the
repository [GraphMinorPapers](https://github.com/hajiaghayi/GraphMinorPapers).

The corrections are in the order of the pages.

## 1. Section 1, page 242: the observation on $a(c)$ and $ins(W)$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper uses "the easy observation that for a closed walk $W$ in $K$ of
length $<2\theta$, and for $c \in A(\Gamma)$, $a(c) \subseteq ins(W)$ if and only if
$c \cap ins(W) \ne \emptyset$", and gives no proof. The copy adds one.

**Proof.** We prove this observation. Each circuit $C$ of $K | W$ has length $< 2\theta$, and so
$ins(C)$ is defined, and it is a closed disc with boundary $U(C)$. We use two facts. First, an edge
of $\Gamma$ misses $U(K)$, by (i). Second, let $L$ be a subdrawing of $K$ with a point in a region
$c$ of $\Gamma$, and let $v$ be the vertex of $K$ in $c$. Then $v \in V(L)$. For the point is $v$ or
lies in an edge of $L$, and this edge lies in $c$, since it misses $U(\Gamma)$ by (i). By (iii) it
has an end not in $V(\Gamma)$, and this end is not in $U(\Gamma)$, by (i), and so it is in $c$, and
it is $v$ by (ii).

Now if $a(c) \subseteq ins(W)$, then $c \cap ins(W) \ne \emptyset$, since $a(c)$ and $c$ are
non-empty and one of them includes the other. Conversely, let $x \in c \cap ins(W)$. If
$c \subseteq V(\Gamma)$, then $a(c) = c = \{x\}$, and so $a(c) \subseteq ins(W)$. If $c$ is an edge
of $\Gamma$, then $x \notin U(W)$ by the first fact, and so $x \in ins(C)$ for some circuit $C$ of
$K | W$. The region $a(c)$ of $K$ is connected, it misses $U(C)$, which is the boundary of $ins(C)$,
and it meets $ins(C)$ at $x$. So $a(c) \subseteq ins(C) \subseteq ins(W)$. Finally, let $c$ be a
region of $\Gamma$, and let $v$ be the vertex of $K$ in $c$. If $x \in U(W)$, then $v \in V(W)$ by
the second fact, applied to $K | W$, and so $a(c) = \{v\} \subseteq ins(W)$. Otherwise
$x \in ins(C)$ for some circuit $C$ of $K | W$. If $v \in V(C)$, then $a(c) \subseteq ins(W)$. If
not, then $c$ misses $U(C)$ by the second fact, applied to $K | C$. Since $c$ is connected and meets
$ins(C)$ at $x$, it follows that $c \subseteq ins(C)$, and so $a(c) \subseteq ins(W)$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.corresponding_atom_subset_iff` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section1.lean`, with
the second fact `GraphMinor.GM12.vertex_of_mem_region` and
`GraphMinor.GM12.subset_of_disjoint_frontier` in the same file.

## 2. Statement of (1.2), page 242: a drawing with one edge

**Kind.** Degenerate case.

**What goes wrong.** (1.2) states that if $\mathscr{T}$ is a respectful tangle of order
$\theta \ge 2$ in a 2-cell drawing $\Gamma$ with metric $d$, then for every $c \in A(\Gamma)$ there
is an edge $e$ of $\Gamma$ with $d(c, e) = \theta$. Let $\Gamma$ be the drawing in the sphere with
two vertices $u, v$ and one edge $e_0$ joining them. It is 2-cell. Every separation of $\Gamma$ of
order at most 1 has one side that contains $e_0$, and so contains $u$ and $v$. By the third tangle
axiom this side is not small. The other side has no edge, and has at most one vertex, since the
separation has order at most 1. So $\Gamma$ has exactly one tangle $\mathscr{T}$ of order 2, whose
small sides are the sides with no edge. It is respectful, because every $\Gamma$-normal $O$-arc that
meets at most one vertex bounds a disc on the side away from $e_0$, and the part of $\Gamma$ in this
disc has no edge. For $c = e_0$, the only edge is $e_0$, and $d(e_0, e_0) = 0 \ne 2$ by the
definition of $d$ on page 242.

**Correction.** Add the hypothesis $|E(\Gamma)| \ge 2$. With it, (1.2) holds. The new hypothesis
matters only when $\theta = 2$. For let $\theta \ge 3$, and suppose that $\Gamma$ has at most one
edge. Then $\Gamma$ has at most two vertices, since a 2-cell drawing is connected. Let $A$ consist
of the vertices of $\Gamma$ and no edge. Then $(A, \Gamma)$ is a separation of order at most
$2 < \theta$, and both of its sides contain every vertex of $\Gamma$. So by the third tangle axiom
neither $(A, \Gamma)$ nor $(\Gamma, A)$ is in the tangle, against the first axiom.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_1_2` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean`, and the form cited in
Graph Minors XV, `GraphMinor.GM15.GM12Theorem12Statement` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Cited.lean`. The case
$\theta \ge 3$ is `GraphMinor.GM12.two_le_edge_count_of_tangle_order_three` in
`GraphMinor/GM12/Section4DistantEdges.lean`.

## 3. Definition of a trace, clause (ii), page 243: $I_{i-1}$, not $I_i$

**Kind.** Misprint.

**What goes wrong.** A trace of $\phi$ is defined from points $x_1, \dots, x_n$ and open arcs
$I_1, \dots, I_n$ of $S^1$, where $I_i$ has ends $x_i$ and $x_{i+1}$. Clause (ii) says that
$v_{2i-1}$ is the vertex of $K$ in the region of $\Gamma$ including $\{\phi(x) \colon x \in I_i\}$.

$v_{2i} = \phi(x_i)$, so its region is the one that includes the image of the arc between $x_{i-1}$
and $x_i$, which is $I_{i-1}$. Clause (iii) uses $I_{i-1}$ in this way.

**Correction.** In clause (ii), read $I_{i-1}$ for $I_i$, where $I_0$ means $I_n$. With this, (2.1)
holds as printed.

## 4. Definition of a trace, clause (iii), page 243: the edge $e_{2i}$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** Clause (iii) defines $e_{2i}$ as the unique edge $e$ of $K$ joining
$v_{2i-1}$ and $v_{2i}$ such that $\phi_e$ is homotopic in $r_{2i-1} \cup \{v_{2i}\}$ to a curve in
$r_{2i-1}$, and says "We omit the proof that $e$ exists and is unique and does not depend on $h$."
The copy gives this proof, for a drawing $\Gamma$ with an edge. If $\Gamma$ has no edge, then by
condition (iv) of the definition of a radial drawing on page 242, $K$ has no edge, and so a curve
of positive length has no trace.

**Proof.** We prove the assertion omitted in (iii). The assertion used in (iv) follows from it by
running $\phi$ backwards. Let $v = v_{2i}$ and $r = r_{2i-1}$, and suppose that $\Gamma$ has an
edge. (If $\Gamma$ has no edge, then by condition (iv) on page 242, $K$ has no edge, and so a curve
of positive length has no trace.) Then $v$ is an end of an edge of $\Gamma$. For otherwise a small
circle around $v$ bounds a closed disc in the region of $\Gamma$ around $v$, since this region is an
open disc. These two closed discs have the same boundary and are different, since only the small one
contains $v$, and so their union is open and closed in $\Sigma$. Since $\Sigma$ is connected, they
cover $\Sigma$, and so $U(\Gamma) = \{v\}$, a contradiction.

For an edge $e$ of $K$ joining $v_{2i-1}$ and $v$, and for $h \in I_{i-1}$, let us say that $e$
*enters $v$ like $\phi$ from $h$* if $\phi_e$, formed with this $h$, is homotopic in $r \cup \{v\}$
to a curve in $r$. Since $\phi$ maps $I_{i-1}$ into $r$, adding to $\phi_e$ or removing from it an
initial part of $\phi$ in $r$ does not change this property. So if $e$ enters $v$ like $\phi$ from
one $h$, then it does so from every $h \in I_{i-1}$.

First we show that some edge $e$ enters $v$ like $\phi$ from some $h$. Suppose first that at least
two edges of $\Gamma$ are incident with $v$, a loop counted twice. Then the edges of $\Gamma$ at $v$
divide a small closed disc around $v$ into closed sectors, each a closed disc with $v$ in its
boundary, and the points of the small disc not in $U(\Gamma)$ are interior points of the sectors.
Since $\phi$ maps the part of $I_{i-1}$ near $x_i$ into $r$, which misses $U(\Gamma)$, and this part
is connected, $\phi$ maps it into the interior of one sector $Q$. By condition (iv) on page 242, the
edges of $\Gamma$ and of $K$ at $v$ alternate in the cyclic order around $v$, and so some edge $e$
of $K$ at $v$ starts inside $Q$. Let $P$ be the set formed by $v$ and the interior points of $Q$
near $v$. It is simply connected, since it contracts to $v$, and $P - \{v\}$ is path connected. Also
$P - \{v\}$ misses $U(\Gamma)$ and contains points of $\phi(I_{i-1}) \subseteq r$, and so it lies in
$r$. Since $e$ is connected and misses $U(\Gamma)$, and its points near $v$ are in $P - \{v\}$, it
lies in $r$, and so its other end is $v_{2i-1}$, the vertex of $K$ in $r$. Choose $h \in I_{i-1}$
such that $\phi$ maps the portion of $I_{i-1}$ between $h$ and $x_i$ into $P$, choose a point $z$ of
$e$ such that $e$ runs inside $P$ from $v$ to $z$, and join $\phi(h)$ to $z$ by a path in
$P - \{v\}$. Since $P$ is simply connected, the restriction of $\phi$ to the portion of $I_{i-1}$
between $h$ and $x_i$, followed by a curve tracing $e$ from $v$ to $z$, is homotopic in $P$ to this
path. Hence $\phi_e$ is homotopic in $r \cup \{v\}$ to this path followed by a curve tracing $e$
from $z$ to $v_{2i-1}$, which is a curve in $r$. So $e$ enters $v$ like $\phi$ from $h$.

Now suppose that just one edge $f$ of $\Gamma$ is incident with $v$, and it is not a loop. By
condition (iv) on page 242, exactly one edge $e$ of $K$ is incident with $v$. Remove from a small
disc around $v$ a short initial part of $f$. The rest of this disc is a simply connected set $P$,
and $P - \{v\}$ is path connected and misses $U(\Gamma)$. It meets $r$, since $v$ is in the closure
of $r$, and so it lies in $r$. The points of $e$ near $v$ are in $P - \{v\}$, and so, as before, $e$
lies in $r$ and joins $v$ and $v_{2i-1}$. Also $\phi$ maps the part of $I_{i-1}$ near $x_i$ into
$P$, since this part is mapped near $v$ and into $r$. So the same argument with this set $P$ shows
that $e$ enters $v$ like $\phi$.

Next let $e$, $e'$ be edges of $K$ joining $v_{2i-1}$ and $v$ that enter $v$ like $\phi$, from $h$
and from $h'$ say. By the second paragraph, both enter $v$ like $\phi$ from $h$. So $\phi_e$ and
$\phi_{e'}$, formed with $h$, are homotopic in $r \cup \{v\}$ to curves in $r$ with the same ends
$\phi(h)$ and $v_{2i-1}$. These two curves are homotopic in $r$, since $r$ is an open disc, and so
$\phi_e$ and $\phi_{e'}$ are homotopic in $r \cup \{v\}$. Cancelling their common initial part, we
find that the curves tracing $e$ and $e'$ from $v$ to $v_{2i-1}$ are homotopic in $r \cup \{v\}$. If
$e \ne e'$, then $\bar{e} \cup \bar{e}'$ is an $O$-arc null-homotopic in $r \cup \{v\}$, and so by
[5, Theorem (7.2)] it bounds a disc $\Delta \subseteq r \cup \{v\}$. Since
$\Delta - \{v\} \subseteq r$ misses $U(\Gamma)$, no edge of $\Gamma$ at $v$ enters $\Delta$, and so
two edges of $K$ are consecutive in the cyclic order around $v$, contrary to condition (iv) on page
242. So $e = e'$, which proves that $e$ is unique and does not depend on $h$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.existsUnique_entersLike` and `GraphMinor.GM12.existsUnique_leavesLike`
in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section2EntryLemma.lean`, with the property `GraphMinor.GM12.EntersLike` in
`GraphMinor/GM12/Defs.lean`. The existence is `GraphMinor.GM12.exists_entersLike` in
`GraphMinor/GM12/Section2NormalEntryExistence.lean`, with the case of two or more edge ends
`GraphMinor.GM12.exists_entersLike_of_two_halfEdges` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section2NormalEntry.lean`

`GraphMinor/GM12/Section2LeafEntryExistence.lean`. The uniqueness is
`GraphMinor.GM12.entersLike_edge_unique` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section2EntryUnique.lean`.

## 5. Proof of (4.2), page 249: control of a minor of $L'$

**Kind.** Missing proof.

**What is missing.** The paper says "The proof is clear, and we omit it." The copy adds the proof.

**Proof.** Let $\alpha$ be a function showing that $\mathscr{T}$ controls an $L'$ minor of $G$.
First, for each edge $f$ of $L'$, the edge $\alpha(f)$ belongs to no $\alpha(w)$ $(w \in V(L'))$.
For suppose that $\alpha(f) \in E(\alpha(w))$. Then both ends of $\alpha(f)$ are in $V(\alpha(w))$.
If $f$ has distinct ends $u, u'$, then by (ii) $\alpha(w)$ meets both $\alpha(u)$ and $\alpha(u')$,
and so $w = u$ and $w = u'$ by (i), which is impossible. If $f$ is a loop with end $u$, then by
(iii) $\alpha(w)$ meets $\alpha(u)$, and so $w = u$ by (i), contrary to (iii).

Since $L$ is isomorphic to a minor of $L'$, there are mutually disjoint non-empty sets
$Y(v) \subseteq V(L')$ $(v \in V(L))$, and distinct edges $\eta(e)$ of $L'$ $(e \in E(L))$, with the
following properties. If $e \in E(L)$ has distinct ends $u, v$, then $\eta(e)$ has one end in $Y(u)$
and the other in $Y(v)$. If $e \in E(L)$ is a loop with end $v$, then both ends of $\eta(e)$ are in
$Y(v)$. For each $v \in V(L)$, the subgraph $M(v)$ of $L'$ with vertex set $Y(v)$ and edge set all
edges of $L'$ with both ends in $Y(v)$ which are not of the form $\eta(e)$ is connected. (Take for
$Y(v)$ the set of vertices of $L'$ contracted into $v$, and for $\eta(e)$ the edge of $L'$ that
becomes $e$.)

For $v \in V(L)$, let $\alpha'(v)$ be the union of the subgraphs $\alpha(w)$ $(w \in Y(v))$,
together with the edges $\alpha(f)$ $(f \in E(M(v)))$ and their ends. For $e \in E(L)$, let
$\alpha'(e) = \alpha(\eta(e))$. We claim that $\alpha'$ shows that $\mathscr{T}$ controls an $L$
minor of $G$. For (i), $\alpha'(v)$ is non-null, and it is connected, since each $\alpha(w)$ is
connected, $M(v)$ is connected, and for each edge $f$ of $M(v)$ the edge $\alpha(f)$ joins
$\alpha(w)$ and $\alpha(w')$, where $w, w'$ are the ends of $f$, by (ii) and (iii) for $\alpha$. For
distinct $u, v \in V(L)$, the subgraphs $\alpha'(u)$ and $\alpha'(v)$ are disjoint, since
$Y(u) \cap Y(v) = \emptyset$, and since the subgraphs $\alpha(w)$ $(w \in V(L'))$ are mutually
disjoint and include all ends of the edges $\alpha(f)$. For (ii), let $e$ have distinct ends $u, v$.
Then $\eta(e)$ has ends $w \in Y(u)$ and $w' \in Y(v)$, and so by (ii) for $\alpha$, $\alpha'(e)$
has ends in $V(\alpha(w)) \subseteq V(\alpha'(u))$ and in $V(\alpha(w')) \subseteq V(\alpha'(v))$.
For (iii), let $e$ be a loop with end $v$. Both ends of $\eta(e)$ are in $Y(v)$, and so by (ii) and
(iii) for $\alpha$, both ends of $\alpha'(e)$ are in $V(\alpha'(v))$. Moreover
$\alpha'(e) \notin E(\alpha'(v))$, because $\alpha'(e)$ belongs to no $\alpha(w)$, as we saw, and
$\alpha'(e) \ne \alpha(f)$ for $f \in E(M(v))$ by (iv) for $\alpha$, since $f \ne \eta(e)$. For
(iv), if $e, e' \in E(L)$ are distinct, then $\eta(e) \ne \eta(e')$, and so
$\alpha'(e) \ne \alpha'(e')$ by (iv) for $\alpha$. For (v), the sets $Y(v)$ $(v \in V(L))$ are
mutually disjoint and non-empty, and so $|V(L)| \le |V(L')|$. Hence $\mathscr{T}$ has order
$\ge |V(L)|$, by (v) for $\alpha$. Let $(A, B) \in \mathscr{T}$ have order $< |V(L)|$, and suppose
that $V(\alpha'(v)) \subseteq V(A)$ for some $v \in V(L)$. Choose $w \in Y(v)$. Then
$V(\alpha(w)) \subseteq V(\alpha'(v)) \subseteq V(A)$, and $(A, B)$ has order $< |V(L')|$, contrary
to (v) for $\alpha$. This proves (4.2).

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_4_2` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean`, proved as
`GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_4_2_proof` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section4Monotone.lean`. The steps are
`GraphMinor.GM12.controlsMinor_has_minorModel` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section4Model.lean`,
`GraphMinor.GM12.minorModel_comp` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section4ModelComposition.lean`,
`GraphMinor.GM12.minorModel_vertex_ncard_le` and `GraphMinor.GM12.controlsMinor_of_minorModel` in
`GraphMinor/GM12/Section4ModelBack.lean`, and `GraphMinor.GM12.controlsMinor_of_target_model` in
`GraphMinor/GM12/Section4ControlComposition.lean`.

## 6. Statement of (5.2), page 254: order 1

**Kind.** Degenerate case.

**What goes wrong.** (5.2) states that, for a crosscap $\Sigma_2$ of $\Sigma$, the tangle in
$\Gamma$ induced by a tangle $\mathscr{T}_1$ of order $\theta$ in $\Gamma \cap \hat{\Sigma}_1$ is
respectful towards $\Sigma$, under hypotheses on the $\theta$ paths of $\Gamma \cap \Sigma_2$ and on
free sets. This fails for $\theta = 1$. Let $\Sigma$ be the projective plane, cut along an $O$-arc
$C_1$ into a closed disc $\Sigma_1$ and a Möbius band $\Sigma_2$, so that $\hat{\Sigma}_1$ is a
sphere. Let $\Gamma$ have two vertices $u, v$ on $C_1$, a path in $\Sigma_1$ joining them, and a
path in $\Sigma_2$ joining them that runs close to one of the two arcs of $C_1$ between $u$ and $v$.
This second path does not cross the band, so it misses the core circle of the band. The drawing
$\Gamma \cap \hat{\Sigma}_1$ is connected, so in its only tangle $\mathscr{T}_1$ of order 1 every
small side is null. Hence $\mathscr{T}_1$ is respectful towards the sphere $\hat{\Sigma}_1$, since
every $O$-arc that misses $\Gamma \cap \hat{\Sigma}_1$ bounds a disc on the side away from it, and
every set of one vertex is free. But the core circle misses $\Gamma$ and bounds no disc in $\Sigma$,
since it is one-sided. So the induced tangle is not respectful towards $\Sigma$.

**Correction.** Assume $\theta \ge 2$. With this, (5.2) holds (the proof needs a further step, see
the correction to the proof of (5.2) below). Its use in Graph Minors XV, (3.3), has $\theta \ge 2$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_5_2` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean`, and the form cited in
Graph Minors XV, `GraphMinor.GM15.GM12Theorem52Statement` in `GraphMinor/GM15/Cited.lean`.

## 7. Proof of (5.3), page 256: infinitely many pieces of $F$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof takes the components $X_i$ ($i \in I$) of $F - \Sigma_2$, attaches to
each a closed disc $\Delta_i \subseteq \Sigma_1$, and applies [1, Theorem (1.7)] in
$\Sigma_2 \cup \bigcup (\Delta_i : i \in I)$. The set $I$ may be infinite. For example, $F$ may
touch $C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_t$ from inside $\Sigma_1$, without entering $\Sigma_2$, at a sequence
of intervals that converge to a point $p$. The proof does not show that
$\Sigma_2 \cup \bigcup (\Delta_i : i \in I)$ is a 2-manifold near $p$, which [1, Theorem (1.7)]
needs.

**Correction.** Before taking the pieces $X_i$, push the parts of $F$ that touch
$C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_t$ from inside $\Sigma_1$ off $\Sigma_2$, inside a collar of
$C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_t$ in $\Sigma_1$. After this, $F$ crosses between $C_1 \cup \dots \cup C_t$
and a slightly smaller copy of $\Sigma_2$ inside $\Sigma_2$ only finitely many times, so only
finitely many pieces remain, and the argument of the paper applies to them. (5.3) holds as printed.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_5_3` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean`, with the main case
`GraphMinor.GM12.respect_of_cross` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section5Respect.lean`. The collar is

## 8. Proof of (5.2), page 257: condition (iii) of (5.3)

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The paper says that (5.2) follows immediately from (5.3). Conditions (i) and
(ii) of (5.3) follow from the hypotheses of (5.2), but condition (iii) does not follow at once. It
asks that every continuous $\phi \colon S^1 \to \Sigma_2$ of length $< \theta$ with $|\phi|$
$\Gamma$-normal is null-homotopic in $\Sigma_2$. The paper gives no reason for this, and for
$\theta = 1$ it is false: in the example in the correction to (5.2) above, the core circle of the
band has length 0 and is not null-homotopic.

**Correction.** Prove (iii) for $\theta \ge 2$ as follows. Let $P$ be one of the $\theta$ paths of
$\Gamma \cap \Sigma_2$. The ends of the paths come in opposite pairs on $C_1$, so each other path
has one end on each of the two arcs of $C_1$ between the ends of $P$. Such a path joins the two arcs
and misses $P$, so cutting $\Sigma_2$ along $P$ leaves it connected. A Möbius band cut along a path
that leaves it connected is a closed disc. So $\Sigma_2 - U(P)$ is simply-connected. Now let $\phi$
be as in (iii). If $|\phi|$ met all $\theta$ paths, then, since the paths are disjoint, at least
$\theta$ points of $S^1$ would map into $U(\Gamma)$, and $\phi$ would have length $\ge \theta$. So
$|\phi|$ misses one of the paths, say $P$, and $\phi$ is null-homotopic in $\Sigma_2 - U(P)$, and so
in $\Sigma_2$. Then (5.3) gives (5.2) for $\theta \ge 2$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.exists_missed_path` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section5Final.lean` (a curve of
length $< \theta$ misses one of the paths), `GraphMinor.GM12.exists_cut_band` and
`GraphMinor.GM12.no_split` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section5Band.lean` (cutting the band along a path),
and `GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_5_2` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean`.

**Kind.** Degenerate case.

**What goes wrong.** The setting of (6.1), on pages 257–258, allows $\theta = 2$, and (6.1) states
that $d(e_1, e_{\theta/2}) \ge \theta$. When $\theta = 2$ we have $\theta/2 = 1$, so

## 10. Proof of (6.1), page 258: the choice of the radial drawing

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The metric $d$ is defined on page 242 from a radial drawing $K$ of $\Gamma$.
The proof of (6.1) chooses a special radial drawing $K$ and computes $d$ in it. But the paper does
not show that $d$ is the same for every radial drawing. Graph Minors XI, page 75, says that a radial
drawing is unique up to homeomorphisms of $\Sigma$ that fix $U(\Gamma)$, without proof, and neither
paper says how the metric changes under such a homeomorphism.

**Correction.** Insert after the choice of $K$: the metric does not depend on the radial drawing.
For if $d'$ is the metric computed from another radial drawing, then (7.7)(ii), applied with
$\Gamma' = \Gamma$ and $\mathscr{T}' = \mathscr{T}$ in both orders, gives $d \ge d'$ and $d' \ge d$.
The proof of (7.7) uses only (1.2) and (2.1), not (6.1), so the argument is not circular. So we may
compute $d$ in $K$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.HasTangleMetric.eq` and `GraphMinor.GM12.HasTangleMetric.of_radial` in

## 11. Proof of (6.1), page 258: no short walk around $e_1$ and $e_{\theta/2}$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** The proof says that there is no closed walk $W^*$ of $K$ of length $< 2\theta$
with $U(W^*) \subseteq \Delta$ and $e_1, e_{\theta/2} \subseteq \mathrm{ins}(W^*)$, and gives no
reason.

**Correction.** Insert a proof by levels. Every edge of $K$ in $\Delta$ lies in some $\bar{D}_i$ and
joins $v_i$ to a vertex of $P_i$ or of $P_{i+1}$, because $v_i$ is the vertex of $K$ in $r_i$ and
$r_i \cap \Delta \subseteq \bar{D}_i$. Give $v_i$ the level $2i$ ($0 \le i \le \theta/2$) and each
vertex of $P_i$ the level $2i - 1$ ($1 \le i \le \theta/2$). Then each edge of $K$ in $\Delta$
changes the level by one. Let $W^*$ be such a walk. Since $e_1 \cap U(W^*) = \emptyset$, some
circuit $C$ of $K | W^*$ has $e_1 \subseteq \mathrm{ins}(C)$, and
$\mathrm{ins}(C) \subseteq \Delta$. If $v_0 \notin V(C)$, then $U(C)$ misses $D_0$, and $D_0$ meets
$\mathrm{ins}(C)$ since $e_1 \subseteq \bar{D}_0$. So $D_0 \subseteq \mathrm{ins}(C)$, because $D_0$
is connected. Then $\mathrm{ins}(C)$ contains a point of $F$ not in $U(C)$, which is impossible for
a closed disc in $\Delta$ bounded by $U(C)$. So $v_0 \in V(W^*)$, and in the same way
$v_{\theta/2} \in V(W^*)$. A closed walk through a vertex of level $0$ and a vertex of level
$\theta$ has length at least $2\theta$. With this and the correction above, (6.1) holds for every
even $\theta \ge 4$.

`GraphMinor.GM12.Pieces61.exists_high` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section6Claims.lean`, and
`GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_6_1` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean`.

## 12. Proof of (7.3), page 261: parallel edges of $K$ at $v$

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** To prove (ii), the paper says that no two edges of $K$ incident with $v$ are
parallel, since $\Gamma \setminus v$ is 2-cell. This is false when $\Gamma$ has a loop at $v$ that
bounds a disc. Take $K_4$ in the sphere, put a new vertex $v$ in the middle of one edge, and add at
$v$ a loop $e$ that bounds a disc with no other part of $\Gamma$, inside a region $r_2$ at $v$. Then
$\Gamma \setminus v$ is $K_4$ with one edge deleted, which is 2-cell. But $r_2$ meets $v$ at two
corners, on the two sides of $e$, so $K$ has two parallel edges between $v$ and the vertex of $K$ in
$r_2$.

**Correction.** First apply (7.2) repeatedly, to delete what lies inside each $\Gamma$-normal
$O$-arc $F$ with $F \cap V(\Gamma) = \{v\}$ whose inside contains an edge of $\Gamma$. This removes
such loops, does not change $\Gamma \setminus v$, and does not decrease the metric. After that, no
two edges of $K$ incident with $v$ are parallel, and the rest of the proof is as printed. (7.3)
holds as printed.

## 13. Proof of (7.5), page 265: a dual tangle of order 2

**Kind.** Proof gap.

**What goes wrong.** In the case where $\bar{r} - r$ is an $O$-arc, the proof takes the tangle
$\mathscr{T}_2$ of order $\theta - 1$ in $\Gamma_2$ and lets $\mathscr{T}_3$ be the dual tangle in
$\Gamma_3$, by Graph Minors XI, (6.6). When $\theta = 3$ this is (6.6) for order 2, which fails when
one of the two drawings has a loop (see the correction to (6.6) in [GM11.md](GM11.md)). Here
$\Gamma_3$ may have loops: an edge of $\Gamma$ not in $\bar{r}$ with both ends in $\bar{r}$ becomes
a loop at $r_2^*$ when the edges in $\bar{r} - r$ are contracted. For instance, if $\Gamma_2$ is one
edge in the sphere, then $\Gamma_3$ is one loop, and the tangle of order 2 in $\Gamma_2$ has no dual
tangle in $\Gamma_3$. The paper does not deal with this case.

**Correction.** The step still holds, because $\mathscr{T}_2$ comes from the tangle $\mathscr{T}_1$
of order 3 in $\Gamma_1$. If the pretangle induced in $\Gamma_3$ were not a tangle, then a loop of
$\Gamma_3$ with no small side would give three small sides of $\mathscr{T}_1$ that cover $\Gamma_1$,
against the second tangle axiom. So $\mathscr{T}_3$ is a tangle, and (7.5) holds as printed.

## 15. Statement of (7.11), page 271: $z$ an edge and $\lambda \le 1$

**Kind.** Statement mistake.

**What goes wrong.** (7.11) states that if $\Gamma$ is 2-cell with $E(\Gamma) \ne \emptyset$,
$z \in A(\Gamma)$, and $0 \le \lambda < \theta$, then the subdrawing $\Gamma'$ formed by the edges
$e$ with $d(z, e) > \lambda$ and their ends is 2-cell. This fails when $z$ is an edge and
$\lambda \le 1$. Let $\Gamma$ be a path with three edges $f_1, z, f_2$, in this order, drawn in the
sphere. It is 2-cell. Let $\mathscr{T}$ be the tangle of order 2 whose small sides are the sides
that do not contain $z$. It is a tangle: each separation of order at most 1 has $z$ in exactly one
side, three small sides miss $z$ and so do not cover $\Gamma$, and a small side does not contain
both ends of $z$, since these ends are in the other side and the separation has order at most 1. It
is respectful, because every $\Gamma$-normal $O$-arc that meets at most one vertex bounds a disc on
the side away from $z$. Let $\lambda = 1$. Since $K$ is bipartite, its closed walks of length
$< 2\theta = 4$ have length 0 or 2. Such a walk either goes along one edge and back, and then its
inside is that edge, or it is a 2-edge circuit through an end of $z$, and then its inside is the
side away from $z$. So no such walk has $z$ in its inside, and
$d(z, f_1) = d(z, f_2) = 2 > \lambda$, while $d(z, z) = 0$. So $\Gamma'$ consists of $f_1$, $f_2$
and their ends. It is not connected, so it is not 2-cell. The proof fails at "$e \in E(\Gamma')$ if
and only if $r \not\subseteq Z$": here no closed walk of length $\le 2\lambda$ has the region of $K$
including $z$ in its inside, so $Z = \emptyset$, and yet $z \notin E(\Gamma')$.

**Correction.** Add the hypothesis that if $z \in E(\Gamma)$ then $\lambda \ge 2$, as (7.8) and
(7.10) have for $c$. With it, (7.11) holds, and no hypothesis on $|E(\Gamma)|$ is needed: the proof
uses (1.2) only to show that $Z \ne \Sigma$, and this also follows from Graph Minors XI, (8.12). In
the drawing with one edge from the correction to (1.2) above, $\theta = 2$, so the new hypothesis
excludes $z = e_0$. Graph Minors XV restates (7.11) as its (4.3), and uses (4.3) only with
$\lambda \ge 2$.

**Lean.** `GraphMinor.GM12.gm12_theorem_7_11` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Section711Cited.lean` proves the
corrected statement, in the form `GraphMinor.GM15.GM12Theorem711Statement` in
`GraphMinor/GM15/Cited.lean`. `GraphMinor.GM12.theorem_7_11` in `GraphMinor/GM12/Results.lean` is a
variant that assumes $E(\Gamma') \ne \emptyset$ in place of $\lambda < \theta$.
